(U) A routine security assessment revealed that surveillance cameras fitted to the U.K. Royal Navy and Royal Marines' new drone boats contained Chinese-made components that were secretly transmitting data back to an IP address in China.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Affected Fleet:** The issue impacts the **K3 Scout uncrewed surface vessels (USVs)**—a 12 million pound fleet built by British defense contractor Kraken Technology Group and utilized since March for special operations and littoral surveillance.
- **The Data Transmission:** Investigators discovered that the third-party cameras were sending automated **"heartbeat communications"** (small data packets confirming that the devices were online and functioning normally) to a server location in China.
- **Official Response & Containment:** The U.K. Ministry of Defence (MoD) and Kraken stated that a joint audit found **no evidence** that classified military data, reconnaissance footage, or operational systems were compromised or transmitted externally. However, as an immediate precaution, the MoD stripped all internet connectivity from the cameras.
- **Supply Chain Oversight Failures:** Though Kraken acquired the cameras from a third-party supplier that had provided formal security guarantees, the incident exposed deep vulnerabilities in military procurement, raising serious concerns over reliance on globally sourced commercial components.
- **National Security Concerns:** Because the drones operate near sensitive locations—such as the Special Boat Service (SBS) headquarters in Dorset—critics and opposition lawmakers called for urgent fleet-wide inspections to guard against potential espionage.
