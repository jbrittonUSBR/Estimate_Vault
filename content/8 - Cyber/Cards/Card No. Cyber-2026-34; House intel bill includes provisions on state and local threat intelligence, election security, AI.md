(U) **House intel bill includes provisions on state and local threat intelligence, election security, AI** (CyberScoop, July 21, 2026) The House Intelligence Committee advanced its fiscal 2027 intelligence authorization legislation on Monday. The bill includes several cyber- and AI-related provisions.

**State and local threat intelligence sharing**:
- Authorizes a pilot program in which the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) would select one state to receive monthly unclassified briefings from ODNI, DHS, FBI, and others on “timely, specific, and actionable information regarding cyber threats.”
- After one year, ODNI would report on the viability of expanding the program.
- Requires ODNI to produce a strategy on information sharing with state and local governments.
- Orders a Government Accountability Office (GAO) assessment of current sharing efforts, deconfliction among agencies, and obstacles posed by security clearances.
- These provisions come amid frustration over federal cutbacks in cyber aid to states and localities and the Trump administration’s shift of more responsibility to the local level.

**Election security**:
Democrats successfully added three amendments:
- Rep. Jim Himes (D-Conn.): Requires an unclassified IC assessment of foreign intelligence threats to the 2026 midterms.
- Rep. Jason Crow (D-Colo.): Partially withholds ODNI funding until overdue reports on the 2024 and 2026 elections are provided.
- Rep. Chrissy Houlahan (D-Pa.): Protects analysts from retribution by IC leadership for working on products related to foreign influence in U.S. elections.

**Artificial intelligence**:
(U) According to a Republican summary, the bill significantly increases funding for expanded access and use of frontier AI models for intelligence and cyber missions, codifies and expands the role of the NSA’s Artificial Intelligence Security Center, and strengthens information sharing on AI threats. Chairman Rick Crawford (R-Ark.) highlighted a strong focus on the global AI race. The bill also orders an assessment by the Department of Energy’s Office of Intelligence and Counterintelligence on foreign cyberthreats to critical energy infrastructure. Intelligence authorization measures are often incorporated into the annual National Defense Authorization Act.
