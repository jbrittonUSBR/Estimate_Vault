(U//FOUO) Cyber actors present a persistent threat to US aviation operations, and their impact likely will be magnified during the upcoming six-week-long FIFA World Cup 2026. We are most concerned about distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, which overwhelm websites with unwanted traffic, and ransomware attacks because precedent suggests these are the cyber tactics most likely to disrupt aviation operations. While we lack specific, credible intelligence indicating planned attacks, cyber actors have historically viewed these types of events as attractive targets.
- (U//SSI) Hacktivists and cyber criminals are constantly using automated scanning and phishing to identify vulnerabilities and compromise credentials in aviation sector networks, according to industry press. Cyber attacks in recent years have frequently caused flight delays or cancellations, disruptions in baggage handling, and flight information outages, according to DHS and open-source reporting, and TSA’s database review. High travel periods, such as for large-scale sporting events, magnify the impact of disruptions and strain public-facing aviation network platforms, like ticketing and scheduling services, increasing their susceptibility to DDoS attacks, judging from open-source reporting.
- (U//SSI) DDoS attacks against aviation entities, primarily by hacktivists and unidentified cyber actors, are the most commonly observed cyber attacks against global aviation infrastructure. TSA is aware of an average of 22 DDoS attacks per month against global aviation infrastructure between 2023 and 2025, making such attacks likely during the FIFA World Cup 2026, judging from DHS and open-source reporting. In 2025, one attack briefly shut down Atlanta’s international airport website, according to open-source reporting. Pro-Iranian, pro-Palestinian, and pro-Russian groups are the most active and capable hacktivist threats, though any hacktivist group may exploit World Cup events to highlight their cause, according to TSA’s database review.
- (U//SSI) TSA is aware of an average of two ransomware attacks per month against global aviation entities between 2023 and late 2025. In 2025, a cyber criminal group, LokiLocker, conducted a ransomware attack that caused widespread flight delays and cancellations at major international airports in at least three countries, according to open-source reporting.

(U//FOUO) The risks of DDoS and ransomware during the FIFA World Cup 2026 could be reduced by implementing cybersecurity measures that prevent network overload and initial network access, and by training corporate staff to recognize ransomware solicitation tactics. CISA and other US government agencies offer free tools and guidance to help aviation entities implement these protective measures, including CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and the Stop Ransomware Guide.
