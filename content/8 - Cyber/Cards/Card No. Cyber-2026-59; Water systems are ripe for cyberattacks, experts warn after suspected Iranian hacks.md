(U) Following a wave of cyberattacks targeting municipal utilities across at least a dozen states, cybersecurity experts and officials warned that America's public water systems remain dangerously exposed as "low-hanging fruit" for foreign hackers.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Scale of the Campaign:** Coordinated cyber intrusions have impacted water systems in roughly 12 states, with Minnesota alone seeing at least 30 systems targeted (such as in Plymouth). U.S. intelligence agencies suspect Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) is behind the operations.
- **The Core Vulnerability:** Many of the nation's more than 150,000 water systems rely on aging, internet-connected Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) that manage critical functions like water pressure and chemical treatments. Many of these devices lack proper firewalls, virtual private networks (VPNs), or strong passwords.
- **Regulatory and Political Hurdles:** Efforts to mandate baseline federal cybersecurity standards have faced fierce political resistance. A previous push by the EPA to require security assessments for water infrastructure was successfully challenged and struck down following lawsuits by Republican-led states.
- **Varying State Responses:** While federal mandates stalled, individual states have taken different paths. Jurisdictions like Indiana, Maryland, and New York have enacted their own laws requiring water system cybersecurity risk assessments, leaving them largely insulated from this specific wave of attacks. Meanwhile, heavily secured or completely "air-gapped" systems (like Cedar Rapids, Iowa) avoided exposure by refusing to connect industrial controls to the public internet.
