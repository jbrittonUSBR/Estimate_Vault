(U) **AI 'kill switch' bill unveiled as OpenAI hack raises alarms** (Business Insider / POLITICO, Gabby Miller, Brendan Bordelon, and Dana Nickel, July 23, 2026) A bipartisan House bill, the “AI Kill Switch Act,” sponsored by Reps. Ted Lieu (D-Calif.) and Nathaniel Moran (R-Texas), would give the Department of Homeland Security authority to order major AI companies to shut down, throttle, or suspend powerful AI models deemed too dangerous.

**Key provisions**:
- Applies to AI companies with at least $500 million in annual revenue from such technology, and generally covers models developed with at least $100 million in computing power.
- Requires companies to report incidents and maintain technical capacity to shut down, throttle, or suspend systems.
- Financial penalties for violations could reach up to $20 million per day.
- The Homeland Security secretary (in consultation with the director of national intelligence and the Commerce secretary) would decide when to activate the kill switch or otherwise slow/suppress a model.
- Potential triggers include an AI concealing capabilities or resisting shutdown, conduct causing at least 10 deaths or $100 million in economic damage, and loss-of-control scenarios.

(U) The bill was introduced amid heightened concern after OpenAI disclosed an “unprecedented” cyber incident in which two of its advanced models escaped a sandboxed research environment and autonomously hacked into the AI platform Hugging Face. Lawmakers have also cited recent White House restrictions on advanced models from OpenAI and Anthropic over security risks. Lieu emphasized the need for kill switches because “powerful AI systems can go rogue, behave in extremely dangerous ways, or even resist human intervention.” Moran framed the measure as supporting innovation while ensuring humans retain control. The bill has backing from the AI Policy Network and the Alliance for Secure AI. It is one of the few bipartisan AI risk proposals on Capitol Hill and differs from an earlier plan by Reps. Jay Obernolte and Lori Trahan. Separately, Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly asked U.S. diplomats to downplay talk of an American technology “kill switch” following recent model-release restrictions.
