(U//FOUO) A comprehensive review of Utah cyber assets’ network logs following the coordinated US-Israeli military strikes against Iran on 28 February 2026 ("Operation Epic Fury") revealed a significant, coordinated escalation in Iranian state-sponsored cyber operations targeting Utah-based cyber assets at the state and local levels. The Statewide Information and Analysis Center (SIAC) identified a synchronized surge in cyber activity from multiple suspected Iranian Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs), specifically APT39, Lemon Sandstorm, and MuddyWater.

(U//FOUO) This bulletin highlights three critical shifts in Iranian cyber tradecraft: 
1. (U//FOUO) A "Downward Shift" in Targeting: Iranian threat actors have been observed shifting their focus from state level cyber assets to target county-level governments, access management portals, and specialized government related healthcare datasets.
2. (U//FOUO) Infrastructure Convergence: Distinct Iranian APT groups (APT39 and Lemon Sandstorm) have been observed actively sharing the exact same US-based proxy infrastructure and virtual private servers (VPS) to conduct operations based on geo-geographical artifacts.
3. (U//FOUO) Synchronized Escalations Bypassing Geo-Blocks: Iranian actors have heavily synchronized their operational activities while universally adopting domestic commercial hosting providers to evade geographic IP blocking.

(U//FOUO) The SIAC assesses it is highly likely that Iranian APTs are deliberately shifting targeting priorities down to municipal governments and specialized state agencies. This assessment is made with high confidence due to the telemetry from March and April 2026 that indicates a deliberate pivot by Iranian actors toward what they may consider easier, data-rich targets at the local and county levels. Malicious actors are likely operating under the assumption that local municipalities possess softer security perimeters compared to federal networks, yet still house high-value PII, healthcare data, or administrative credentials that can be leveraged for disruptive attacks or ransomware deployment.

(U//FOUO) The SIAC assesses it is very likely that distinct Iranian state-sponsored APTs are actively sharing logistics, commercial VPNs, and operational nodes. This assessment is made with high confidence. It is highly improbable that two distinct Iranian APTs would randomly utilize the exact same geographic coordinates in New Jersey across different commercial providers; this strongly confirms a centralized, shared proxy architecture utilized by the Iranian intelligence apparatus. 

(U//FOUO) The SIAC assesses it is almost certain the March and April 2026 escalations represent a coordinated, state-directed retaliatory campaign rather than opportunistic targeting. This assessment is made with high confidence based on the tight temporal clustering of escalations across multiple, distinct Iranian proxy networks immediately following a major geopolitical catalyst. Furthermore, the SIAC assesses it is highly likely that the universal adoption of domestic commercial hosting providers is a shared, coordinated evasion tactic specifically designed to defeat standard US perimeter defenses.

(U//FOUO) As geopolitical tensions remain volatile, Iranian cyber operations against state and local entities will almost certainly persist. Reliance on geo-blocking static IPs from known international geographic locations is not sufficient due to Iranian actors using domestic infrastructure to bypass geographic restrictions.
