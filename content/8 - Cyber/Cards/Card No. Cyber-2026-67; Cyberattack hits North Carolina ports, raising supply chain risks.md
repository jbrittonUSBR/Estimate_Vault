(U) A cyberattack targeted the IT infrastructure of the North Carolina State Ports Authority, causing a systems-wide outage that forced a temporary shift to manual cargo processing across key maritime and inland logistics hubs.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **Affected Facilities:** The incident disrupted operations at the **Port of Wilmington**, the **Port of Morehead City**, and the **Charlotte Inland Port**, which collectively handle millions of tons of bulk, breakbulk, and container cargo annually.
- **Operational Impact:** Detected on August 4, the cyber intrusion disrupted automated port gate processing, credential verification, and tracking workflows. This forced facilities to temporarily delay gate openings and rely on manual processing workarounds for incoming truckers and freight.
- **Response and Containment:** Port officials immediately activated their cybersecurity contingency plan, isolated affected IT systems to prevent further spread, and brought in outside forensic experts. State agencies and the **U.S. Coast Guard** stepped in to monitor the situation, confirming that maritime safety was not compromised.
- **Unanswered Details:** Authorities confirmed the system was breached by an outside actor, but have not publicly disclosed the initial access vector, whether ransomware was involved, or if any data was exfiltrated. No threat group has claimed responsibility.
- **Supply Chain Vulnerability:** Cybersecurity experts highlighted that attackers do not necessarily need to breach physical operational technology (like cranes or control systems) to cause chaos; crippling digital availability and slowing down essential logistics is enough to create significant downstream economic disruption. Normal gate schedules resumed shortly after, though recovery efforts and residual delays persisted.
