(U) A routine cybersecurity assessment by the U.K. Ministry of Defence (MoD) discovered that camera systems aboard the Royal Navy’s new uncrewed surface vessels (USVs) contained Chinese-made components that were actively transmitting data back to an internet address in China.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Affected Fleet:** The issue impacts the **Kraken K3 Scout USVs**—a fleet of roughly 20 drone boats built by Kraken Technology Group and utilized by the Royal Navy and Royal Marines for surveillance and littoral operations.
- **"Phoning Home" to China:** During a vulnerability assessment, investigators found that the third-party cameras were sending automated "heartbeat" signals (small data packets used to check if a device is online) to an IP address located in China.
- **Official Response and Containment:** The U.K. MoD and Kraken Technology Group emphasized that a joint audit found **no evidence** that classified military data, reconnaissance video, or systems were compromised, accessed, or transmitted externally. However, as a precaution, internet connectivity was immediately stripped from the affected camera hardware.
- **Supply Chain Oversight Failures:** Though the drones were British-built, the cameras were sourced from a third-party vendor that had provided assurances of compliance with Western procurement security standards (such as U.S. NDAA regulations restricting certain Chinese components).
- **Broader Implications:** The incident highlights deep-seated vulnerabilities in modern military supply chains, exposing the risks of relying on globally sourced, off-the-shelf commercial components for sensitive defense hardware.
