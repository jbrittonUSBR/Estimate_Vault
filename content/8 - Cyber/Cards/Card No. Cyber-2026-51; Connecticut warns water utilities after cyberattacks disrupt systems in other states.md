(U) Following a wave of cyberattacks targeting water and wastewater infrastructure across multiple U.S. states, Connecticut state officials issued urgent warnings to local water utilities to secure their industrial control systems.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Nationwide Threat:** Coordinated cyberattacks—suspected to be tied to foreign threat groups—have targeted vulnerable programmable logic controllers (PLCs) at water and wastewater facilities in at least seven to twelve states.
- **Connecticut's Status:** State officials and the Connecticut Intelligence Center confirmed that no water systems within Connecticut had reported a successful cyberattack or operational disruption at the time of the warning.
- **The Vulnerability:** Authorities and cybersecurity experts highlighted that many smaller or regional utilities rely on legacy software, default passwords, or industrial control hardware connected directly to the open internet, leaving them exposed to remote tampering.
- **Precautionary Measures:** State agencies and federal partners (such as CISA and the FBI) urged local operators to disconnect critical controllers from the public internet, implement multi-factor authentication, strengthen firewalls, and prepare manual operational workarounds.
