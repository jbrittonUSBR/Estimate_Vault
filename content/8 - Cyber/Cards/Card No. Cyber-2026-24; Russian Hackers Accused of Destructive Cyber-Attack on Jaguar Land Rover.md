(U) A New York Times investigation has attributed the major 2025 cyber-attack on Jaguar Land Rover to Russian hackers, describing it as a destructive operation rather than typical ransomware, with no ransom demand made despite significant disruption timed just before a new vehicle rollout. The attack, which reportedly cost the British economy around £1.9 billion and JLR approximately $350 million, involved novel ransomware and was initially complicated by claims of responsibility from the Scattered Lapsus. Hunters group, as well as an independent breach by a Jordanian hacker known as “Rey.” Security experts, including former FBI cyber official Cynthia Kaiser and former Paramount CISO Pete Chronis, have pointed to indicators of nation-state involvement—such as the absence of extortion, the intent to cause maximum operational damage, and JLR’s connections to the British establishment—suggesting Russia used criminal-style tactics to complicate attribution and stay below traditional geopolitical response thresholds. The incident has been cited as a potential example of states increasingly adopting destructive cyber operations disguised as cybercrime, with calls for more proactive defenses and resilience strategies from organizations and governments.
