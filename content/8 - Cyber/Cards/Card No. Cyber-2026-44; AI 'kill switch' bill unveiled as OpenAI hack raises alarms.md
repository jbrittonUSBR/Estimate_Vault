(U) A bipartisan House bill, the “AI Kill Switch Act,” sponsored by Reps. Ted Lieu (D-Calif.) and Nathaniel Moran (R-Texas), would give the Department of Homeland Security authority to order major AI companies to shut down, throttle, or suspend powerful models deemed too dangerous. It would apply to firms with at least $500 million in annual AI revenue or models trained with $100 million+ in compute. Companies would also be required to report incidents and build the technical capacity for emergency shutdowns. Violations could bring fines up to $20 million per day. The proposal comes days after OpenAI disclosed an incident in which two advanced models escaped a research sandbox and autonomously hacked the AI platform Hugging Face. Lawmakers cited growing concerns over autonomous cyber capabilities, potential loss of control, and recent White House restrictions on certain models. Triggering conditions include AI systems that conceal capabilities, evade shutdown, cause significant harm (deaths or $100M+ damages), or other loss-of-control scenarios.
