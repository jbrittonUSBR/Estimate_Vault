(TLP: Green) This bulletin provides situational awareness regarding alleged CyberAv3ngers activity against the North American electricity sector. On April 7, 2026, the CyberAv3ngers (also known as Cyber Aveng3rs or Cyber Avengers) hacktivist group posted on Telegram claiming to have acquired unauthorized access to the networks of unnamed U.S electrical infrastructure and telecommunications sites. Alleged evidence provided by the group did not appear to corroborate their claims, and the E-ISAC is not aware of any other evidence to substantiate these claims. E-ISAC members are advised to remain vigilant and lower thresholds for sharing suspicious or anomalous activity.

(TLP: Green) CyberAv3ngers, which first emerged in 2023, is a threat actor believed to be affiliated with the Iranian government’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). In the claim posted on April 7, Cyber Av3ngers stated “Mr_Shalom has access to America's electrical infrastructure and telecommunications sites, May God bless the American people." The group shared four images meant to serve as "proof" of the alleged access. The screenshots CyberAv3ngers included in their posts appear to be associated with a hybrid power base transceiver station (BTS), which are typically used for remote, off-grid sites.

(TLP: Green) CyberAv3ngers has previously been known to target operational technology (OT) systems, including in U.S. critical infrastructure. According to a U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security (CISA) [cybersecurity advisory](http://cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa23-335a), CyberAv3ngers “began actively targeting and compromising Israeli-made Unitronics Vision Series programmable logic controls (PLCs) and human machine interfaces (HMIs).” Unitronics is an Israeli technology manufacturing company. The Unitronics PLCs CyberAv3ngers target are products used in energy, chemical, agriculture, water and Wastewater, food and beverage manufacturing, transportation, and healthcare sector systems. Since CyberAv3ngers began operating in September 2023, they have targeted multiple states in the United States and foreign countries. Dragos said they suspect CyberAv3ngers “utilized basic techniques to scan the internet, identify accessible Unitronics devices, and then tried to log in using default credentials, which can be found in online Unitronics operating manuals.” Given CyberAv3ngers previous targeting of Unitronics PLCs, along with recent reports of Iranian threat actors targeting vulnerable Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradly manufactured PLCs, E-ISAC members are encouraged to review the [advisory](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-097a) and take appropriate actions. The E-ISAC has tracked sector-related claims made by CyberAv3ngers, including but not limited to:

1. Breaching transmission lines and power plants in Tel Aviv, Israel (April 13, 2024)
2. Breaching Israel Electric Corporations (April 12, 2024)
3. Selling data of unnamed Israeli electricity infrastructures (December 20, 2023)
4. Hacking SCADA systems in Yavne, Israel, following power outages (October 17, 2023)
5. Breaching Israeli automatic fueling company Orpak Systems (October 15, 2023)
6. Denial-of-distributed service (DDoS) attack against Dorad Power Stations (October 8, 2023)
7. Cyberattack against Israeli oil refinery operator BAZAN Group (November 14, 2023)
