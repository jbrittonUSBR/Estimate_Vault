(U) Al-Qaeda and the Islamic State are increasingly incorporating artificial intelligence into their digital strategies, yet they approach the technology with distinct philosophies and operational mindsets. While both extremist groups recognize AI's potential to streamline content creation, overcome language barriers, and expand their propaganda reach, their internal debates reflect broader differences in how they perceive risk, authenticity, and organizational authority. The Islamic State and its decentralized supporters have generally shown a willingness to experiment more aggressively with generative AI for visual media and messaging, sometimes sparking internal friction over digital authenticity. In contrast, al-Qaeda tends to exercise a more cautious, deliberate approach, reflecting divergent organizational priorities as both groups navigate the evolving technological landscape.
