(U) Five U.S. federal agencies issued a joint urgent advisory warning that malicious actors are actively using AI coding assistants to develop exploit scripts targeting internet-exposed industrial control systems.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Threat:** Agencies including the NSA, CISA, FBI, Department of Energy, and EPA warned that AI-driven attacks against critical infrastructure are **"not a theoretical risk"** but an active, ongoing campaign.
- **Targeting Siemens PLCs:** The campaign heavily targets internet-exposed **Siemens S7 Series Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs)** used across water, energy, manufacturing, and food sectors.
- **The AI Component:** Attackers are combining AI coding assistants with open-source automation libraries (`snap7.dll` and `python-snap7`) to quickly write custom exploitation scripts. These AI-generated tools are disguised to mimic legitimate operational technology (OT) monitoring software, giving hackers read and write access to PLC memory and ladder logic.
- **Lowering the Barrier:** Officials emphasized that AI significantly lowers the technical barrier and time required to mount sophisticated industrial control system attacks, bridging the gap between a known vulnerability and a working weaponized script.
- **Mitigation Guidance:** Operators are urged to immediately inventory their PLCs, apply critical security patches, ensure controllers are entirely disconnected from the public internet, and monitor networks for anomalous protocol traffic.
