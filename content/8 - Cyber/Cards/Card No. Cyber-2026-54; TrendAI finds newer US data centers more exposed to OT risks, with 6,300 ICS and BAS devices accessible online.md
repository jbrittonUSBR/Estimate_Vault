(U) A research report published by TrendAI revealed that thousands of industrial control systems (ICS) and building automation systems (BAS) near U.S. data centers are directly accessible via the public internet, leaving critical physical infrastructure exposed to potential cyber threats.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Findings:** Researchers identified **6,300 high-confidence ICS and BAS devices** (such as BACnet controllers, Tridium Niagara platforms, and specialized Vertiv/Liebert data center equipment) exposed to the internet within a one-kilometer radius of over 1,000 U.S. data centers.
- **Bypassing Traditional Security:** These exposed devices entirely bypass standard security models (like the Purdue Model's DMZ), creating direct pathways from the public internet to the physical equipment that controls climate, airflow, power distribution, and backup generators.
- **Newer Facilities Carry Higher Risk:** Counterintuitively, newer data centers permitted since 2021 showed an exposure rate of **13.1%**, which is more than double the **4.9%** rate found in pre-2010 facilities. This suggests that the rapid pace of modern deployments and remote-first building designs have prioritized speed over baseline security.
- **Physical and Operational Impact:** Because data centers rely heavily on precise climate and power management, compromising these exposed systems could allow attackers to trigger thermal alarms, force automated protective shutdowns, or disrupt overall availability.
- **Mitigation Recommendations:** Cybersecurity experts urge data center and utility operators to prioritize complete asset discovery, close unnecessary public internet interfaces, implement network micro-segmentation, and enforce multi-factor authentication for all remote access.
