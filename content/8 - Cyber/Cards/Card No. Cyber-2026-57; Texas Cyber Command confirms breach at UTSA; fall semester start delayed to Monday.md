(U) The University of Texas at San Antonio (UTSA) was forced to take key technology systems offline and delay the start of its fall semester following a detected cybersecurity breach.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Incident & Detection:** Unauthorized activity targeting the university's network was identified over a weekend just before classes were set to begin. University Technology Solutions detected the activity at the edge of the network and took immediate containment steps before it could penetrate core systems.
- **State Confirmation:** The newly formed **Texas Cyber Command** (led by chief TJ White) confirmed that UTSA was the victim of a data breach, noting that the university notified state officials early and coordinated well on forensic evaluations.
- **Semester Delay:** To ensure that digital tools, accounts, and student services were operating securely and optimally, UTSA President Taylor Eighmy announced that the start of the fall semester would be pushed back from Wednesday to the following Monday.
- **Operational Impact & Adjustments:** The outage temporarily cut off student access to online accounts, class schedules on Canvas, and financial services. In response, the university extended payment deadlines, kept registration and waitlist adjustments open, and instructed the campus community to await instructions for resetting account passphrases.
