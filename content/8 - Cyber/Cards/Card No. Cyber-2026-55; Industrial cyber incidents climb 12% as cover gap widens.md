(U) Global ransomware attacks on industrial organizations increased by 12% in the second quarter, fueling a widening gap in insurance protection as many businesses operate without proper cyber cover.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **Rising Global Incidents:** Data from operational technology (OT) security firm Dragos revealed 1,140 ransomware attacks on industrial entities worldwide in Q2 (up from 1,020 in Q1). Manufacturing was hardest hit, accounting for 65% of the total.
- **IT Systems Driving OT Disruption:** Interestingly, attackers did not directly manipulate industrial control systems (ICS). Instead, operational shutdowns occurred because attackers encrypted enterprise IT networks, ERP platforms, and virtualization layers that production lines depend on.
- **The Insurance Coverage Gap:** Many industrial businesses face a dangerous disconnect between their risk and insurance policies. Key insurance questions include whether standard policies cover IT-caused operational shutdowns (when the ICS itself wasn't breached), whether coverage triggers on data-theft extortion without file encryption, and if business interruption limits match extended industrial recovery timelines.
- **Shifting Tactics:** Extortion is increasingly shifting toward data-theft-only models (threatening to leak engineering documents or specs) alongside common tactics like credential theft and edge-device exploitation. Meanwhile, overall cyber insurance uptake among businesses continues to face pressure or downward trends.
