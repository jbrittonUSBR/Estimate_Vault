(U//FOUO) Qilin is a Russian-speaking cyber threat group that began its operation in 2022, initially under the name "Agenda". Throughout 2025 and into 2026, the group remains the most active ransomeware group. Qilin operates a ransomeware-as-a-service model, enabling affiliates to leverage its tools and infrastructure to conduct ransomeware attacks in exchange for a percentage of the proceeds.

(U//FOUO) Qilin is a financially-motivated criminal operation that offers a ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) payload platform to affiliate partners. The group experienced rapid growth after the disruption of groups like lockBit and ALPHV/BlackCat, absorbing affiliate partners like Scattered Spider, Moonstone Sleet', Pistachio Tempest", and STAC4365'. Qilin is funded via a profit-sharing model, with the group taking a 15-to-20-percent share of ransom payments. While Qilin and its affiliates have historically targeted healthcare and public health sector entities, recent trends indicate an increase in the targeting of government agencies, educational institutions, and critical infrastructure entities.
- (U//FOUO) In January 2026, Qilin posted on its dark website 18 images of exfiltrated data from an Oklahoma airport. The leak included financial records, internal emails, and employee ID inormation. Unlike previous posts from the group, the post did not describe the victim company or the exfiltrated data.
- (U//FOUO) In November 2025, Qilin posted on its dark website details of exfiltrated data from a las Vegas-based global provider of digital gaming, sports betting, and financial technology for casinos and online platforms. The attackers claimed approximately 10 gigabytes of data was stolen, with the data being marked as "publicated," indicating that the victim did not pay the groups ransom demands.
- (U//FOUO) In September 2025, Qilin posted on its dark website details of exfiltrated data from a Washington public utilities provider. The cyberattack caused network disruption of the victim's network and bill payment system. The group posted a proof sample of the exfiltrated data but did not describe the exfiltrated data.
- (U//FOUO) In September 2025, Qilin posted on its dark website details of exfiltrated data from a Louisiana law enforcement agency. While the cyberattack did not impact jail security operations, it did result in an outage in the agency's docket management system. The leak included contracts, inmate intake documents, and expense information. The post included screenshots of the exfiltrated data, which amounted to 842 gigabytes.
- (U//FOUO) On 1 August 2025, Qilin posted details on its dark website details of exfiltrated data from an Arkansas public school district, which included financial documents and employee payroll data. The incident resulted in a multi-day shutdown of all communication systems, though the incident did not disrupt school services.

(U//FOUO) **The RaaS Platform:** Qilin offers its affiliate partners access to their ransomware tools and infrastructure in exchange for a share of the affiliate partners' profits. Access to a RaaS platform often means an affiliate gains access to customizable malware, decryption keys, technical support, private forum access, payment processing portals, custom ransom notes, and secure negotiation tools. Qilin's RaaS platform offers cross-platform ransomware and supports data encryption and exfiltration (allowing double-extortion tactics'). In 2025, Qilin announced a "call lawyer" function for their affiliate panel, which allows
an affiliate partner to bring in legal counsel during the negotiation phase to increase pressure on victims.

(U//FOUO) **Affiliate Tradecraft:** Qilin provides ransomware and infrastructure, but the breach itself relies on affiliate tradecraft - specialized methods tailored to bypass a victim's defenses. Affiliates use a diverse range of entry vectors from spear-phishing to sophisticated zero-day exploitation of unpatched devices, servers, and software. Once inside the target victim's network, affiliates attempt to move laterally within the network in search of valuable data and ways to increase their permission levels to gain more access to the network and evade defenses.
