(U) In February 2024, a ransomware attack using the BackMyData strain hit Romania’s widely used Hippocrates medical software system, infecting 26 hospitals and disrupting operations across more than 100 facilities that relied on it for patient records, lab results, and prescriptions. Romania’s National Cyber-Security Directorate (DNSC), led by Dan Cimpean, responded by ordering the immediate disconnection of over 100 hospitals from the internet to contain the spread, forcing medical staff to revert to pen-and-paper records, offline Excel tools, and manual processes for registering patients, ordering tests, and managing care. The attackers demanded €160,000 in Bitcoin, but authorities refused to pay; instead, hospitals used recent backups to restore systems, with most returning online within about five days, though re-entering paper records took weeks. No deaths or serious patient harm were reported, and the coordinated response—combining rapid isolation, analogue workarounds, and technical recovery—was later praised internationally as a model for cyber resilience in healthcare. Experts noted that while greater digitization increases vulnerability, the ability to fall back on manual methods and the existence of recent backups proved critical in preventing a worse outcome during what became one of the most significant healthcare-targeted ransomware attacks globally.
