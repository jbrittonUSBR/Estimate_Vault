(U) The Five Eyes cyber security agencies from the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand issued a joint statement warning that frontier AI models are fundamentally transforming both offensive and defensive cyber capabilities on a timeline of months rather than years, dramatically accelerating the speed, scale, and sophistication of attacks while shrinking the window between vulnerability discovery and exploitation. The agencies stressed that AI is already here and lowers barriers for malicious actors, introduces new and zero-day vulnerabilities, and makes risk assumptions outdated quickly, while simultaneously offering powerful tools that defenders can use to detect issues earlier, improve software quality, monitor behavior, and respond faster. They urged boards and executives to treat cyber resilience as a core business risk and leadership responsibility rather than a purely technical issue, calling for urgent action to reduce attack surfaces, accelerate patching, address legacy systems, strengthen identity and access controls, adopt secure-by-design and defense-in-depth principles, and prepare for inevitable breaches through testing, training, and rapid containment. The statement emphasized that success will come from getting the basics right, acting quickly, integrating cybersecurity into core business strategy, and deliberately using AI in security operations, while warning that organizations failing to do so will face growing operational, financial, and strategic disadvantages in an increasingly hostile threat environment.
