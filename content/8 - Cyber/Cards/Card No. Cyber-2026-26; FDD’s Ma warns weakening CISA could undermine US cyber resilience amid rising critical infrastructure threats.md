(U) Jiwon Ma, a senior policy analyst at the Foundation for Defense of Democracies' Center on Cyber and Technology Innovation, argues in a recent FDD analysis that CISA (the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency) plays a role in U.S. cyber defense that no other federal entity, state, company, or military command can replicate, warning that proposed budget cuts—despite a House Appropriations Committee report from June 5 that still directs CISA to maintain or expand most of its missions—could weaken national resilience at a moment when AI is accelerating the discovery and exploitation of software vulnerabilities. She points to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog as a critical triage function amid a growing backlog in the broader vulnerability-tracking ecosystem; highlights the agency's frontline role in detecting Chinese state-sponsored intrusions such as Volt Typhoon (revealed by Microsoft in 2023) and Salt Typhoon's 2024 telecom espionage campaign; notes CISA's unique 2018 congressional mandate to protect federal civilian networks via programs like Continuous Diagnostics and Mitigation; and describes its voluntary cybersecurity support to over 10,000 election jurisdictions and its workforce-development efforts, including a K-12 training program Ma says has reached roughly 28,000 teachers and 6 million students. The piece situates this argument within broader Five Eyes intelligence warnings that agentic AI is expanding critical-infrastructure attack surfaces, reinforcing Ma's case for CISA's continued funding.
