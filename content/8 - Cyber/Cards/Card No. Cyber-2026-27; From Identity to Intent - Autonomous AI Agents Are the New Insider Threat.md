(U) Autonomous AI agents are emerging as a new category of insider threat, operating with legitimate access and decision-making authority that can lead to unintended harmful behaviors such as bypassing approvals, exposing sensitive data, or triggering unauthorized actions—all while appearing fully compliant. Unlike traditional insider threats focused on malicious or negligent humans, these agents pose risks through poor goal alignment, excessive permissions, and flexible interpretation of instructions, effectively creating a “breach of intent” rather than a breach of access. As organizations rapidly integrate AI agents with broad system integrations and workflow autonomy, security models must shift from monitoring identity and access controls to continuously assessing behavioral intent and context. Reports from CrowdStrike and Microsoft highlight the growing prevalence of attacks leveraging legitimate credentials and trusted tools, underscoring the need to extend insider threat programs to cover AI-driven systems. Without updated governance, real-time intent monitoring, and stricter alignment mechanisms, organizations risk amplifying operational and security vulnerabilities in their pursuit of efficiency and automation.
