(U) **Qilin Ransomware Claims 1,358 Victims as Global Attacks Reach New Record** (Cyber Security News, Tushar Subhra Dutta, July 21, 2026) Qilin ransomware has become one of the most prominent global extortion threats, encrypting systems, stealing data, and pressuring victims with public leak threats. According to a Black Kite report covering April 2025–March 2026, the group claimed **1,358 victims**—a 443% increase from the prior year. It operated in more than 50 countries and accounted for roughly one in every five to six disclosed ransomware victims.

**Broader ransomware trends**:
- Black Kite recorded 7,551 publicly disclosed victims overall in the period (up 24.9% year-over-year).
- March 2026 alone hit a record 861 victims.
- By June 2026 there were 146 active ransomware operations.
- The top five groups controlled 43.6% of disclosed victims, with no single actor dominating as in previous years.
- Manufacturing was the most targeted sector (1,660 victims), followed by professional, scientific, and technical services (1,389).
- Mid-sized organizations ($50–100 million revenue) made up a growing share of victims.

(U) Qilin has used techniques such as abusing RDP history and exploiting flaws in internet-facing systems (e.g., PAN-OS authentication weaknesses) for rapid access and deployment.

**Post-incident risks**:
(U) Black Kite’s scans found that even after recovery, many victims remained exposed: 43.5% still had critical patch vulnerabilities and 30.8% had known-exploited vulnerabilities. The report stresses treating recovery as ongoing, with structured reviews at 30/60/90 days focusing on stolen credentials, critical vulnerabilities, and the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. It also highlights risks from third-party applications and OAuth token abuse. Recommendations include prioritizing patches based on active exploitation, inventorying connected apps, reviewing OAuth permissions, rotating credentials after suspicious activity, and enforcing multi-factor authentication.
