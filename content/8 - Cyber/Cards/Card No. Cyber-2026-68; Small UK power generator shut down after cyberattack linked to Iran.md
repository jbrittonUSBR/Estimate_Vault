(U) Hackers linked to the Iranian regime carried out a cyberattack that successfully forced a small British power plant offline for four days in July. According to reports, this is believed to be the first successful state-backed cyber intrusion to shut down a UK energy generation facility.
### **Key Details**

- **The Target:** The incident involved a small-scale, gas-fired "peaker" plant with a capacity of roughly 15 megawatts. Because of the facility's small size, the shutdown had no meaningful impact on the broader UK electricity supply or the national grid.
- **Impact & Recovery:** Staff spent four days working to restore operational control to the facility. British authorities declined to publicly name the exact plant due to ongoing security concerns.
- **Broader Context:** The timing of the attack coincided with a wave of cyberattacks targeting community water and critical infrastructure systems across multiple states in the U.S.
- **Official Response:** While the UK's Department for Energy Security and Net Zero and the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) stressed that the wider energy system was never at risk, the government has since briefed energy sector executives and issued updated cybersecurity guidance to guard against similar threats.
