(U) The Five Eyes intelligence agencies from the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand have issued a joint warning that frontier AI models are transforming the cyber threat landscape on a timeline of months rather than years, dramatically accelerating the speed, scale, and sophistication of attacks while shrinking the window between vulnerability discovery and exploitation. The agencies urged boards and executives to treat cybersecurity as a core business and leadership responsibility rather than a purely technical issue, calling for urgent steps to reduce attack surfaces, accelerate patching, strengthen identity and access controls, address legacy systems, and prepare for inevitable breaches through secure-by-design principles, defense-in-depth strategies, and deliberate integration of AI into security operations. They emphasized that organizations must assume breaches will occur and focus on rapid detection, containment, and recovery to protect operational continuity, market confidence, and long-term value, while also noting that AI tools can equally empower defenders to detect issues earlier, improve software quality, and respond faster. Experts commenting on the warning highlighted particular risks to embedded systems, operational technology, and critical infrastructure where patching cycles are slow, stressing the need to make vulnerabilities harder to exploit through resilience-focused design and calling on governments and AI labs to give defenders faster access to advanced models rather than limiting them to pilot or trusted-partner programs. The statement reinforces that success will come from getting the basics right quickly and embedding cybersecurity into core business strategy, while those that delay will face growing operational and strategic disadvantages in an AI-accelerated threat environment.
