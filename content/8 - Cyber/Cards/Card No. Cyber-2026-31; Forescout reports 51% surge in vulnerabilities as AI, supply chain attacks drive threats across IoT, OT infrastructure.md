(U) **Forescout reports 51% surge in vulnerabilities as AI, supply chain attacks drive threats across IoT, OT infrastructure** (Industrial Cyber, July 22, 2026)

Forescout’s 2026 H1 Threat Review highlights a sharp rise in cybersecurity threats driven by sophisticated supply chain attacks, growing abuse of AI by threat actors, and continued exploitation of specialized network, IoT, OT, and IoMT devices. The firm tracked activity from 107 threat actors, with China-, Russia-, and Iran-linked groups accounting for 32%. The U.S., U.K., Germany, France, and India were the most frequently targeted countries; government, technology, financial services, education, and healthcare were the most attacked sectors.

**Key findings**:
- **Vulnerabilities**: Published vulnerabilities rose 51% compared with the previous period. More than half (55%) of newly disclosed flaws were rated high or critical; 54 were exploited as zero-days. CISA added 146 CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog (up 11% from H1 2025); nearly half (46%) of those had been published before 2026. AI-assisted research may be accelerating discovery, though the exact contribution is unclear.
- **Ransomware**: Claimed attacks increased 25% to 4,544 incidents (average ~757 per month / ~25 per day). Active ransomware groups rose 16% (from 89 to 103). The U.S. accounted for 44% of incidents; professional/business services, manufacturing, technology, retail, and healthcare were the most targeted industries.
- **Hacktivism**: More than 5,700 attack claims were tracked across 98 Telegram channels (mainly DDoS, system compromises, data breaches, and defacements). Israel, the U.S., Ukraine, Indonesia, and Iran were the top targets.

**Supply chain attacks**:
Notable incidents included compromises involving Trivy, Checkmarx KICS, LiteLLM, Telnyx Python SDK, TanStack CI/CD, Nx Console, Vercel OAuth (via Lumma Stealer and stolen tokens), Axios npm packages (North Korea-linked Sapphire Sleet/UNC1069, ~600,000 downloads of malicious versions), node-ipc, and multiple Laravel-Lang packages. Attackers focused on trusted developer tools and software ecosystems.

**AI and geopolitics**:
Frontier AI models (e.g., Anthropic’s Mythos, GPT-5.5-Cyber) are identifying vulnerabilities at large scale. Google reported what it assessed as the first known use of an AI-generated exploit for a zero-day in a real attack. Geopolitical tensions (especially Middle East escalations) continue to drive cyber activity, often progressing from noisy claims/DDoS to higher-impact intrusions and opportunistic crime.

**Iranian-linked activity**:
Detailed coverage of MOIS- and IRGC-linked groups (including MuddyWater, APT34, APT35, APT42, CyberAv3ngers, etc.) using credential theft, social engineering, vulnerability exploitation, custom malware (e.g., IOCONTROL for OT/ICS), living-off-the-land techniques, and unconventional C2 methods (Ethereum smart contracts, Telegram bots, cloud services).

**Recommendations**:
Extend visibility and controls across network perimeter, OT, healthcare, and IoT assets; reduce exposure of management interfaces (VPN, RDP, SSH, edge devices) with allowlisting, MFA, and logging; prioritize patching of internet-facing and high-consequence systems; validate segmentation between IT/OT/medical networks; and prepare for disruptive scenarios beyond ransomware.
