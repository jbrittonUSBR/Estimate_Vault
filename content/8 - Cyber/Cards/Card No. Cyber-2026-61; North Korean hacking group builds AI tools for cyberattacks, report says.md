(U) A cybersecurity report revealed that the North Korean state-backed hacking group **Kimsuky** has begun building and organizing local artificial intelligence tools to automate cyberattacks, analyze stolen data, and generate highly convincing spear-phishing campaigns.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **Local AI Infrastructure:** According to South Korean cybersecurity firm Genians, Kimsuky set up tools to run and manage large language models (LLMs) locally—including **Ollama, GPT4All, and Msty**—alongside retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) technology.
- **Operational Privacy:** By hosting these frameworks locally rather than using external cloud-based services, the hackers can process sensitive documents and draft attack materials without leaking data or triggering external safety filters.
- **Expanding Capabilities:** Beyond simple text generation for phishing lures, researchers found evidence of AI-assisted coding tools (**Cursor**), speech-to-text software, and agent development frameworks. This indicates a shift toward integrating AI directly into malware development and attack automation.
- **AI-Generated Decoys:** The group has leveraged AI to produce sophisticated finance- and cryptocurrency-themed decoy documents designed to mimic legitimate investment reports to trick targets in government, academia, and defense.
- **Broader Context:** The findings highlight how state-sponsored threat actors are increasingly leveraging generative AI to scale up cyber-espionage and financial theft operations, bypassing traditional skill barriers and accelerating attack lifecycles.
