(U) A Congressional Research Service (CRS) report highlights how the rise of **agentic artificial intelligence**—systems capable of operating with limited human supervision—has escalated cybersecurity threats, pushing governments to adapt their defense and policy frameworks.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Threat of Agentic AI:** Autonomous AI agents allow threat actors (ranging from state-sponsored groups to smaller criminal syndicates) to automate complex cyberattacks. These systems can analyze targets, generate exploitable code, and sift through stolen data much faster and more efficiently than human hackers.
- **Real-World Precedent:** The report points to a notable milestone in mid-September 2025, when Anthropic detected a sophisticated cyber espionage campaign by a Chinese state-sponsored group (labeled GTG-1002). The operation automated 80% to 90% of a large-scale attack against roughly 30 global organizations, with humans only stepping in for high-level strategic decisions.
- **Defense & Military Integration:** Militaries are increasingly exploring agentic AI for defense and simulation. For example, the Department of Defense’s AIR program trains AI pilots and autonomous robotic wingmen to make split-second decisions at speeds beyond human capability.
- **Congressional Guardrails:** To counter these emerging threats, the fiscal year 2026 National Defense Authorization Act directed the Pentagon to develop comprehensive counter-AI strategies. Additionally, the AI Futures Steering Committee was tasked with analyzing adversarial AI developments, with a formal report due to congressional defense committees by early 2027.
