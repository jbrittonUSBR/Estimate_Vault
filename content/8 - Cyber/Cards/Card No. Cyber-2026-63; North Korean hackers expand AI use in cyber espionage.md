(U) A cybersecurity report published by South Korean firm Genians revealed that state-sponsored North Korean hacking groups—specifically the notorious **Kimsuky** collective—are rapidly expanding their use of artificial intelligence to automate cyber operations, analyze stolen data, and scale up spear-phishing campaigns.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **Local and Offline AI Infrastructure:** Rather than relying on public or cloud-based AI systems that trigger safety filters or leak data, Kimsuky has set up local, offline frameworks to run large language models (LLMs) like **Ollama, GPT4All, and Msty**.
- **Advanced Integration:** Alongside localized LLMs, researchers discovered retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) technology, AI agent frameworks, speech-to-text software, and AI-assisted coding tools like **Cursor**. This indicates a shift toward embedding AI directly into malware development, data analysis, and attack automation.
- **High-Quality Social Engineering:** The group has heavily utilized AI to mass-produce polished, highly convincing decoy documents—such as finance- and cryptocurrency-themed investment reports—to target professionals in diplomacy, military, and academic sectors without detection.
- **Broader State Tactics:** The findings underscore how Pyongyang continues to leverage advanced technologies to refine its cyber espionage and financial theft operations, which are heavily used to fund the regime's strategic objectives.
