(U) The U.S. Army’s DEVCOM Armaments Center has developed a new counter-unmanned aerial systems (C-UAS) fire-control system that integrates the Gunslinger technology with the Common Remotely Operated Weapon Station (CROWS), enabling ground vehicles to detect, track, and destroy hostile drones while moving. Tested successfully at Aberdeen Proving Ground in April 2026, the system combines advanced fire-control algorithms with multiple vehicle sensor feeds for real-time tracking and precise aiming, addressing the growing threat from commercially available and military-grade drones seen in recent global conflicts. Its modular open-system architecture supports easy integration across Army platforms and future upgrades, with the aim of boosting first-round hit probabilities to reduce ammunition use. Project officer Nick Cascia and deputy project lead James Little described the tests as a promising start that demonstrated effective drone engagement, with further development planned for higher vehicle speeds and faster drone targets to better replicate combat conditions. This mobile capability strengthens layered air defense and battlefield survivability against evolving drone threats in modern warfare.
