(U) **Exclusive: US, Ukraine Reach Breakthrough Agreement On Drone Cooperation** (RFE/RL, Alex Raufoglu, July 22, 2026) Ukraine and the United States have signed a bilateral statement of intent establishing a framework for deeper drone cooperation. According to two anonymous sources familiar with the deal, this is the first agreement of its kind between the two countries and allows Ukrainian combat drones (land, air, and maritime) to be exported to the United States for military testing and evaluation.

The statement of intent is not a production contract but creates a formal pathway that could inform future Pentagon procurement and eventually support joint manufacturing of Ukrainian-designed drones on U.S. soil. It follows months of negotiations as the Pentagon seeks rapid access to Ukraine’s low-cost, combat-proven unmanned systems developed during more than four years of war with Russia. The move aligns with the U.S. “Drone Dominance” initiative.

Analysts described it as a significant step. Dan Rice (president of American University Kyiv and former adviser to Ukraine’s armed forces) called it “a giant leap forward for American defense,” emphasizing the need for the U.S. to learn from Ukrainian wartime innovation. Daniel Balson of Razom for Ukraine highlighted that Ukrainian drones are battle-tested and among the best in the world.
### Related development
On July 21, the Ukrainian Embassy announced a memorandum of understanding between Ukrainian firm UFORCE and U.S. shipbuilder ReconCraft. This will enable production of the combat-proven MAGURA family of autonomous maritime drones in the United States (starting with 24-foot vessels), with potential scale-up to hundreds or thousands of units for possible U.S. Navy use. The partnership expands cooperation on unmanned aerial, maritime, and ground systems and follows recent U.S. combat use of unmanned surface vessels.

For the United States, the broader agreement offers access to rapidly evolving, affordable drone technologies. For Ukraine, it advances integration of its defense industry into Western supply chains.
