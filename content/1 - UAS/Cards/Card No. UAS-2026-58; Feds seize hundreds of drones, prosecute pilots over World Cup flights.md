(U) Federal authorities seized more than 700 drones flown over or near 2026 FIFA World Cup venues and are fining or prosecuting more than 350 pilots for violating temporary flight restrictions. Officials called it the largest civilian anti-drone operation in U.S. history, with roughly $500 million spent on related software and systems across 11 sites. Many operators were recreational flyers seeking views of the games, while others appeared to test the boundaries. Enforcement used jamming, radar, cameras, and net-firing interceptor drones (including systems from Fortem Technologies). Miami, Houston, Atlanta, and Dallas saw the highest activity. Violators face fines up to $10,000 and possible criminal charges; several individuals have already been charged.
