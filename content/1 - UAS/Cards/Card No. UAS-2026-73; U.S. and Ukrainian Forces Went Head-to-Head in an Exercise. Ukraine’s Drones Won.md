(U) During a major NATO war game (**Combined Resolve**) at a training range in Hohenfels, Germany, U.S. armored forces went head-to-head against battle-tested Ukrainian drone operators and suffered a decisive defeat.
### **Key Details**

- **The Exercise & Scenario:** The training involved roughly 3,500 U.S. troops (primarily from an armored brigade on rotation from Texas) tasked with attacking positions defended by a mock enemy backed by Ukraine's **412th Unmanned Systems Forces ("Nemesis" regiment)**.
- **The Drone Advantage:** Heavy U.S. armored vehicles kicked up thick dust clouds, making them easy targets for Ukrainian reconnaissance. Ukrainian pilots quickly simulated strikes using FPV kamikaze drones and drone-dropped munitions.
- **Devastating Efficiency:** The simulated destruction of American hardware happened so rapidly that "eliminated" U.S. vehicles had to be continuously rotated back into the exercise as fresh formations just to keep the training scenario running.
- **Adaptation and Lessons Learned:** Following the initial setbacks, U.S. forces adapted by improving camouflage, dispersing more effectively, and better utilizing electronic warfare systems. Later in the exercise, Ukrainian operators fought alongside American forces to demonstrate offensive drone capabilities.
- **Broader Implications:** Military officials noted that this highlights a wider trend: Western militaries are urgently revising their tactics, recognizing that traditional heavy armor and troop concentrations are fundamentally vulnerable to cheap, transparent, and sophisticated drone warfare perfected on Ukraine's front lines.
