(U) **Feds seize hundreds of drones, prosecute pilots over World Cup flights** (USA TODAY, Trevor Hughes, updated July 21, 2026) Federal authorities seized more than 700 drones that flew over or near 2026 FIFA World Cup venues and are fining or prosecuting more than 350 pilots for violating temporary flight restrictions. The FBI and Department of Transportation described this as the largest civilian anti-drone enforcement effort in U.S. history. Software used to manage operations at 11 sites cost taxpayers about $500 million.

Homeland Security officials detected more than 1,500 drones during the events (which drew an estimated 6.8 million fans), including hundreds found before takeoff. Many operators were unaware of the restrictions or simply sought aerial views of the games; others appeared to deliberately test boundaries. Officials noted that even inexpensive consumer drones can pose security risks, drawing parallels to their use in conflicts such as Ukraine.

Enforcement was heaviest in Miami, Houston, Atlanta, and Dallas. Violators face fines of up to $10,000 and possible criminal charges. Examples include four Texans charged for flying in a restricted Houston area and a Mexican national facing up to three years in prison for flying near AT&T Stadium in Dallas.

Counter-drone methods included radar-and-camera systems, jamming to backtrack signals to pilots, and technologies such as Fortem Technologies’ autonomous net-firing drones. The Department of Homeland Security, FBI, and FAA coordinated the intercepts. FBI Director Kash Patel stated that authorities would not risk distinguishing between minor and more serious intent: “If you’re in the space, we’re going to take you down.”
