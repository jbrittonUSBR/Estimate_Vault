(U) South Korea's Ministry of National Defense announced on 26 June plans to rapidly expand its unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) and counter-unmanned aircraft systems (C-UAS) capabilities by procuring 20,000 low-cost UAVs, including short-range reconnaissance platforms and loitering munitions similar to the US Low-cost Unmanned Combat Attack System (LUCAS) used in Operation ‘Epic Fury' against Iran, while also accelerating AI-enabled systems for UAV swarm operations. The initiative aims to strengthen the Republic of Korea Armed Forces' strike operations, including suppression of enemy air defences, through expendable and cost-effective platforms that may be procured from the US or produced domestically under a potential ‘Korean-LUCAS' variant. In the short term, C-UAS will be deployed to counter small UAV threats along border areas and forward operating bases, with commercially sourced systems scheduled to enter service from 2027, alongside efforts to expand trained personnel. The plans build on LUCAS specifications of approximately 10 ft in length, 8 ft wingspan, and unit costs ranging from USD10,000 to USD55,000, designed to emulate systems like the Iranian Shahed 136.
