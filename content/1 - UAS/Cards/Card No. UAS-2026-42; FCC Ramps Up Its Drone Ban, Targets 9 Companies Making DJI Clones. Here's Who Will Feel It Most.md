(U) **FCC Ramps Up Its Drone Ban, Targets 9 Companies Making DJI Clones. Here's Who Will Feel It Most** (PCMag, Jim Fisher, July 21, 2026)

The FCC is escalating its restrictions on DJI-related products by retroactively revoking device approvals for nine companies that sell what it describes as clones of DJI drones and cameras. These brands are expected to be added to the FCC Covered List after a required 30-day public comment period (around mid-August 2026, barring a change). Placement on the Covered List effectively prevents new FCC authorizations and is described as a major barrier to selling consumer electronics in the US.

This is notable as the first time the FCC has used its authority to pull prior approvals in this context. It builds on earlier actions that placed DJI on the Covered List and broader foreign drone restrictions under the 2025 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) Section 1709, which covers not only DJI but also entities linked via design, manufacture, assembly, development, subsidiaries, affiliates, partners, joint ventures, or technology-sharing/licensing agreements.
### Key companies and products affected
- **Skyrover (via SZ Knowact)**: Clones of DJI Mini-series drones (e.g., Skyrover X1 matching the Mini 4 Pro in hardware, specs, app/API behavior, and interface; yellow vs. gray finish). The related Skyrover S1 (Mini 3 variant) was certified under WaveGo.
- **Xtra**: Rebranded/cloned DJI Osmo action and vlogging cameras (Muse ≈ Pocket 3; Sphra360 ≈ Osmo 360; Edge ≈ Action 4; Edge Pro ≈ Action 5; Atto ≈ Nano). Upcoming Muse 2 / Muse 2 Pro (clones of the banned Osmo Pocket 4 / 4P) will also be blocked from the US market.
- **Others**:
  - Cogito: Specta Mini (≈ Mini 4K) and Specta Air (≈ Air 3).
  - Fikaxo: At least one drone matching a DJI Lito variant (from FCC filings).
  - Lyno Dynamics: LD220 agricultural UAV and related accessories (based on DJI Agras-series).
  - Skyhigh Tech: Agricultural crop-sprayer drone (DJI Agras-like).
  - Spatial Hover: Versions of the Mavic 3 Enterprise (e.g., T-series with thermal camera).
  - WaveGo: Linked to the Skyrover S1.
  - XAG: Agricultural drones; FCC asserts underlying DJI technology despite differing appearance.

DJI has not publicly acknowledged involvement in these brands. Investigative reporting (e.g., from DroneXL) has noted strong hardware, software, and app similarities for products like the Skyrover X1. Original DJI models that these clone remain approved and available for now, though the article notes the possibility of future action against DJI’s back catalog.

### Impact and context
Content creators and enthusiasts who viewed these as workarounds after DJI’s restrictions will be affected, especially for cameras (Xtra’s removal closes a remaining path for newer Osmo-style products). New foreign drones were already broadly restricted. Alternatives mentioned include products like the Insta360 Luna Ultra for certain vlogging needs. A separate FCC action also targets a drone from Odyssey Robot.

The piece frames this as further escalation in US government actions against Chinese drone/camera technology over security concerns, with more bans possible.
