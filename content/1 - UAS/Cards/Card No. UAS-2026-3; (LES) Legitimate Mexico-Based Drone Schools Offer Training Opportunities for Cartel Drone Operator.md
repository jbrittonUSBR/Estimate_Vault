(U//LES) DHS CBP. "Legitimate Mexico-Based Drone Schools Offer Training Opportunities for Cartel Drone Operator." Serial: INB-NIW-26-3033645, March 20, 2026. HSIN. Accessed April 3, 2026. 

(U//LES) Commercially operated drone schools in Mexico offer access to expert-level drone assembly, repair, and operational training (for both recreational and commercial applications) that cartels could leverage in their illicit drone operations. These schools are accessible via open registration with minimal requirements beyond payment and proof of Mexican citizenship, and the school websites do not list a requirement for clients to submit a criminal history. The Government of Mexico (GoM) has officially recognized the need for improved regulation of its drone ecosystem and has enacted new air protection rules via its Ministry of National Defense (SEDENA) to address safety and security concerns. While there is currently no report of overt ties between Mexican cartels and Mexican drone school training programs, cartels’ ability to access legitimate drone training will likely enhance their tactical and operational experience, presenting a concern for U.S. security efforts and personnel along the U.S. Southwest Border (SWB).
(U) There are numerous schools in Mexico offering training on both recreational and commercial Da-Jiang Innovations (DJI) models and Remotely Piloted Aerial Systems (RPAS) certification programs needed to operate drones over 25 kilograms (kg) in Mexico. These include schools located in Los Mochis, Sinaloa State; Las Amapolas, Veracruz State; and Penjamo, Guanajuato State, which offer training to individuals, commercial drone operators, and anyone wishing to have professional classroom or field training. DJI emphasizes that its program provides training in drone assembly/disassembly, flight simulation, maintenance, application training; and online video.
(U) DJI’s Enterprise Mexico Consulting Group, located in Puebla, Mexico, offers a wide range of GoM-authorized drone training programs, including a certification course which complies with GoM requirements to obtain a RPAS Pilot License for drones over 25 kg, as well as operate any kind of equipment for recreational, commercial, and private non-commercial purposes. These courses are advertised as in-person or online.
(U) CraMex Aerospace, located in Mexico City, offers drone training that includes an advanced course on the use and understanding of aeronautical radio communications via the Restricted Aeronautical Radio Operator Certification (RTAR) course at the Air Traffic Control Center (ATC, CTR). CraMex courses emphasize an understanding of aeronautical radio communications, VHF band communication equipment, standard aviation phrases, and air traffic control messages.
(U//LES) The NIW notes that CBP has consistently recovered DJI drones used by narcotics and human smugglers along the SWB. Mexican cartels seem to prefer DJI drones for surveillance and assault operations. Cartels’ preference for DJI drones is likely due to their low price point and the accessibility of DJI drones via online markets. Of note, the GoMlicensed drone schools do not exclude clients from any specific size or make and model of drone training.
