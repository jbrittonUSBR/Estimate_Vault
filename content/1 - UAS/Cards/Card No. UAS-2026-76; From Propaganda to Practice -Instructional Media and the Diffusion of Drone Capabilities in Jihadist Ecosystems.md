(U) The article examines how instructional media and digital propaganda produced by groups like the Islamic State (IS) are successfully diffusing technical drone capabilities to isolated, online extremists. Rather than remaining isolated to organized conflict zones, the knowledge required to build, modify, and weaponize drones is increasingly being packaged into structured, step-by-step digital manuals for decentralized actors.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **Evolution of Instructional Guides:** Extremist media entities (such as the _Modern Warfare_ series and publications linked to groups like the Al Saqri Foundation) have shifted from broad propaganda to highly technical, accretive manuals. These guides cover everything from the basics of small quadcopters to advanced 3D-printed parts (like custom camera mounts, landing gear, and rotary drums) and weaponization.
- **Lowering Barriers to Entry:** The structured nature of these tutorials significantly lowers the technical barrier for aspiring attackers, allowing younger and less scientifically trained individuals to conceptualize drone-based attacks (such as combining explosives with "bomb drones").
- **Adapting to Counterterrorism:** Faced with increased digital enforcement, extremist networks have adapted their distribution methods—utilizing decentralized chat apps (like Element), direct PDF uploads, file-sharing "dead drops," and secure messaging channels to keep their manuals accessible.
- **Real-World Impact:** The report highlights concrete instances where these digital ecosystems intersect with real-world plots, noting that isolated individuals and younger extremists are increasingly attempting to integrate drones into their attack planning.
### **Conclusion**

(U) The diffusion of drone capabilities highlights a shifting threat landscape where digital instructional media bridges the gap between extremist intent and technical execution. As tutorials become more sophisticated, security agencies face a growing challenge in countering the proliferation of open-source terrorist innovation online.
