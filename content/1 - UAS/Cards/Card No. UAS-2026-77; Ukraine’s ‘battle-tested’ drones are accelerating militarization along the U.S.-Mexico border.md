(U) The report investigates how drone technologies and surveillance strategies **battle-tested in the Russia-Ukraine war** are being adopted by law enforcement and border agencies along the **U.S.–Mexico border**. This pipeline of military innovation is accelerating the militarization of domestic American borderlands.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **From Foreign Battlefields to Domestic Borders:** Technologies originally adapted by Ukraine to counter a larger military force—such as modified off-the-shelf commercial drones, enhanced thermal cameras, and extended-range antennas—are being integrated into U.S. border enforcement.
- **The "Invasion" Rhetoric and Expansion:** Fueled by billions of dollars in federal funding and political rhetoric describing immigration as an "invasion" or "war," agencies are utilizing dual-purpose military-grade tech to create a virtual wall of surveillance.
- **Efficiency vs. Oversight Concerns:** Border guards note that a single drone can replace dozens of personnel for round-the-clock monitoring. However, civil liberties and defense experts warn that regulatory oversight has failed to keep pace, blurring the lines between foreign military combat tools and domestic policing.
- **Global Tech Pipeline:** Training centers originally established in Ukraine to meet wartime needs (such as Dronarium) have expanded globally, symbolizing how modern conflict zones have become incubators for surveillance technology used internationally.
### **Conclusion**

(U) The adoption of Ukrainian frontline drone tactics highlights a broader trend where wartime innovations quickly bleed into domestic law enforcement, raising ongoing questions about privacy, civil liberties, and the expanding footprint of surveillance on U.S. soil.
