(U//SSI) Transportation Security Administration Intelligence and Analysis (TSA I&A) assesses threat actors are unlikely to conduct unmanned aircraft system (UAS) attacks against the transportation domain in the Homeland because they likely lack the intent to do so, despite having some capability. In 2025, UASs disrupted aviation operations in the Homeland in 74 incidents that affected airport or flight operations (10), caused a near miss or required a pilot to take evasive action (28), or were operating or recovered within an airport perimeter (36). There were two reported incidents of a UAS collision with an aircraft. We assess negligent recreational UAS use remains the predominant threat posed by UASs to the transportation environment based on trends observed in the majority of reported 2025 UAS incidents.

- (U//SSI) During 2025, TSA received 443 reports of UAS incidents in the Homeland, the majority of which were related to aviation transportation as opposed to surface modes of transportation. There are no indications threat actors were responsible for any of the 2025 UAS incidents in the Homeland. However, we lack detailed UAS operator information because the operators are rarely located following a reported incident, though the use of electronic systems is aiding the location of operators whose negligent UAS flights affect the aviation domain.
- (U//SSI) Of the 443 incidents reported, there were 316 aviation incidents, 45 freight rail incidents, 76 hazardous liquid and natural gas (HLNG) pipeline incidents, 5 mass transit and passenger rail (MTPR) incidents, and 1 and highway motor carrier (HMC) incident.

(U//SSI) Of the 443 UAS incidents reported, 316 (71 percent) were related to aviation. TSA’s five categories for UAS aviation-related incidents are: sightings outside of the airport (240 incidents or 76 percent of the total); UAS operating within the airport perimeter (36 incidents or 11 percent of the total); activity affecting airport or flight operations (10 incidents or 3 percent of the total); incidents requiring pilots to take evasive action (28 incidents or 9 percent of the total); and collisions with aircraft (2 incidents or 1 percent of the total). Overall, aviation incidents in 2025 decreased by 2.47 percent compared to 2024 and by 49 percent compared to 2023. Significant incidents that disrupted airport operations are described below:

- (U//SSI) On 9 January, a UAS collided with an aircraft conducting firefighting operations for the Palisades fire in Los Angeles County, California. When the aircraft returned to Van Nuys Airport (VNY), Van Nuys, California, the post-flight inspection found the UAS embedded in the leading edge of its left wing.
- (U//SSI) On 7 January at Tulsa International Airport (TUL), Tulsa, Oklahoma, a UAS was observed flying between 900 – 1,700 feet to the west of the airport. The UAS reportedly interfered with aircraft flight patterns, so all aircraft were moved to the south side of the airport.
- (U//SSI) On 2 June at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL), Atlanta, Georgia, aircrews on multiple aircraft observed a red and blue quadcopter UAS near Runway 26L. Airport Operations closed Runway 26L for 30 minutes and moved all flight operations to Runway 26R.
- (U//SSI) On 3 December at Raleigh-Durham International Airport (RDU), Raleigh, North Carolina, a commercial aircraft pilot reported a UAS .5 miles during approach to Runway 23R. The UAS was also observed at Taxiway Charlie, which is in the middle of the airport. Air Traffic Control stopped flight operations for 15 minutes until the UAS departed.

(U//SSI) Of note, in 2025 there were 36 incidents where a UAS flew within the perimeter of an airport. Airport employees and security personnel found seven UASs within the perimeters of domestic airports in 2025: one in the first quarter and two each in the subsequent quarters. The UASs most likely experienced battery depletion or loss of signal between the UAS and the operator’s controller. We did not receive any reports of a UAS striking an aircraft within an airport’s perimeter. The following are significant examples of UASs found within an airport perimeter:

- (U//SSI) On 13 January, an employee of a construction company notified Houston Airport System (HAS) operations at George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH), Houston, Texas, that they had lost communication with an authorized UAS operating near a construction area by Terminal 2. Because the UAS was operating near the departure runways, the FAA closed them for 40 minutes while HAS searched for and recovered the UAS.
- (U//SSI) On 10 May, a UAS falling from the sky almost hit a ramp agent near a Boeing 787 on the air operations area outside Terminal C at Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR), Newark, New Jersey. FAMS and Port Authority Police responded and confiscated the UAS, which was described as small, yellow, with four propellers, and the number nine on its body. There was no impact to flight operations nor was the UAS operator identified.
- (U//SSI) On 11 July at Dallas-Fort Worth International Airport (DFW), Dallas, Texas, Airport Operations responding to a report of Foreign Object Debris check at Gate 17 discovered remnants of a UAS. The UAS was collected and retained for further investigation.
- (U//SSI) On 5 November at Miami International Airport (MIA), Miami, Florida, a commercial airline ramp worker discovered a black quadcopter UAS on the ground in front of the airline’s hangar within the air operations area. Miami-Dade Sheriff’s Office responded and confiscated the UAS.
