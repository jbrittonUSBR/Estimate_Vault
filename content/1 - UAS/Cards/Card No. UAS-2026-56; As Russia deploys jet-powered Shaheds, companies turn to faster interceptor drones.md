(U) Russia has been deploying increasing numbers of faster, jet-propelled versions of its Shahed (Geran) attack drones. These can reach speeds of up to about 500 km/h (311 mph), which shortens the time available for detection and interception compared with the standard propeller-driven models. 

(U) In response, several companies are developing higher-speed interceptor drones:

- (U) SkyFall (Ukrainian manufacturer) unveiled the P1-SUN Jetkiller at the Farnborough Airshow. It is a faster version of their existing interceptor (claimed top speed ~370 km/h vs. the prior model’s ~310 km/h). The company says it has already been battlefield-tested and has downed more than a dozen jet-powered Shaheds, with mass production planned to start in August. Their regular P1-SUN is claimed to have hit more than 5,500 Shaheds since November 2025.
- (U) General Cherry (another Ukrainian firm) is developing a faster version of its Bullet interceptor, aiming for speeds over 400 km/h (and potentially up to 500 km/h). It is still finalizing propulsion (electric vs. small turbo-jet) and hopes for front-line testing in early August and mass production in September, while trying to keep costs near the current Bullet’s roughly $2,000 price point.
(U) Firebolt Engineering (British-Ukrainian company) has a jet-powered Griffen interceptor currently reaching ~350 km/h. It is working with Britain’s Dynamic Propulsion on a new engine to push speeds toward ~400 km/h. Pricing is under $10,000 for Ukraine (higher than non-jet interceptors).

(U) The article notes that roughly 15–20% of recent Russian Shaheds have been jet-powered (per a Ukrainian air force commander cited earlier by Reuters). Keeping interceptor costs low while matching the higher speeds is repeatedly described as a major challenge. Reuters states it could not independently verify the battlefield performance claims made by the manufacturers.
