(U) The UK Ministry of Defence plans to scrap the replacement of its ageing Type 45 destroyers with Type 83 vessels and instead build at least six new Common Combat Vessels designed to deploy and coordinate drones across air, surface, and underwater domains for more resilient and cost-effective air defence. This approach, outlined in the long-delayed defence investment plan (DIP) due for publication before the NATO summit in early July, is intended to better address modern threats, including Russian activity in the North Atlantic, protection of critical underwater infrastructure, and broader NATO deterrence, while supporting British shipyards. The shift follows the resignations of former Defence Secretary John Healey and Armed Forces Minister Al Carns, who cited insufficient Treasury funding to close capability gaps, though incoming Defence Secretary Dan Jarvis has secured some additional resources short of the £28 billion previously sought. The government has committed to raising defence spending from the current 2.3% of GDP to 2.5% by 2027 and 3.5% by 2035, with the refreshed DIP approved by Andy Burnham, expected to succeed Keir Starmer as prime minister. The new vessels are slated for delivery in the 2030s as part of a wider naval modernisation including support ships and advanced drone technology.
