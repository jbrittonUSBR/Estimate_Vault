(U//FOUO) The FAA forecasts the number of registered commercial unmanned aircraft systems (UAS), aka "drones," in the U.S. will reach 955,000, while the number of registered recreational drones will surpass 1.8 million 2027. As a result, LEOs and other first responders in the JRIC six-county area of responsibility are likely to encounter drones on the job. (U) The FAA classifies drones based on their weight and use, imposing fewer regulations on drones used recreationally under 55 pounds (lbs) (aka, small UAS or "sUAS"), while the Department of Defense classifies by their altitude, speed, and weight. Manufacturers classify drones by wing type and vertical takeoff and landing capabilities (VTOL) which generally fall into four major categories-multirotor, rotary wing (RC helicopter), fixed wing (non-VTOL), and hybrid fixed wing (VTOL). The graphic below gives a general overview of the differing capabilities and features of these drone types. (U) UAS technology is evolving rapidly with manufacturers, hobbyists, and nefarious actors continuously innovating and customizing drones to achieve unique functions, applications, and aesthetics. However, the fundamental UAS platforms-multirotor, rotary wing (RC helicopter), fixed wing (non-VTOL), and hybrid fixed wing (VTOL)- remain the
same. Below are brief overviews of these platforms. 

(U) MULTI ROTOR DRONES I The most common drone type due to ease of use and maneuverability, small quadcopters dominate the hobbyist market, while larger ones have industrial and agricultural uses. Multirotor drones are further divided into categories based on the number and configuration of their rotors.

(U) An estimated 70 percent of drones used in the United States are manufactured by OJI, a China-based company specializing in multirotor drones. The company is best known for small quadcopters, including the Mavic drones used in the Russia-Ukraine conflict, but also produces agriculture drones with chemical sprayers. In January 2025, OJI removed a built-in geofence system designed to prevented drones from flying into prohibited airspace, replacing it with a warning notification on a control device. This update presents a serious enforcement challenge.

(U) ROTARY WING (RC HELICOPTER) DRONES: This uncommon drone type is used for aerial light/laser detection and ranging (LiDAR), agriculture, and search and rescue. Larger models are expensive and pose an increased risk of injury due to long rotator blades and the use of combustion engines on some models. 

{U) FIXED WING (NON-VTOL) DRONES: These drones are used for surveying, mapping, and military purposes. Fixed wing UAS require a launcher or a runway for takeoff and landing. Their simple design is low-maintenance, and the lack of VTOL motors makes them more energy-efficient and capable of longer flight times and higher payload capacity. 

(U) FIXED WING HYBRID VTOL DRONES: These drones are used for aerial mapping, surveillance, and long distance deliveries. They have the VTOL capability of rotor drones in addition to the range, altitude, and energy efficiency of fixed wing drones. While capable of hovering, they are typically unstable, with only few models capable of limited multidirectional flight.
