(U) Facing rising threats from Russia and reduced U.S. support, Germany is overhauling how it develops and buys weapons. After years of cautious, traditional procurement, the military is opening up to startups, faster innovation cycles, drones, autonomous systems, and AI.

(U) Key shifts include awarding nearly $1 billion in contracts for loitering munitions (kamikaze-style drones) to newer suppliers (some less than a decade old), investing in Ukrainian firms for long-range missiles, and partnering with tech startups that the German military previously avoided. Defense Minister Boris Pistorius has emphasized that large traditional arms programs alone are no longer sufficient. The piece frames this as part of Germany’s broader rearmament effort to adapt to 21st-century warfare.
