(U) **As Russia deploys jet-powered Shaheds, companies turn to faster interceptor drones** (Reuters, Cassell Bryan-Low, July 21, 2026)

Russia is increasingly using faster jet-propelled versions of its Shahed (Geran) attack drones—originally Iran-designed, low-cost, long-range systems—against Ukraine. These jet-powered variants can reach speeds of up to about 500 kph (311 mph), reducing detection and response time compared with propeller-driven models. Ukrainian air force commander Yuriy Cherevashenko previously estimated that roughly 15-20% of Shaheds launched by Russia use jet engines.

In response, several companies are developing or fielding higher-speed interceptor drones, with demonstrations and announcements tied to the Farnborough International Airshow.

### Key developments
- **SkyFall (Ukrainian manufacturer)**: Unveiled the P1-SUN Jetkiller, a faster version of its existing P1-SUN interceptor (deployed since late 2025). The new model reaches about 370 kph (vs. up to 310 kph for the original). A company spokesperson said it has been battlefield-tested, has downed more than a dozen jet-powered Shaheds, and that mass production is planned for August. The original model is claimed to have hit more than 5,500 Shaheds since November 2025, with production capacity cited at up to 50,000 units per month. Reuters could not independently verify the battlefield claims.
- **General Cherry (Ukrainian)**: Developing a faster version of its Bullet interceptor specifically for jet-powered Shaheds, targeting speeds above 400 kph (aiming for 500 kph). Power options under evaluation include electric and small turbojet. Front-line testing is planned for early August, with potential mass production in September. Cost is a priority; the company aims to stay near the existing Bullet’s price of about $2,000 or less. The current Bullet is claimed to have destroyed more than 1,000 regular Shaheds since last November.
- **Firebolt Engineering (British-Ukrainian)**: Has a jet-powered Griffen interceptor currently reaching up to about 350 kph. It recorded its first interception (of a non-jet Shahed) in May. Work is underway with British firm Dynamic Propulsion on a new engine to push speeds toward 400 kph. Cost remains a focus for disposable systems; the jet-powered version is priced under $10,000 for Ukraine (several times higher than non-jet interceptors), according to an investor.

The broader context at Farnborough includes other air-defense efforts against mass low-cost drone threats, such as MBDA’s interceptor for large numbers of munitions and Lockheed Martin’s lower-cost Patriot interceptor. Keeping interceptor costs low while matching the speed of jet-powered Shaheds is repeatedly highlighted as a central challenge.
