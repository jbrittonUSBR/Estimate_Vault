(U) Animal-rights organizations are lobbying the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to oppose proposed **"no-drone zones"** over agricultural facilities. While farmers argue that unauthorized drones harass workers and panic livestock, activists maintain that overhead surveillance is a vital form of public oversight used to expose alleged animal abuse.
### **Key Details**
- **The FAA Proposal:** Stemming from an executive order to protect critical infrastructure from surveillance and security threats, the FAA has classified agriculture as one of 16 critical infrastructure sectors. Under the proposed rules, agricultural producers could apply for permission to establish FAA-enforced no-drone airspaces.
- **Arguments from Farmers & Ag Groups:**
    - Agricultural producers state that low-flying, camera-equipped drones startle livestock (triggering dangerous stampedes, such as incidents reported by a California cattle company) and annoy workers.
    - Industry groups like the National Pork Producers Council and the North Carolina Poultry Federation argue that drones threaten biosecurity, risk crashing into sensitive farm equipment, and disrupt ventilation systems.
    - Some officials suggest expanding these protected zones to include grain elevators, feedlots, and meat-packing plants.
- **Arguments from Activists:**
    - Coalitions such as Animal Outlook, FarmSTAND, and We Animals have filed comments urging the FAA to remove agriculture from the critical infrastructure list entirely.
    - Activists frame their drone pilots as photojournalists, arguing that overhead footage is frequently the only way the public can witness conditions behind the walls of industrial farming facilities.
