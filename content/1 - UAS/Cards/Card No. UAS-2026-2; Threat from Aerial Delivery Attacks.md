(U) Threat from Aerial Delivery Attacks (U) In recent years, with the rapid growth in commercially available and affordable “off-the-shelf” unmanned aerial systems (UAS), or drones, threat actors across the ideological spectrum and around the world have adopted the technology for use in attacks against a variety of targets. In the US, accelerationist DVEs have encouraged and attempted the use of drones in attacks targeting critical infrastructure, including the individual who was arrested in November 2024 in a plot to destroy an energy facility in Nashville, TN. Aerial devices provide threat actors easier access to restricted and/or remote areas, allow for greater anonymity and operational security, and potentially allow for attacks to initially go undetected. Notably, DVEs in the US have been observing drone use by state actors in overseas conflicts, specifically in the ongoing war between Ukraine and Russia. In September 2024, footage emerged of the Ukrainian military using first-person view (FPV) drones to spray thermite over forested areas where Russian troops and equipment were suspected to be located, igniting large areas of forest in flames. Meanwhile, videos posted online by Russian troops utilizing FPV drones reveal a pattern in which an initial explosion will occur causing a fire, and once rescue and fire crews arrive on scene a second targeted strike is conducted. In October 2025, a Telegram channel announced plans to develop its “first military course,” in collaboration with the leader of international accelerationist neo-Nazi group, the Base. The training course, expected to be released in 2026, includes up-to-date guides on drone usage and the rules of engagement for “both sides of the Russian-Ukrainian conflict.” The accessibility of drone technology and the prevalence of rhetoric and guidance in extremist groups surrounding drone use, combined with observations by extremist actors of drone capabilities to conduct arson overseas highlights the potential for UAS to be used as a tool for arson induced wildfire in the homeland and presents an increasingly complex threat environment for law enforcement, first responders, and emergency managers.
