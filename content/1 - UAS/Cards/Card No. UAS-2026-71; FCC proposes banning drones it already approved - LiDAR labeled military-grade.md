(U) The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has advanced a proposal (Notice DA 26-758) to retroactively ban the import and sale of foreign-made drones equipped with **LiDAR sensors**, reclassifying the technology under a broad "military-grade" umbrella. The move targets popular consumer and professional models that the agency had previously cleared for U.S. sale.
### **Key Details**

- **The Scope of the Ban:** Beyond LiDAR (which manufacturers use primarily for civilian obstacle avoidance and navigation), the proposed reclassification sweeps in other features. It covers drones with thermal imaging, agricultural spray dispensers, docking stations, swarm coordination capabilities, and units weighing 55 pounds or more.
- **Impact on Popular Models:** If adopted, the rule would pull widely used foreign-made models—such as DJI's _Air 3S_, _Avata 360_, and _Mini 5 Pro_—off U.S. shelves.
- **Current Owners:** The proposal does not target existing owners; people who already own these drones can legally keep and fly them. However, future retail availability and long-term access to official replacement parts or repairs would be heavily restricted.
- **Industry & Manufacturer Pushback:** Major drone makers like DJI have criticized the action as a "total reversal" of prior FCC approvals and a category error, arguing that LiDAR is a standard safety feature rather than a weapon.
- **Timeline:** The public comment period for the proposal runs until **September 2, 2026**. If finalized, the sales and import ban would take effect roughly 180 days after publication.
