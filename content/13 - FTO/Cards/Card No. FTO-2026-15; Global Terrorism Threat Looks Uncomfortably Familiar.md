(U) **Global Terrorism Threat Looks Uncomfortably Familiar** (Jamestown Foundation / Substack, Sara Harmouch, July 21, 2026) Analyst Sara Harmouch argues that several warning indicators preceding earlier waves of external terrorist attacks against the West are reappearing across al-Qaeda and Islamic State networks, creating an environment that looks “uncomfortably familiar” even if it is not a direct replica of the pre-9/11 period.

**Key points**:
- In May 2026, al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP) released *Inspire* videos explicitly calling for lone-wolf attacks in the West and framing such operations as capable of bringing governments “to their knees.” This coincided with the U.S. Counterterrorism Strategy identifying al-Qaeda—and especially AQAP—as a primary external threat.
- Indicators again visible include: explicit calls for Western attacks, permissive sanctuaries, cross-theater facilitation, leadership guidance, rising arrests, and disrupted plots. Propaganda has grown more operationally specific, shifting from grievance to guidance and instruction (including bomb-making and attack methods).
- Senior leaders have returned to “far enemy” framing. Figures linked to al-Qaeda have referenced plans for large attacks, training, and preparation, with some rhetoric echoing pre-9/11 mobilization.
- Sanctuaries have expanded or hardened in Afghanistan (under Taliban protection for al-Qaeda elements; ongoing ISIS-K activity), the Sahel, Somalia, Nigeria, and other African theaters, giving groups space to recruit, fundraise, train, and plan.
- The current threat is more dispersed, technologically enabled, and networked than in the past. Fragmentation itself is part of how the threat is evolving, making isolated warning signs easier to dismiss even as they accumulate.

(U) Harmouch stresses that no single indicator predicts an imminent attack, but the convergence of rhetoric, capability, sanctuary, facilitation, and disrupted plots historically preceded major external operations. She warns against dismissing these fragmented signals.
