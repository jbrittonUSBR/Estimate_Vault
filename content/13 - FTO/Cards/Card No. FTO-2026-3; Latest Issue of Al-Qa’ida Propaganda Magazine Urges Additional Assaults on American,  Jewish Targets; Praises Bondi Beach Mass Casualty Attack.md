(U//LES) Al-Qa’ida in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP) continues to prioritize messaging content aimed at mobilizing homegrown violent extremists (HVEs) to commit acts of terrorism in the West through the publication of its Arabic- and English-language Inspire Guide propaganda magazine. The 12th and latest installment of this publication, released on 23 March, featured praise of the ISIS-inspired mass shooting targeting a Hanukkah celebration at Sydney’s Bondi Beach on 14 December 2025, alongside recycled guidance on how to create a command-detonated elbow pipe bomb with commercially-available fireworks powder. The use of English in this publication is an enduring concern as it increases the potential resonance of this broad tactical propaganda with HVEs. This issue of “Inspire Guide” also demonstrates AQAP’s continued willingness to exploit and praise attacks that it has no connectivity to, and to challenge its readers for being “coward(ly),” unlike those it featured in the guide.

- (U//LES) AQAP encouraged its readersto emulate two aspects of the Bondi Beach attacks in lone-offender assaults. First, it advised embarking on months of training and preparation and secondly, manufacturing improvised explosive devices (IEDs). The magazine claimed that the Bondi Beach mass shooting had the “benefit” of forcing Western governments to spend more money on the defense of Jewish sites, while raising fear in Jewish populations globally, and encouraging supporters to sacrifice more than “social media posts and marches.” AQAP noted that further attacks of this kind would impose greater pressure on the West and increase fear in Jewish communities.
- (U//LES) The AQAP magazine recycled improvised explosive device (IED)—specifically pipe bomb—manufacturing guidance from the “Open Source Jihad” section of Inspire Magazine, Issue #14, which was released by AQAP’s Al-Malahem Media in the summer of 2015. This reference, highlights the enduring nature of AQAP’s tactical instruction content more than a decade after its initial publication. The guide instructed potential attackers to construct bombs with commercially-available fireworks fuses and powder, along with household electronic switches, bolts, and elbow pipes. The magazine also instructed would-be assailants to test and experiment with the homemade bomb before using it in an attack. None of the bombs in the Bondi Beach assault ultimately detonated, which may explain AQAP’s focus on this tactic.
- (U//LES) AQAP seemingly criticized its followers who are uninvolved in attacks and praised the two ISIS Bondi Beach attackers for refusing to “sit back with their families and friends in safety.” The magazine instructed its readers “living in America or Europe” that the “least you can do today” is to emulate these ISIS attackers. AQAP’s direct call for supporters to commit acts of violence similar to the Bondi Beach assault follows a focus in the 11th issue of Inspire Guide—which highlighted the ISIS supporter responsible for a deadly attack during Yom Kippur in Manchester, UK—and likely represents an ongoing willingness by AQAP to identify ISIS-inspired attackers as examples to follow, potentially to increase its own relevance amid a relative lack of AQAP-inspired perpetrators.
