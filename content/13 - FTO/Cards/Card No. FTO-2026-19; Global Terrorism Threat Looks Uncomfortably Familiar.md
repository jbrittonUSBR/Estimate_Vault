(U) The piece argues that current indicators of jihadist external-attack intent resemble patterns that preceded earlier major attack cycles (pre-9/11, embassy bombings, USS Cole, etc.), even though the threat is more dispersed and technologically enabled today. Key points:

- In May 2026, al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP) released _Inspire_ videos explicitly calling for lone-wolf attacks in the West, coinciding with the new U.S. Counterterrorism Strategy identifying AQAP as a primary external threat.
- Warning signs are again accumulating: explicit calls for Western attacks, operational propaganda and guidance, leadership messaging prioritizing the “far enemy,” permissive sanctuaries (Afghanistan under the Taliban, parts of Africa), cross-theater facilitation, rising arrests, and disrupted plots.
- Propaganda volume and specificity have increased since Western withdrawals and the October 7, 2023 Hamas attacks. Senior al-Qaeda figures have renewed focus on striking the United States.
- Safe havens allow intent to mature into capability; today’s sanctuaries are more fragmented but still enable training, facilitation, and planning within a networked jihadist ecosystem.

()U The author cautions against dismissing these fragmented signals, noting that past catastrophic attacks were preceded by similar convergences of rhetoric, capability, and opportunity rather than a single definitive warning.
