# The Assessment Gap in U.S. Counter-Terrorism Strikes

[Source](https://warontherocks.com/the-assessment-gap-in-u-s-counter-terrorism-strikes/)

(U) WASHINGTON, United States — In a War on the Rocks commentary, Jim Loucks, a former programs advisor in the State Department's Bureau of Counterterrorism from 2018 to 2025, argued that U.S. counterterrorism assistance is subject to rigorous monitoring and evaluation while kinetic strikes are not. Loucks wrote that he oversaw about 20 projects in East Africa worth roughly $80 million a year, that programs were measured against pre-agreed indicators with funding withheld for poor performance, and that a defector program helped more than 1,200 people leave al-Shabaab between 2020 and 2024. He contended that across two combatant commands the United States has stopped publishing information on whom strikes kill and injure and that the mechanisms that once produced it lapsed within the past 18 months, leaving outside observers unable to judge lawfulness or effectiveness. The piece is expert commentary drawing on the author's own experience rather than a news report.
