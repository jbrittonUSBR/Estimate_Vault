(U) ASIO Director Mike Burgess has exposed a disturbing link between Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and Australian organised crime, revealing that the IRGC protected and supported exiled Melbourne gangster Kazem Hamad, who orchestrated the 2024 firebombing of the Adass Israel synagogue and monopolized Victoria’s illicit tobacco trade through extortion and violence. Hamad, recruited via Iraqi militias and deported in 2023 after serving time for heroin trafficking, continued criminal activities that aligned with Iranian interests until his subsequent imprisonment in Iraq for drug offenses. Analyst Michael Shoebridge noted that lucrative crime networks now provide the IRGC—a state sponsor of groups like Hamas and Hezbollah—with a ready-made infrastructure in Australia, marking a shift from traditional crime groups avoiding terrorism due to law enforcement risks. This development comes amid ASIO’s warnings of strained social cohesion, Middle East conflict fueling grievances amplified by social media, and the re-emergence of global terrorism threats from groups like Islamic State and al-Qa’ida, with the agency having foiled 31 major plots since 2014. The revelations underscore evolving hybrid threats where state actors exploit criminal enterprises for influence and terror operations on Australian soil.
