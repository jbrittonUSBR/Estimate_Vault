(U) DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin testified that Mexican cartels control the entirety of Mexico’s northern border through a network of “plazas” that oversee human smuggling and drug trafficking, describing it as a major national security threat with no inch of the border free from cartel influence. He highlighted the effectiveness of border walls in creating choke points that allow Border Patrol agents to better anticipate and respond to illegal crossings. The Trump administration is advancing construction of the remaining 698 miles of primary border wall along the southwest border and installing buoy barriers in the Rio Grande, with plans to complete 536 miles in the Rio Grande Valley sector. On the same day, the Supreme Court upheld the administration’s policy of turning back asylum seekers at ports of entry before they reach U.S. soil, prompting White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller to declare that “America’s doors are closed fully to asylum seekers” and to criticize payments to cartels—now designated as foreign terrorist organizations—while urging potential migrants to apply for asylum in other countries with which the U.S. has agreements. The statements reflect the administration’s hardline approach to border security and immigration enforcement.
