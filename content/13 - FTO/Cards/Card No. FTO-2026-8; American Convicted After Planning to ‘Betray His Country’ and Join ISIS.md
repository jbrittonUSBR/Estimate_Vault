(U) Alexander Justin White, a 30-year-old man from Durham, North Carolina, was sentenced to eight years in federal prison after being convicted of conspiring to provide and attempting to provide material support to ISIS. Authorities arrested him at an airport as he attempted to board a flight to Morocco to join the terrorist group and engage in fighting, following months of online activity in which he promoted ISIS ideology, shared jihadist videos, encouraged recruitment, and sought to transfer funds for terrorist purposes between May and October 2024. White had expressed his intent to become a “mujahid” and indicated a willingness to commit acts of violence, including against U.S. military personnel and civilians. The case, investigated by the FBI’s Raleigh-based Joint Terrorism Task Force, underscores ongoing concerns about online radicalization leading individuals to support or join designated terrorist organizations like ISIS, which—despite losing most of its territorial caliphate—continues to inspire attacks through propaganda, including recent calls for lone wolf operations targeting the 2026 World Cup in North America. Prosecutors and FBI officials emphasized that the U.S. will not tolerate such support, whether from “keyboard warriors” or those attempting to travel abroad to fight.
