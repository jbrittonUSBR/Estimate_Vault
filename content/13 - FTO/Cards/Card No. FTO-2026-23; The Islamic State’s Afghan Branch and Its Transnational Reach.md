(U) The commentary examines how the **Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP)** has evolved from a localized Afghan splinter group into a highly resilient, decentralized transnational threat that no longer relies on holding fixed territory to project global violence.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Shift to Decentralization:** Although targeted counterterrorism operations by the Taliban have shrunk ISKP's physical strongholds within Afghanistan, the group has adapted by abandoning rigid hierarchies. Instead of massive standing armies or border infiltrations, it relies on autonomous, localized terror cells worldwide.
- **Transnational Operations and Reach:** ISKP has demonstrated its capacity for high-impact external attacks far beyond South Asia—exemplified by major strikes such as the Crocus City Hall massacre in Moscow and the deadly bombing at a memorial for Qassem Soleimani in Iran.
- **Digital Playbook and Modern Tech:** The group leverages digital platforms for cross-border recruitment, pulling in diverse ethnicities. Notably, its propaganda has incorporated modern tools, even framing technological and AI literacy as a religious imperative for its followers. It also relies heavily on decentralized financial mechanisms like cryptocurrency to fund operations.
- **The Taliban Dilemma:** While several regional states engage the Taliban pragmatically without granting formal diplomatic recognition, the Taliban regime remains incapable of completely eradicating ISKP from Afghan soil. This persistent security failure strains international relations and complicates broader regional counterterrorism efforts.
