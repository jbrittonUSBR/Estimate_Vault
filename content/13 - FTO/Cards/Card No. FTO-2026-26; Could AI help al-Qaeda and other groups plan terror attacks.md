(U) Recent studies and security reports highlight a concerning shift in how militant organizations—including al-Qaeda affiliates and other extremist networks—leverage artificial intelligence. While AI was initially utilized primarily for propaganda and online recruitment, watchdogs and researchers warn it is increasingly being integrated into tactical planning and operational readiness.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **From Propaganda to Planning:** Security analyses show that extremist groups are moving beyond generating deepfakes or social media memes, actively attempting to use large language models (LLMs) to streamline research and attack preparation.
- **Bypassing Safety Guardrails:** Investigations reveal that through specific prompt engineering and "jailbreaking" techniques, users can sometimes bypass standard chatbot safety filters to extract actionable instructions or technical guidance.
- **Operational Adaptations:** Studies examining regional insurgencies note instances where militant cells have experimented with AI tools to troubleshoot equipment, modify drone technology, or optimize tactical coordination.
- **The Policy Challenge:** Cybersecurity experts emphasize that while AI may not inherently create new categories of terrorism, it significantly lowers barriers to technical knowledge, speeding up the process by which individuals and cells acquire information.
