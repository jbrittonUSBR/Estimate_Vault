(U) Core claims of the article

- A distinct “Palestinian” national identity and culture are relatively modern (roughly a century old or less), not ancient.
- The designation “Palestinian” for the Arab population of the area largely stems from the British Mandate after World War I.
- He cites historians and figures (including Daniel Pipes dating a key shift to around 1920, and even Rashid Khalidi acknowledging that a strong Palestinian national identity was novel and limited before WWI).
- He references a 1958 Mike Wallace interview with Abba Eban in which neither used the word “Palestinian,” instead referring to “Arab refugees.”
- He quotes statements attributed to figures such as Gamal Abdel Nasser (1959) pushing a “Palestinian entity” and Zuheir Mohsen (PLO/As-Saiqa, 1977) saying the Palestinian people were created for tactical reasons against Israel.
- The article’s central thesis is that Palestinian identity/culture was shaped largely in opposition to Zionism/Israel and is uniquely defined by the political goal of eliminating Israel, unlike broader Arab, Jordanian, or Syrian identities.

(U) Context: This is an advocacy/analysis piece from IPT (an organization focused on Islamist terrorism and related issues). It presents a particular interpretive view of the historical emergence of Palestinian nationalism. Mainstream academic historians generally agree that modern Palestinian national consciousness solidified in the 20th century (especially after the British Mandate, 1948, and 1967), though there is ongoing debate about the depth of earlier regional identities, local attachments, and continuity.
