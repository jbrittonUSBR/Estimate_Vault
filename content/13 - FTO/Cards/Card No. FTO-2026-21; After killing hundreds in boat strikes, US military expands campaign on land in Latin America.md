(U) Following a series of lethal maritime operations that have killed hundreds of people aboard alleged drug-smuggling vessels in the Caribbean Sea and eastern Pacific Ocean, the U.S. Defense Department has announced an expansion of the military campaign onto land across Latin America.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **Escalating Maritime Toll:** The cumulative death toll from the Trump administration's controversial campaign of bombing suspected cartel boats has surpassed 210 fatalities across more than 60 strikes.
- **The Shift to Land Operations:** Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced that the U.S. military offensive is expanding inland. During a visit to Panama, Hegseth stated that regional partners had agreed to permit joint U.S. military operations against criminal cartels directly on their soil.
- **Regional Friction and Pushback:** While the Pentagon has framed the campaign as a necessary kinetic effort to impose "total systemic friction on narco-terrorists," the announcement has sparked diplomatic friction. For instance, following the U.S. assertions, officials from Guatemala publicly denied that an agreement had been reached allowing U.S. troops to conduct operations within its borders.
- **Legal and Strategic Questions:** International law experts and human rights advocates continue to question the legality, oversight, and rules of engagement governing these lethal military strikes against suspects in international waters and foreign sovereign territory.
