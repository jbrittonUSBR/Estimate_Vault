(U) German authorities are investigating the March 2022 sale and subsequent liquidation of Gazprom Germania, the Berlin-based subsidiary of Russia’s state-owned gas giant Gazprom, as a suspected attempt to sabotage Germany’s energy security shortly after Moscow’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Prosecutors say the company—which at the time controlled at least 25% of Germany’s natural gas storage capacity—was transferred through indirect share sales to a Moscow-based entity with no apparent ties to the energy industry, which then moved to liquidate it. Raids were carried out this week in Berlin and Frankfurt targeting a Russian suspect and a Frankfurt business as part of the probe, though no arrests have been made. The case comes amid Germany’s broader efforts to reduce its heavy pre-war dependence on Russian gas supplies, a shift former Chancellor Olaf Scholz and EU officials have described as essential to avoiding future strategic vulnerabilities, with the bloc aiming to phase out Russian gas imports by the end of 2027.
