(U) AT&T has asked the FCC for permission to discontinue service on sections of its aging copper network damaged by vandalism, theft, and equipment failures, citing over 500 outages in May 2026 alone and arguing that repairs are uneconomic and unnecessary given available alternatives. In a 500-page filing, the company stated it does not intend to restore the affected lines, as it plans to retire most of its legacy copper infrastructure and comparable substitute services exist for impacted customers, many of whom are in California where copper theft has caused thousands of outages in recent years. The request is part of AT&T’s broader effort to shed carrier-of-last-resort obligations and challenge state rules requiring continued copper service for new customers, though stakeholders have pushed back, noting that landlines remain a critical lifeline in areas with limited mobile coverage. While some regions like Louisville, Kentucky, have reduced copper theft through law enforcement partnerships, AT&T maintains that continuing to maintain damaged portions of the network does not serve the public interest amid its transition to newer technologies.
