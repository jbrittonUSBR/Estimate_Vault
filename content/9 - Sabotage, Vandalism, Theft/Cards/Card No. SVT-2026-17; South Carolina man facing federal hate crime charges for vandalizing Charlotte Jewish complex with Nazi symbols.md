(U) CHARLOTTE, N.C. — A South Carolina man faces federal hate crime charges for [allegedly vandalizing multiple buildings](https://www.wcnc.com/crime) at a Charlotte Jewish community complex with Nazi symbols and threatening imagery. Dalton Ray Mullis, 24, of Indian Land, was arrested Thursday and appeared in federal court Friday. Prosecutors allege Mullis traveled to the Foundation of Shalom Park complex on the evening of Jan. 19 and defaced several buildings with antisemitic flyers. The complex houses the Jewish Community Center, Charlotte Jewish Day School, Holocaust Memorial and other facilities used for Jewish religious, cultural and educational programs. According to the criminal complaint, surveillance cameras captured Mullis spraying what appears to be adhesive on an entrance to the Jewish day school and placing a flyer on the door. The flyers allegedly displayed a noose, swastika and Totenkopf — a "death's head" symbol associated with the Nazi SS. Prosecutors also allege Mullis posted antisemitic messages and imagery on social media accounts between January and April. "Posting Nazi and lynching symbols on the Jewish Community Center is pure hate and it's disgusting," U.S. Attorney Russ Ferguson said. "This is America, which was founded on the free exercise of religion and people ought to be free to worship without being threatened." The Jewish Federation of Greater Charlotte shared a message about the incident. "For many in our community, the presence of antisemitic symbols on a campus that serves Jewish children, families, and organizations was deeply painful and disturbing," the letter said, in part. "While the materials were removed quickly, their impact on many members of our community lasted far longer." FBI Charlotte Special Agent in Charge Reid Davis said the alleged acts "undermine public safety and violate federal law," adding that the FBI will work to ensure all community members can "gather, learn, and worship" without fear. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Police Chief Estella Patterson emphasized that acts targeting houses of worship "have no place in Charlotte." Mullis remains in federal custody and faces up to 20 years in prison if convicted.
