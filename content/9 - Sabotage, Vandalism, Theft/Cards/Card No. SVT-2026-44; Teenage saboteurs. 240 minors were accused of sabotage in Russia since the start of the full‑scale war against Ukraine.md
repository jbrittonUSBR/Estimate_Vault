(U) Since the start of Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, at least 240 minors have been detained on sabotage or terrorism charges related to arson attacks, according to Mediazona’s count. Most cases involve teenagers seeking easy side jobs online (via Telegram channels and forums for “sportiks”) who respond to anonymous offers to set fires—often framed as insurance fraud or low-risk paid tasks. Targets commonly include railway relay cabinets, locomotives, transformers, cell towers, and occasionally military equipment. Clients (frequently attributed by Russian authorities to Ukrainian intelligence) usually demand video proof with specific hand gestures. In the vast majority of cases (205), the motive was financial; a few cited opposition to the war or influence from scammers. Groups often form among friends, and some adults have recruited or coerced minors. Sentences for those convicted can be severe (up to nine years in a penal colony for 15-year-olds). Russian authorities have responded with warnings, public messaging, and hard prosecutions, sometimes adding treason charges.
