(U) Two major undersea fiber-optic communication cables off the coast of Perth, Australia, suffered "shunt faults" in close succession within a federally protected maritime zone, prompting an urgent investigation by the Australian Federal Police over potential sabotage.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Affected Infrastructure:** The damage targeted two critical systems—**Indigo-West** (connecting Perth to Indonesia and Singapore) and **Indigo-Central** (linking Perth to eastern Australia). Together, they form a vital part of the limited infrastructure that carries the vast majority of Australia's international internet traffic.
- **Suspicious Timing and Location:** Subco founder and CEO Bevan Slattery raised the alarm after the two faults occurred almost simultaneously and in tight geographic proximity to one another. Furthermore, the incident took place inside the federally declared **Perth Submarine Cable Protection Zone**, matching the tracks of suspicious vessel activity detected nearby.
- **What is a Shunt Fault?** A shunt fault occurs when a cable's outer insulation is breached, allowing seawater to contact the internal power conductor (which supplies electricity to the optical signal amplifiers). This causes a short circuit and destabilizes the power feed, even if the fiber threads themselves aren't instantly severed.
- **Broader Security Concerns:** While accidental damage from ship anchors or fishing gear is common globally, defense experts and officials noted that the suspicious circumstances heighten fears regarding gray-zone warfare and the vulnerability of critical subsea assets to state-sponsored sabotage. The Australian Federal Police confirmed they received a formal crime report and are actively assessing the case.
