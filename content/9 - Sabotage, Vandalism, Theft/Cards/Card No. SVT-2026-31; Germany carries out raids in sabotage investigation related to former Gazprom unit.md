(U) German authorities carried out raids in Berlin and Frankfurt on Wednesday as part of a criminal investigation into a suspected attempt to sabotage the country’s gas supply through the opaque 2022 sale and liquidation of Gazprom Germania, the Berlin-based subsidiary of Russia’s state energy giant. Prosecutors say the company—which controlled a significant portion of Germany’s natural gas storage—was transferred via indirect share sales to a Moscow-based entity with no ties to the industry shortly after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine, after which the new owner moved to liquidate it in violation of investment rules. A Russian national is under investigation as an accessory to both violating foreign trade investment regulations and attempted anticonstitutional sabotage for allegedly supporting the liquidation with the aim of disrupting Germany’s energy security, though no arrests have been made. The probe comes amid Germany’s efforts to reduce its pre-war dependence on Russian gas supplies and follows the mysterious explosions that damaged the Nord Stream pipelines weeks after Russia cut off remaining deliveries.
