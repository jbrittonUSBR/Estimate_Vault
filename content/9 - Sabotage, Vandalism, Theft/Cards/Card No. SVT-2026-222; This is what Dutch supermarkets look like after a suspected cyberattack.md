# This is what Dutch supermarkets look like after a suspected cyberattack.

[Source](https://freerepublic.com/focus/f-chat/4399034/posts)

(U) AMSTERDAM, Netherlands — A post shared on Free Republic on October 13, 2026, showed Dutch supermarket shelves left largely empty following a suspected cyberattack, with the comment that hackers had earlier hit cooling systems in French supermarkets and that "this is just the beginning." The underlying incident involved a cyberattack disclosed by Ahold Delhaize, parent of the Albert Heijn chain, which took IT systems offline after detecting suspicious activity on October 12, halting home deliveries and disrupting supplies to stores. Dutch security-sector sources told broadcaster NOS that attack traffic was traced through servers in the United States, Poland, Moldova and Turkey, and that systems were switched off as the traffic came in. Albert Heijn began a "controlled restart" of some systems and restocking two days later, while fresh produce shelves, online ordering and customer service remained affected; the company said it found no indication customer data had been stolen.
