(U) A powerful grassroots movement against automated license plate reader (ALPR) technology is sweeping across the United States, forcing local governments to cancel surveillance contracts, vote down new camera installations, and grapple with a surge of public protest against mass monitoring.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Scale of the Backlash:** Citizen-led resistance—fueled by privacy concerns, potential civil liberties violations, and misuse by law enforcement—has led dozens of municipalities to pause, defund, or completely cancel their contracts with Flock Safety.
- **Bipartisan Opposition:** The pushback has brought together an unusual coalition of political forces. Civil liberties groups (like the ACLU), progressive privacy advocates, and libertarian or conservative lawmakers are increasingly aligning to challenge the rapid expansion of municipal surveillance networks.
- **Civic Discontent and Vandalism:** Frustrated by slow responses from local officials, some citizens have turned to direct action. Across the country, cameras have been physically vandalized, painted over, or sawed down, while online mapping projects like _DeFlock_ track camera locations to empower local resistance.
- **The Industry Dilemma:** In response to the growing public relations and political crisis, Flock Safety has attempted to push back with PR campaigns and software updates aimed at reassuring the public regarding data privacy, retention limits, and restricted police searches. However, critics argue that the core model—collecting and storing billions of location data points on everyday citizens—remains an inherent threat to constitutional rights.
