(U) Seattle has increased reimbursement limits under its “Back to Business” program to help small businesses recover from vandalism and break-ins, raising the Storefront Repair Fund cap from $3,000 to $5,000 per incident and the Security Fund from $6,000 to $10,000, after disbursing over $1 million to affected businesses citywide—including $175,000 to 38 businesses in the Chinatown-International District. Despite a reported 15% drop in property crimes since 2022, many owners say the support remains insufficient amid repeated incidents, high repair costs driven by inflation and tariffs, and limited police response, with some businesses like a Belltown bar suffering six break-ins and $150,000 in losses or a Ballard shop losing tens of thousands in uninsured stolen goods. Program administrators have approved hundreds of claims for repairs and security upgrades, but owners express frustration over underreported crimes, ineligible damages, and a sense of being “thrown to the wolves,” calling for broader coverage, stronger law enforcement, and more comprehensive relief as they struggle to stay afloat. While the city frames the increased payouts as responsive to ongoing challenges, business leaders question whether the aid adequately addresses the scale of the problem in neighborhoods hit hardest by property crime.
