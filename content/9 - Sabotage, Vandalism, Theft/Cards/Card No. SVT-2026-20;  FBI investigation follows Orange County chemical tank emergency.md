(U) The incident began on 22 May 2026, when a 34,000-gallon storage tank containing methyl methacrylate (MMA) overheated and began venting vapors at the company's Garden Grove site, triggering a major hazardous materials response. Emergency services established evacuation zones covering parts of Garden Grove and neighboring communities, with around 50,000 residents affected by precautionary orders and warnings as specialist teams worked to manage the developing situation. Continuous cooling operations, atmospheric monitoring and exclusion measures were implemented to reduce the risk of tank rupture or thermal explosion. During the response, a crack developed in the tank wall, which officials said relieved internal pressure and contributed to a reduction in the vessel's temperature. By 25 May, authorities confirmed that the immediate risk of a catastrophic explosion had been significantly reduced, allowing evacuation orders to be lifted in stages while a reduced exclusion zone and monitoring arrangements remained in place. The incident has since become the subject of both federal and local criminal investigations. On 10 June, the FBI confirmed that agents had executed a search warrant at the facility as part of an inquiry into the circumstances surrounding the emergency. Court documents cited by US media indicated that investigators were seeking records relating to the storage, handling and disposal of MMA, together with samples of materials held on site and documentation associated with cooling systems used to regulate the chemical's temperature. The Orange County District Attorney's Office is also conducting an investigation. No criminal charges have been announced and no findings have been published regarding the cause of the incident. At a community meeting held following the emergency, a representative of GKN Aerospace apologized to residents for the disruption caused by the evacuation and said the company would continue to cooperate fully with the authorities. The company has not publicly commented on the circumstances that led to the tank overheating. Authorities have stated that environmental monitoring has not identified contamination affecting the wider community. Plans to remove the remaining methyl methacrylate from the site have been delayed, with Orange County officials stating that a revised timetable for the operation would be communicated to residents in advance. Investigations by the FBI and local authorities remain ongoing.
