(U) A U.S. judge ordered the release of Melissa Farris, the Kentucky woman facing federal felony charges for allegedly vandalizing the World War II Memorial in Washington, D.C.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Release and Conditions:** According to court records, Farris was ordered released under strict pretrial conditions, which include surrendering her passport and remaining within the continental U.S. unless explicitly approved by the court.
- **Prosecutorial Pushback:** U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro and the Department of Justice strongly criticized the release, arguing that Farris poses a significant flight risk and urging the court to reconsider the decision.
- **Background on the Charges:** Farris, 41, was arrested following an incident where the World War II Memorial was defaced with spray paint (including the phrase “Clean hands Dirty $”) and bubbly soap poured into its fountains. She faces two felony counts: depredation against property of the United States and destruction of veterans' memorials.
- **Motive and Next Steps:** Farris broadcasted portions of the action on social media, stating she intended to protest corporate corruption and the misuse of U.S. tax dollars. A preliminary hearing in the case is scheduled for September 3.
