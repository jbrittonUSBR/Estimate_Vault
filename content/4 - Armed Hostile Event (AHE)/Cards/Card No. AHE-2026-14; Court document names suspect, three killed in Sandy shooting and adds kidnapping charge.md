(U) SANDY, Ore. (KATU) — Court documents revealed the names of the victims involved in a shooting that took place in Sandy on Sunday night. A deadly domestic disturbance in Sandy unfolded over several hours Sunday evening, leaving multiple people dead and a police officer shot, authorities said. Sandy police and other adjacent agencies responded about 4 p.m. Sunday to reports of a domestic disturbance and a shooting in the town. Authorities warned the area was not safe and urged people to stay away. Residents nearby were told to lock their doors and remain inside as officers continued working the scene. Around 9:30 p.m., police said during a press conference that several people had died and that an officer had been shot. Authorities did not provide the number of victims or release any names at that time. Sandy Police Chief Patrick Huskey said officers and Clackamas County deputies responded to reports on Evans Street near Ross Avenue and came under fire once they arrived. Huskey said officers returned fire and an officer was shot. Medical crews later transported the officer via LifeFlight to a hospital with “multiple” gunshot wounds. Huskey, who did not take questions at the Sunday night press conference, said multiple people died. Huskey, who did not take questions at the Sunday night press conference, said multiple people died. During a confrontation with the suspect, officers exchanged gunfire. The officer-involved shooting is being investigated by the Clackamas Interagency Major Crimes Team. On Monday, June 1, an indictment document identified the suspect as Bryan Andrew Moore, born in 1987. The document states Moore is charged with killing 37-year-old Jenna Mary Overson, 70-year-old Mary Beth Overson and 16-year-old Kobyn McClure. The indictment also states Moore injured Sandy Police Department Sergeant Garrett Thornton, and is charged with aggravated murder involving him.

(U) Moore was also charged with kidnapping two separate people for the purposes of holding them as a shield or hostage, and with being a felon in possession of a firearm. The relationship between the victims remains unclear at this time. A fundraiser was started online for one of the victims, [Jenna Overson](https://gofund.me/059a5203c), stating its meant to help three children left behind. KATU News reporter Jennifer Singh was on the scene Sunday evening and spoke to some community members waiting for the scene to wrap up. A man told KATU he was waiting for his daughter who was inside the apartment complex near the shooting took place. "I got a call that said my daughter was up here in the middle of an active shooting, and that's really all I knew," Robert Zeppetella said. When asked what was going through his mind as a parent, Zeppetella said he was "super scared." "I got in the truck and I wanted to dome down here and try to bust her out," the Estacada father said. "That's what my first thought was." As for what he was hoping for at the time, Zeppetella said, "I seen two SWAT trucks pull in there, and I'm hoping that they bust down the door and neutralize the guy and everybody's safe and he gets taken care of, and nobody gets hurt." Another person around the scene at the time was Ray Savig, a Sandy resident who described it as a "quiet town." "Seeing something like this or hearing about something like this is not normal, per se. We don't have a lot of violence, I guess, and that's why I moved from Portland to here, so to speak," Savig said. "So yeah, this is very abnormal." Savig felt unease seeing all the ambulances on the scene near his home. "By our house there's a Life Flight helicopter in the field, so that says a lot right there. Seeing all the ambulances, all the different agencies responding is a sure sign of something not good going on."

(U) He said he felt "something tragic had happened" and that "people are hurt," when he saw the response at the scene. One neighbor said she arrived as SWAT crews were speeding in. "My sister drove me home from my mother's house. We came the back road or down Evans the back way. And we over by the apartments over there we saw civil officers and a officer outside. We went by and really slow, and then he started yelling at us to, 'Go! Go! Go! Go!' and then I looked around there was a bunch of policemen with long guns and pistols and everything. Then I hurried up and got in the house, and then about five minutes later this whole street was covered with cops, SWAT, you name it was here." Adriana Lara, a family counselor, created a memorial space at Meinig Memorial Park just a few blocks from the scene. She said she wants to give people in the community a place to be together and grieve. "I woke up, and I saw the news, and I know our town or community were a very tight community -- Christmas and big events, were all together," she said. "So I know that everybody's hurting, and they need a safe place. And I know that things take time, so I just decided to call the city. The city was so nice that right away they said, 'Hey, you know what? The gazebo is available. Go ahead and use it.' And I just went and bought little things and just made this." Sandy Mayor Kathleen Walker [expressed condolences in a social media post](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/17RYKphjob/) “Tonight, our Sandy community grieves the unimaginable loss of lives from a domestic violence incident,” the post read in part. “Our small town is not shielded by distance or size.” Walker also urged the community to allow investigators to do their work and to support those affected.
