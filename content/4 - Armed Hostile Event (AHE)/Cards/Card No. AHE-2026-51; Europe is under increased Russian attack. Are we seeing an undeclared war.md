# Europe is under increased Russian attack. Are we seeing an undeclared war?

[Source](https://theconversation.com/europe-is-under-increased-russian-attack-are-we-seeing-an-undeclared-war-290729)

(U) CANBERRA, Australia — An academic analysis published September 4, 2026 argued that escalating Russian drone and sabotage attacks on European countries amounted to an undeclared war against the West. The author, University of Canberra law and security professor Sascha-Dominik Bachmann, cited a U.S. Congressional Research Service report finding that Russian hybrid attacks in Europe quadrupled from 2023 to 2024, with at least 151 reported incidents from February 2022 to March 2026. The article referenced Russian drone attacks on Germany's Leipzig Airport and months of drone incursions into Romanian airspace, including a May 2026 drone strike on an apartment building that injured two people. It noted that both Germany and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen had publicly blamed Russia for the escalating campaign against European Union critical infrastructure.

Russia’s full scale war of aggression against Ukraine has entered its fifth year. The war is stuck in a violent stalemate characterised by an [escalating exchange of drone and missile attacks](https://www.intellinews.com/kyiv-blog-the-ukraine-war-stuck-in-a-violent-stalemate-450572/) that target critical infrastructure and civilians in Ukraine.

The conflict is also spilling out beyond the borders of the those two countries and into continental Europe more broadly in unconventional and indirect ways.

Russia has been attacking European countries using what’s called “[hybrid warfare](https://theconversation.com/explainer-what-is-hybrid-warfare-and-what-is-meant-by-the-grey-zone-11884)”: the use of unconventional methods as part of a multi-domain warfighting approach. These methods aim to disrupt and disable an opponent’s actions without engaging in open hostilities.

Just last month, Russia [attempted drone attacks](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/25/germany-drone-suspected-military-explosives-found-near-leipzig-airport) on Germany’s Leipzig Airport.

The escalating, intensifying attacks on countries such as Germany and Romania amount to an undeclared war against the West, with the potential for escalation.

Since the beginning of the invasion in February 2022, Russia has been attacking NATO’s European member states [with a mix of tactics](https://smallwarsjournal.com/2023/07/17/how-russias-hybrid-warfare-changing/). These have included sabotage, [assassination attempts](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/08/14/poland-says-it-thwarted-russian-assassination-attempt-us-citizen-warsaw/) and cyber attacks on energy grids and communication infrastructure. It has also launched [physical attacks](https://www.politico.eu/article/russia-sabotage-undersea-cables-baltic-sea-europe-war/) on NATO’s critical undersea cable infrastructure.

These attacks were taking place below the threshold of conventional open warfare in the so-called “[grey zone of conflict](https://theconversation.com/explainer-what-is-hybrid-warfare-and-what-is-meant-by-the-grey-zone-118841)”. They were unconventional, non-attributable or falling short of physical conflict.

An example of this type of conflict is the [Nord Stream gas pipeline sabotage in September 2022](https://theconversation.com/hybrid-warfare-nord-stream-attacks-show-how-war-is-evolving-191764). This was a clandestine operation that damaged two main natural gas pipelines in the Baltic Sea and effectively put a stop to any Russian-Germany liquid gas energy business. [German prosecutors](https://www.dw.com/en/nord-stream-blast-ordered-by-ukraine-say-german-prosecutors/a-77798230) blame Ukraine, but there are [conflicting views](https://www.globalresearch.ca/who-blew-up-nord-stream-pipelines/5898792) on who was behind it.

Russia and Belarus were accused of [weaponising migrants](https://theconversation.com/is-the-belarus-migrant-crisis-a-new-type-of-war-a-conflict-expert-explains-171739) in 2021 by opening up to the Middle East, then funnelling migrants to the borders with European Union countries. The strategy aimed to destabilise their neighbours and [killed dozens](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/oct/02/beatings-dog-bites-and-barbed-wire-life-and-death-on-the-poland-belarus-border) of migrants.

Russia has stepped up hybrid warfare operations against Europe since then. According to [a recent report](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R49134) by the United States Congressional Research Service:

> the number of Russian hybrid attacks in Europe quadrupled from 2023 to 2024; another investigation compiled at least 151 incidents of reported Russian operations from February 2022 to March 2026.

Whether it’s [arson attacks](https://lansinginstitute.org/2026/08/28/from-skyeton-to-rheinmetall-a-new-stage-in-russias-sabotage-war-in-europe/) against Western arms manufacturing sites or coordinated GPS jamming of Western navigation systems [targeting civilian navigation and aviation](https://tvpworld.com/95036347/russian-gps-interference-disrupts-signal-in-polands-tricity), Russia is stepping up its operations. All of them have the potential to create a civilian mass casualty event.

These operations still fall under the umbrella of hybrid warfare, as both deniability and lack of attribution are still key elements of Russia’s approach. But they are starting to test Western resilience.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, when addressing the Leipzig Airport incident, [warned](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cddv71p4j2jo) the attackers would have to “pay a price” and that Germany was increasingly becoming a target of such attacks. Germany’s government has since [officially blamed Russia](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-02/leipzig-drone-russia/107105194).

Romania has also come under Russian attack.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen reacted to reports of the Romanian military destroying a naval drone near one of its oil platforms in the Black Sea by calling out an “[escalating campaign of threats](https://www.theguardian.com/world/live/2026/aug/20/ukraine-volodymyr-zelenskyy-russia-vladimir-putin-kyiv-europe-live-latest-updates)” against the European Union and its critical infrastructure.

This recent incident follows [months of Russian drone incursions](https://abcnews.com/International/russia-drone-incursions-nato-airspace-jump-exponentially-2026/story?id=135771388) into Romanian airspace, as well as increased drone strikes close to Romania’s border.

The action has affected civilians. In May 2026, a Russian drone [hit an apartment building](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c93x4nxlkjeo) in Romania and injured two people.

These incidents demonstrate Russia’s increasing willingness to openly use military force.

## The NATO response

NATO has ramped up its air policing along its Eastern border to secure the airspace. As part of its commitment to its collective security mandate (also called Article 5), where an attack against one member is treated as an attack against all members, it has also provided more air defence capabilities to affected border states.

These actions are in addition to existing NATO counters to Russian clandestine operations of sabotage and other hybrid warfare tactics. NATO’s operations [Baltic Sentry](https://shape.nato.int/eva/baltic-sentry), [Eastern Sentry](https://shape.nato.int/eva/eastern-sentry) and [Arctic Sentry](https://shape.nato.int/eva/eastern-sentry) enhance the organisation’s capability to counter Russian threats to NATO interests on land, air and sea.

It seems clear the nature of Russia’s undeclared war against the West for its support of Ukraine has progressed beyond its original hybrid warfare form.
