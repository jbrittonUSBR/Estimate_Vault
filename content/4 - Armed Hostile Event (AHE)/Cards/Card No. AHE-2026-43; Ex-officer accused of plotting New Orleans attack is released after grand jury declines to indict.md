(U) Christopher Gillum, a former North Carolina police officer accused of plotting a mass shooting targeting Black people at a New Orleans festival around the time of Jazz Fest, was quietly released from custody after Louisiana prosecutors declined to pursue state terrorizing charges.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Arrest:** Gillum was initially arrested in Destin, Florida, after family members alerted authorities to troubling threats he made regarding going to New Orleans to "kill a Black person". Deputies recovered a handgun and approximately 200 rounds of ammunition from his hotel room.
- **Grand Jury & Release:** A Louisiana grand jury declined to indict Gillum. He was released from custody after state charges were refused, and his attorney confirmed that he is not facing charges in any other jurisdiction.
- **Jurisdictional Challenges:** According to Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill, complex jurisdictional issues contributed to the case's dismissal. The threats occurred in North Carolina, and Gillum was intercepted in Florida, meaning he never physically entered Louisiana prior to his arrest and extradition.
- **Inaccurate Target Claims & Outcome:** Gillum's defense attorney stated that initial police claims regarding a specific threat to the New Orleans Jazz & Heritage Festival were inaccurate. Following his release, Gillum's family moved him to an out-of-state facility for medical treatment.
