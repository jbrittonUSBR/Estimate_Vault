(U) The Hancock County Sheriff’s Department conducted a four-day active shooter training session for its entire staff, including courthouse and jail personnel, at the vacated New Palestine Junior High School building in Indiana ahead of its demolition this fall. The drills, held the week of June 27, 2026, involved realistic scenarios such as clearing classrooms, confronting an active shooter, applying tourniquets and packing wounds to stop bleeding, making rapid assessments, and creating a safety corridor for other first responders. Instructors emphasized hands-on practice as more valuable than range training, with tactics evolving since the 1999 Columbine incident and incorporating input from national leaders. Maj. Bobby Campbell highlighted long-standing cooperative agreements with local school corporations to use facilities during non-use periods, while Sgt. Nick Ernstes noted recent training updates and plans to extend the program countywide in coordination with police, fire, EMS, and communications personnel.
