(U) **Father, son charged with kidnapping 2 U.S. Forest Service biologists at gunpoint** (NBC News, Andrew Blankstein and Dennis Romero, July 21, 2026) Joseph Charles Henrichsen, 49, and his adult son Phoenix Henrichsen, 23, have been charged with kidnapping a federal employee and aiding and abetting after allegedly holding two U.S. Forest Service biologists at gunpoint in Northern California. According to a federal affidavit, on Thursday morning Joseph approached the two biologists (a man and a woman, not publicly identified) who were conducting frog habitat research near Gumboot Lake Campground in Shasta-Trinity National Forest, about 290 miles north of San Francisco. Armed with an AR-15-style rifle, he zip-tied their hands behind their backs and ordered them to a nearby camping trailer, where Phoenix joined them.

(U) Joseph allegedly drove one of the biologists (the woman) several miles in the Forest Service vehicle, used her phone to leave a voicemail for a federal forest ranger stating, “I’ve taken two fed biologists hostage from the Forest Service… Her hands are behind her f---ing back, and I’ve got live rounds ready,” and requested to speak with a federal investigator, preferably from the FBI. They then returned to the trailer, where the victims were held for hours at gunpoint. During negotiations, Joseph allegedly claimed to have a grenade and threatened to use it if officers did not back away. Negotiators secured the victims’ release after 1 a.m. Friday; the suspects then surrendered. No motive is listed in the charging documents. The pair appeared in federal court in Sacramento on Monday but did not enter pleas. If convicted of kidnapping a federal employee, they face a possible life sentence. A preliminary examination is scheduled for Aug. 3.

(U) Joseph Henrichsen previously lived in Orange County, California. In 2022 he was arrested in Whatcom County, Washington, on allegations that included throwing fireworks at a neighbor’s home, leaving homemade handcuffs and a note, and carrying a gun; that case (which included a hate crime charge) was dismissed due to delays in providing psychiatric treatment. Siskiyou County officials said the suspects were not previously known to local law enforcement.
