(U) Joseph Charles Henrichsen, 49, and his son Phoenix Henrichsen, 23, were charged with kidnapping a federal employee after allegedly holding two U.S. Forest Service biologists at gunpoint in California’s Shasta-Trinity National Forest on July 17. According to the affidavit, Joseph approached the biologists (who were studying frog habitats near Gumboot Lake Campground) while armed with an AR-15-style rifle, zip-tied their hands, and ordered them into a nearby trailer. One victim was driven several miles in a Forest Service vehicle while Joseph left a voicemail for a ranger stating he had taken two federal biologists hostage and had “live rounds ready.” The pair were held for hours; Joseph allegedly claimed to have a grenade during negotiations. The victims were released after 1 a.m. Friday following an hours-long standoff, and the suspects surrendered. No motive was listed in the charging documents. Both appeared in federal court in Sacramento but did not enter pleas. Conviction on the kidnapping charge carries a potential life sentence.
