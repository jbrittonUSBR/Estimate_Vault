(U) A shooting in Montreal’s Snowdon/Côte-des-Neiges Jewish neighborhood on June 22, 2026, left a police officer, a civilian, and the suspect dead, with another officer seriously injured but later reported out of danger. Around 11:15 a.m., gunfire erupted near the Hilton Garden hotel on Westbury Avenue, prompting a large police response, shelter-in-place alerts, and lockdowns at nearby Jewish institutions, schools, and St. Joseph’s Oratory. The deceased officer was 34-year-old Constable Mohamed Lamine Benredouane, while the civilian victim was identified as Michael (Michel) Moshe Mizrahi, a member of the local Jewish community; the suspect was shot and killed by police, with no other suspects confirmed. Montreal police Chief Fady Dagher described the incident as a “nightmare,” noting it had been 24 years since the last officer was killed in the line of duty, and emphasized that the motive remained unclear while urging the public to avoid rumors. The investigation was transferred to Quebec’s Bureau des enquêtes indépendantes, with community organizations like MADA providing shelter to evacuees and Federation CJA monitoring the situation amid recent antisemitic incidents in the area.
