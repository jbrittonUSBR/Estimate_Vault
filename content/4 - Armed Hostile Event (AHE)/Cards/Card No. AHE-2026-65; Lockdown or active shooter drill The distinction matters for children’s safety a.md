# Lockdown or active shooter drill? The distinction matters for children’s safety and well-being

[Source](https://theconversation.com/lockdown-or-active-shooter-drill-the-distinction-matters-for-childrens-safety-and-well-being-290133)

(U) LOWELL, United States — In an analysis for The Conversation, Emily Greene-Colozzi, an assistant professor of criminology and justice studies at UMass Lowell, wrote that lockdown drills and hyperrealistic active shooter simulations are routinely conflated even though they work very differently. She described a lockdown drill as three basic steps — locks, lights and moving out of sight — which can be called for reasons unrelated to a shooter, while active shooter simulations can involve actors, mock gunfire, fake blood and nonlethal projectiles. She noted that 37 of 50 states required schools to run active shooter or lockdown drills at least once a year as of 2025, that high-intensity simulations are restricted in some states including New York and California, and that many states have no guidelines for simulations. The piece is academic analysis and commentary rather than a report of a new incident.
