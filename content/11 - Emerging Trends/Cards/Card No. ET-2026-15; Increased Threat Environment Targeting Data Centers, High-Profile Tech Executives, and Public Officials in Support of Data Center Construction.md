(U//FOUO) Between 06 and 10 April 2026, there were two notable attacks in the US reportedly motivated by anti-technology grievances and violent extremist beliefs. These attacks correspond with an increase in both online and offline threats in 2026 toward data centers, high profile technology professionals, and/or public officials in support of data center construction and infrastructure. This elevated threat environment corresponds with a broader spike in anti-technology grievances and
extremism, which has risen in recent years in response to rapid technological innovations, such as the implementation and widespread use of Artificial Intelligence (Al).
- (U) On 10 April 2026, an individual traveled from Texas to California and threw a Molotov cocktail-style incendiary device at the residence of OpenAl's CEO. After fleeing from the residence, the individual then attempted to set a fire at the company headquarters before being arrested. In a three-part manifesto recovered by law enforcement, the individual highlighted their anti-technological motivations, specifically their opposition to Al and executives of various Al companies. The individual advocated for "others to kill and commit (similar) crimes," including a list of board members and executives of various Al companies and investors as potential targets.
- (U) On 06 April 2026, an unidentified individual fired 13 shots at the private residence of an Indianapolis City-County Councilor, leaving behind a note that read: "No Data Centers." The shooting occurred days after the Indianapolis Metropolitan Development Commission approved a data center development project on 01 April 2026.

(U//FOUO) Within both non-ideological and ideological violent extremist online spaces, advancements in technology, especially the construction of data centers and employment of Al, are often represented as an existential threat to society that requires immediate, and sometimes violent, action. Antitechnology extremist narratives appeal to a broad array of ideological movements - including anarchists, environmental extremists, and white supremacists - creating a common target between otherwise opposing movements. Since January 2026, there has been an observed growth in threats online against executives of large-scale technology companies, particularly within the Al sector, and/or public officials in support of data center construction.
- (U//FOUO) Following the 10 April 2026 attempted arson targeting OpenAl's CEO, 4chan users reacted to the incident by encouraging additional attacks against technology executives, justifying similar actions as "resistance and rebellion ."
- (U//FOUO) Following the 06 April 2026 shooting in Indianapolis, online accounts across the ideological spectrum praised the shooting and encouraged similar attacks as an effective means of change. Others encouraged the targeting of data centers directly, including attacks on fiber optic cables.
- (U//FOUO) On 31 March 2026, three individuals associated with a Michigan-based white supremacist group held an anti-data center protest in Howell, Michigan. The protest coincided with a nearby town hall organized by the Michigan Attorney General on a proposed data center project. The individuals held a large banner that read: "A Jewish data center has no home here." The group also shared fliers online, that they reportedly disseminated at the protest, criticizing the "Jewish" project, specifically naming the Michigan Attorney General, who is Jewish.
- (U//FOUO) On 18 February 2026, a user wrote in an online forum: "Assassinate your county commissioners ... that might get media attention." The comment was in response to a thread discussing the arrest of an Oklahoma man who expressed disapproval of a proposed data center and exceeded the three-minute time limit at a city council meeting in Claremore, OK.
- (U) On 06 February 2026, an individual was arrested in Trenton, Illinois after posting online threats to physically harm or shoot public officials proposing a nearby data center development project.

(U//FOUO) Both Homegrown Violent Extremists (HVEs) and Domestic Violent Extremists (DVEs) may also potentially target data centers in a bid to cause social and economic disruption in furtherance of their broader ideological agendas. The destruction of data centers represents a significant threat to critical infrastructure, including immediate digital service disruptions, communication failures, and
potential data loss. Since January 2026, CIAC has observed reports of online posts threatening sabotage and destruction of data centers. Several of these examples have provided tactical guidance and advice on a range of social media platforms.
- (U) On 05 April 2026, a collaborative lnstagr.am post from several anarchist-affiliated accounts, including one Durango, Colorado-based group, encouraged attacks on data centers, claiming they were vulnerable targets to arson. The post included an image of a 2021 data center fire in Strasbourg, France, that resulted in the disruption of millions of servers across France, including governmental and financial websites.
- (U//FOUO) Between 23-25 March 2026, several TikTok users reportedly suggested sabotaging data centers, specifically bombing data center HVAC systems using incendiary devices, such as Molotov cocktails. One comment specifically mentioned the construction of a datacenter being built in Joliet, Illinois.
- (U//FOUO) Between 04 and 06 March 2026, several pro-Iranian Telegram groups called for cyber and kinetic attacks on Western technology, including on data centers, which they claimed host military communications and targeting systems. Several comments mentioned US-based companies, such as Amazon, Cloudflare, Google, and Microsoft.
- (U//FOUO) On 16 January 2026, a thread on, 4chan called for the sabotage of Al datacenters across the United States, with several suggesting arson, cable cutting, and contamination of water used in the cooling systems.

(U//FOUO) Colorado currently has an estimated 56 data centers, including at least 48 in the Denver metro area, with dozens more being planned or under  construction. Several local communities, including in Denver's Elyria-Swansea neighborhood and Colorado Springs, have voiced concerns in recent months regarding data center construction.

(U//FOUO) There is currently no known active or credible threat involving any of the Colorado-specific data center projects. However, given recent notable attacks and the increase of threats in 2026 targeting data centers and those affiliated with their development, along with growing public discourse surrounding several large-scale proposed data center projects in Colorado, the CIAC urges safety and security officials to maintain increased awareness toward threats targeting data centers, in addition to high-profile professionals associated with the technology industry, and/or public officials in support of data center construction and infrastructure. These threats may continue to escalate and evolve as Al development fuels further data center construction, potential large-scale reconfiguration of US labor markets, and/or any resulting real or perceived environmental degradation.
