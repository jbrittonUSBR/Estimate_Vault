(U) The China Education Association for International Exchange (CEAIE) presents covert opportunities for foreign influence, intelligence collection, and intellectual property theft from U.S. academia. CEAIE fully omits its relationship with the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and its role in foreign influence efforts. On Western websites, CEAIE presents itself as a non-profit organization fostering international educational exchanges. However, its Chinese doctrine states it’s under absolute control of the CCP, it integrates CCP ideology, and adheres to CCP directives.

(U) To carry out the CCP’s strategic goals of foreign influence and intellectual property theft, the United Front Work Department (UFWD), the Ministry of Education (MOE), and the CEAIE operate in tandem to achieve such goals within the education sector. The UFWD is the CCP’s overarching influence organization and establishes directives for “united front” foreign influence and intelligence collection efforts. Its presence in academia is substantial in that it oversees and weaponizes CCP funded academic programs.3,4 The MOE implements UFWD directives by creating educational initiatives and managing foreign united front activities. The CEAIE acts as a diplomatic extension of the MOE, and ultimately the UFWD, to conduct foreign influence and information operations targeting U.S. academia. With this hierarchy, educational exchanges seemingly funded by the CEAIE and similar organizations are truly funded by, and subjected to the demands of, the CCP.

(U) Intelligence collection and foreign influence concerns arise with the CEAIE’s passively diplomatic targeting of U.S. academia. It harnesses a false sense of cooperation through promoting educational exchange opportunities for international students to travel to China. Conversely, the CEAIE offers Chinese “assistance” to U.S. government funded academic-industry programs directly supporting national security functions, representing explicit nefarious intentions. As China aims to grow its global innovative capacity, it places value on knowledge and technology transfers stemming from foreign exchanges and academic-industry collaborations. China targets U.S. academic-industry collaborators since U.S. research and development efforts have produced distinguished industry breakthroughs; an area that China would struggle to competitively maintain on its own.
