"Emerging Foreign Terrorist Organization Tactics Techniques and Procedures Pose an Increased Threat to Law Enforcement and Public Safety in the Philadelphia Area." Serial: DVIC#2026-00104, March 4, 2026. HSIN. Accessed April 3, 2026. 

(U//FOUO) Although not observed in the U.S., FTOs have frequently used armored vehicles to commit vehicle ramming and vehicle-bourne explosive device (VBIED) attacks, primarily in Africa and throughout the Middle East. 2 FTOs modify vehicles by adding reinforced panels and sidings around the car and windows, differing from typical vehicle modifications. Depictions of armored vehicles often show attackers welding scrap metal or doors from scrapped military vehicles onto functional VBIEDs. However, threat actors could leverage 3D printing to weaponize vehicles. Attackers make these modifications to protect the driver from counterattacks, giving them extra time to reach their target and detonate the explosives. Alternatively, attackers can use an armored vehicle to carry out a standard vehicle-ramming attack due to the modifications increasing durability, protection, and weight.
