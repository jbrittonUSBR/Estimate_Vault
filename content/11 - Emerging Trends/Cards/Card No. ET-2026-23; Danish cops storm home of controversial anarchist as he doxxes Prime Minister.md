(U) Danish police conducted a raid on the home of activist Lars Andersen after he publicly doxxed Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen by posting her private social security and phone numbers online as a form of protest against government policies on encryption and surveillance. Andersen, who describes himself as an anarcho-capitalist, framed the act as sharing his “two favorite numbers,” but authorities treated it as illegal harassment and identity theft, leading to masked officers forcibly entering his apartment, cutting the power, and briefly detaining him. The incident is part of a pattern of controversial actions by Andersen, including prior doxxing of other politicians, offering rewards for personal health records, and a 2025 arrest near the Justice Minister’s residence where he was found with a GPS tracker, knife, and pepper spray. While Andersen claims the raid was disproportionate and that his actions were morally justified even if illegal, critics, including former MP Rasmus Jarlov, view him as dangerous and his behavior as twisted intimidation of public officials. The case highlights ongoing tensions in Denmark between free speech protections, privacy laws, and concerns over government surveillance measures.
