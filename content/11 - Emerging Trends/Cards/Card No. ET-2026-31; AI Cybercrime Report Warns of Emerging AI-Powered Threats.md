(U) **AI Cybercrime Report Warns of Emerging AI-Powered Threats** (eSecurity Planet, Ken Underhill, July 22, 2026)

(U) A new ThreatDown report warns that artificial intelligence is fundamentally reshaping the cybercrime ecosystem by lowering barriers to sophisticated attacks and enabling more autonomous exploitation. Researchers estimate organizations have roughly a six-month window to prepare before the next generation of AI-powered threats becomes mainstream.

**Key findings**:
- **Lowered barriers**: AI enables autonomous reconnaissance, exploitation, and attack planning. In one cited case, an attacker used mainstream AI models to identify a SCADA interface in a Mexican municipal water utility, classify it as critical infrastructure, and recommend an attack path despite lacking OT expertise (the intrusion ultimately failed).
- **Accessible criminal AI tools**: Many underground services wrap legitimate frontier models with jailbreaks or resell access via cloud infrastructure. Researchers found more than 6,600 openly published guardrail-free AI models on Hugging Face, which together accumulated about 22 million downloads in a 30-day period. Locally runnable models evade cloud-provider monitoring.
- **Malware and techniques**: AI agents have automated reconnaissance, credential harvesting, and network penetration. Some malware generates malicious logic dynamically at runtime or runs AI models locally on compromised systems, reducing the effectiveness of signature-based detection and cloud-provider visibility.
- **Insider and agent risks**: Public marketplaces for AI agent “skills” have been targeted with malicious packages that steal credentials, exfiltrate data, install malware, or alter an agent’s long-term memory for persistence. Attackers also use social engineering, such as fake AI guides that deliver near-zero-detection credential stealers.
- **Shadow AI**: Nearly half of employees using generative AI access it through unmanaged personal accounts. One in five organizations experienced a shadow AI-linked breach in 2025, with those incidents costing an average of $670,000 more than standard breaches. Unmanaged tools often hold valuable credentials, OAuth tokens, and API keys.
- **Zero-day acceleration**: The report cites what it describes as the first known criminal use of AI to develop a zero-day exploit. Models such as Anthropic’s Mythos have identified zero-days across major operating systems and browsers and constructed multi-stage attack chains. Similar capabilities could appear on criminal marketplaces within 6–12 months.

**Recommendations**:
- Accelerate vulnerability management and patching.
- Maintain continuous monitoring (security operations or managed detection and response).
- Discover and govern shadow AI, including unauthorized tools, agent skills, and integrations.

(U) The report frames AI not merely as a productivity tool but as a force that allows attackers to automate reconnaissance, speed exploit development, and weaponize AI adoption itself.
