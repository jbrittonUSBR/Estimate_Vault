(U//LES) Horses, roosters, and cockfighting* equipment smuggled over the U.S. Southwest Border (SWB) are likely indicators of further criminal activity in the United States associated with Mexican transnational criminal organizations (TCOs). Horses smuggled into the United States from Mexico are often brought for recreational purposes, such as racing, equestrian shows, or roping; and live roosters are often used in cockfighting. Both animal sports are popular among Mexican criminals and are associated with cartel networks and money laundering. While cockfighting is widespread and legal in most of Mexico, it is illegal in the United States. Cockfighting equipment and paraphernalia, such as gaffs, spurs, and rooster steroids, are therefore subject to seizure if transported across the U.S. border.
- (U//LES) Horse smuggling across the SWB is especially common in Arizona, where horse smuggling routes have historically been used in the Nogales and Tucson sectors. 8,9 In January and February 2026, USBP identified several horse smuggling events in Nogales Sector that indicated TCO activities. Horse smugglers operating in Nogales are often ranch hands from the local area who have past criminal history in moving contraband and illegal aliens over the border. They act as coordinators and guides, acquiring horses in Mexico, identifying a U.S. buyer, and illegally transporting the horses across the border into the United States.10 In Tucson Sector, USBP has encountered smuggled horses that are either ridden or led from Mexico to subjects waiting with horse trailers over the U.S. border. When confronted by law enforcement, the waiting subjects often claim the horses are theirs but fail to provide any proof of ownership.
- (U//LES) CBP reporting indicates that Mexican TCOs are linked to horse smuggling and recreational horse sports in the United States. In February 2026, USBP agents in Nogales received information from a prior employer of a horse smuggler who refused to give his name because of fear of cartel retaliation.13 In July 2025, CBP assessed that Cartel del Noreste (CDN) was very likely using a horse racetrack in Tamaulipas State, Mexico, as a as a front for cartel members to operate with limited scrutiny. CDN members have a historical interest in horse racing; from 2008 to 2013, CDN members purchased, trained, and bred American Quarter Horses in the United States and Mexico to launder money from the sale of drugs.14 As of December 2025, a horse trainer in the United States was suspected of financing the Juarez TCO through wire transfers to Jalisco and Chihuahua States in Mexico.

- (U//LES) Illegal cockfighting events continue to occur in the United States and are often attended by Mexican criminals. According to a March 2026 CBP interview, members of the Los Chapitos faction of the Sinaloa Cartel participate in cockfights and house parties at a home in Glendale, Arizona. Likewise, members of the primarily Mexican Los Paisas prison gang meet and mingle with smugglers at a cockfighting arena in Mission, Texas. Visitors to the arena included armed illegal aliens and drug smugglers. Based on an April 2025 alien interview, the Vallucos TCO, CDN, and Cartel del Golfo (CDG) have multiple stash houses in Zapata, Texas, for smuggled aliens, drugs, and guns. Cockfighting events, accompanied by gambling, also occur at these stash houses. According to CBP reporting, one of the stash house owners used his fighting roosters as an unofficial security system for the property.
