(U) The Jamaica Customs Agency has warned of an emerging threat from synthetic drugs, particularly MDMA/ecstasy, at the country’s borders, with detections rising from three cases in 2024 to six in 2025 and already four in the first half of 2026. Between January 2024 and June 2026, authorities seized a cumulative 34,292 MDMA tablets and pills, with approximately 85% involving inbound shipments primarily from Germany, the United States, Belgium, the Netherlands, Canada, and France, often concealed in postal and courier parcels. The Mail Inspection Centre accounted for over half of the detections, highlighting the use of legitimate shipping channels by traffickers. While the volumes remain lower than those of traditional narcotics like cocaine and marijuana, officials note that the recurring shipments and quantities suggest Jamaica is increasingly exposed to international synthetic drug supply chains and transnational criminal networks. Deputy CEO of Border Protection Marlon Lowe emphasized the agency’s use of intelligence-led enforcement, modern technology, and inter-agency collaboration to disrupt these threats and protect communities from the risks posed by MDMA, a popular party drug with potentially severe effects on the heart and brain.
