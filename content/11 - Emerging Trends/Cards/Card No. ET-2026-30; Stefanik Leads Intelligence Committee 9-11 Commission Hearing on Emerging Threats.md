(U) **Stefanik Leads Intelligence Committee 9/11 Commission Hearing on Emerging Threats** (Congresswoman Elise Stefanik press release, July 22, 2026) House Republican Leadership Chairwoman Elise Stefanik led a House Intelligence Committee hearing focused on new and emerging threat streams that have developed since the September 11 attacks. The hearing is part of a year-long bipartisan review of the 9/11 Commission Report recommendations ahead of the 25th anniversary of the attacks later in 2026. In her opening statement, Stefanik highlighted the “failure of imagination” identified by the original 9/11 Commission as a key shortcoming that allowed the attacks to occur. She noted that threats considered distant or improbable at the time (such as the use of aircraft as weapons) proved catastrophic, and stressed that policymakers, law enforcement, and the intelligence community must avoid repeating that failure.

(U) She described today’s threat landscape as broader and more complex than in 2001, citing examples that include:
- Great power competition
- Artificial intelligence
- Foreign malign influence campaigns
- Drones
- Hostile use of emerging biotechnologies
- Vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure

(U) Stefanik called for the intelligence community and law enforcement to think creatively and proactively, go on the offensive, and take preemptive action against adversaries. She emphasized the need to avoid complacency and noted that the Task Force on the 9/11 Commission has held hearings, conducted briefings with intelligence and law enforcement officials, visited the Pentagon 9/11 Memorial, and plans to visit the memorials in Shanksville and New York City. The statement concluded by thanking the panel of witnesses for participating in the discussion on confronting the next generation of threats.
