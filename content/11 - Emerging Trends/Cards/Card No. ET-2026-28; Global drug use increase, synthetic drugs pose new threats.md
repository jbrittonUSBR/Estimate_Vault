According to the report as summarized in the article, global drug use continues to climb, reaching approximately 331 million users in 2024, with 6.2 percent of the world's population aged 15–64 reported to have used drugs that year, up from 5.2 percent in 2014. Cannabis is described as the most widely used substance (about 256 million users), followed by opioids (63 million), amphetamines (32 million), cocaine (25 million), and ecstasy (21 million). The report flags a shift toward synthetic opioids — fentanyl, nitazenes, and other emerging substances — as traffickers adopt new production methods, technologies, and trafficking routes, which the UNODC warns could reshape opioid markets and raise public health risks. On Afghanistan specifically, the article states the report identifies the Taliban's poppy cultivation ban since 2022 as a major factor behind a sharp decline in opium and heroin production, and notes that global opium production remains below prior levels overall even though Myanmar's output has risen. The piece closes by noting the UN's call for stronger international cooperation, information-sharing, anti-trafficking efforts, prevention and treatment programs, and alternative livelihoods for affected communities.
