On 31 March 2026, the group reached Pijijiapan, Chiapas State after a 82 mile trek. Mexican authorities reportedly observed the caravan without intervening. However, as of 10 April 2026, the caravan had reportedly dispersed in Chiapas; the remaining migrants in the area were transported by Mexicoâ€™s National Migration Institute (INM) to other cities in the region.

(U//LES) CBP reporting indicates that the caravan was organized through social networks and WhatsApp groups. The group intended to follow established northbound routes through the border state of Chiapas, reach the neighboring state of Oaxaca, and continue deeper into Mexico in search of work and stability. Migrants in Mexico who do not have legal refugee status cannot work in factories or shops, and they are also ineligible for Mexican government social security benefits. As a tactic, the Government of Mexico (GoM) typically sets up checkpoints to disperse crowds, offers travel permits to travel to various locations in Mexico, and occasionally offers temporary work visas in response to migrant issues. With tighter border restrictions in the United States, many migrants no longer attempt to reach the United States.

(U//LES) Currently, there is no reporting to indicate that the Genesis 2026 Caravan planned to reach the U.S. Southwest Border (SWB) or enter the United States. This is the latest in a series of caravans in recent months, which have been seeking to regularize migrant status in Mexico.
