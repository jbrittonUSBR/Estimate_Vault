(U) The recent diplomatic thaw and border-calming talks between India and New Delhi do not signify a grand strategic reset or reconciliation. Instead, it represents a **pragmatic supply-chain truce**. India has realized that completely decoupling from China is backfiring on its own domestic manufacturing goals, prompting New Delhi to selectively ease economic restrictions to build up its industrial base.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Cost of Over-Restriction:** Following the 2020 border clashes, India placed tight restrictions on Chinese investments, visas, and business travel. However, this ultimately stifled Indian factories, which heavily rely on Chinese machinery and technical specialists (costing the electronics sector an estimated $15 billion over four years).
- **The Trade Reality:** India's trade deficit with China surpassed $100 billion, with total trade hitting roughly $151 billion. Indian manufacturing relies heavily on crucial Chinese industrial inputs like machinery, electronics, chemicals, and components.
- **Selective Looping of Rules:** India has begun permitting faster approval pathways for investments with up to 10% Chinese ownership in select sectors (like electronics and batteries) and is shifting toward licensing Chinese technology (such as EV platforms) rather than outright equity handovers.
- **Self-Reliance Continues:** Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s push for domestic self-reliance has not disappeared. New Delhi continues to target critical imports (like solar components and EVs) for domestic substitution.
- **The Border Remains Unresolved:** While high-level talks have eased tensions and quieted flashpoints along the Line of Actual Control (LAC), the underlying territorial dispute remains unchanged, and both nations maintain heavy military presence at the frontier.
### **Conclusion**

(U) India and China are engaging in **controlled interdependence**. New Delhi’s strategy has shifted to a harder truth: true self-reliance cannot be achieved by cutting off vital supply chains prematurely, but rather by using short-term dependency on Chinese machinery and know-how to build long-term domestic independence.
