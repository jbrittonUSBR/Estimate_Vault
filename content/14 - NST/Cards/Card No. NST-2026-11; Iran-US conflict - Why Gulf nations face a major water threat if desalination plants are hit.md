(U) The ongoing Iran-U.S. conflict has highlighted a critical vulnerability for Gulf Arab states: their heavy dependence on desalination plants for drinking water.
Gulf countries lack sufficient natural freshwater and rely on energy-intensive desalination of seawater. Approximate dependence levels include roughly 90% for Kuwait and Bahrain, 86% for Oman, and about 70% for Saudi Arabia. Many plants are large, coastal facilities (often co-located with power stations), making them potentially vulnerable to missile or drone strikes. Recent developments include Kuwait reporting Iranian attacks on power and desalination facilities (with fires and temporary suspensions of operations). Earlier in the conflict, similar incidents were reported involving plants in Bahrain and claims of attacks on Iranian facilities. Iran has warned that critical Gulf infrastructure could be targeted in response to U.S. actions. Analysts note that prolonged disruption of major plants could rapidly affect civilian water supplies, as many states maintain only limited reserves (days to about a week). This elevates desalination infrastructure as a strategic risk in the conflict beyond traditional energy targets.
