(U) The Associated Press article details the expansion of U.S. military and counterterrorism operations in Latin America under the Trump administration. This broader campaign marks a distinct strategic pivot toward the Western Hemisphere, utilizing direct military force to target drug cartels and projecting regional preeminence through the framework of a modernized Monroe Doctrine.
### **Key Aspects of the Operations**

- **From Sea to Land:** The administration's military campaign initially focused heavily on a series of deadly strikes against fast boats accused of drug trafficking in international waters across the Caribbean Sea and the eastern Pacific. The scope has since broadened to encompass potential actions on land, including coastal infrastructure strikes and proposed cooperation with regional governments.
- **Strategic Shift:** The White House codified a counterterrorism strategy that establishes the elimination of Western Hemisphere drug cartels as a top national security priority, outpacing other global conflict zones in strategic emphasis.
- **Regional Dynamics and Pushback:** While the strategy has found alignment or allowance with certain political shifts in South America, it has also generated widespread debate. Critics, legal experts, and international watchdogs have repeatedly questioned the legality, transparency, and human rights implications of the continuous kinetic strikes, contrasting sharply with the administration's framing of the campaign as an armed conflict against narco-terrorism.
