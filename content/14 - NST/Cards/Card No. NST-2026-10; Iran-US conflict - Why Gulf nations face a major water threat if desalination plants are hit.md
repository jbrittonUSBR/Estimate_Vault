(U) **Iran-US conflict: Why Gulf nations face a major water threat if desalination plants are hit** (Moneycontrol, July 21, 2026) Gulf nations’ heavy reliance on desalinated seawater for drinking water has become a growing concern amid escalating Iran-U.S. military confrontation, as critical desalination plants emerge as potential vulnerabilities.

**Recent developments**:
(U) Kuwait reported that an Iranian attack hit a power and desalination facility for the second time in a few days. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards claimed attacks on U.S. military installations in Bahrain and Kuwait (air defence systems, radars, and related equipment). Iran’s army separately claimed kamikaze drone strikes on three major U.S. bases in Kuwait (Camp Arifjan, Camp Al Udairi, and Ahmad Al Jaber Air Base). These claims followed U.S. strikes on Iranian military targets. An Iranian official also said a desalination facility in Iran itself had been struck by a U.S. missile attack.

**Why desalination is critical**:
(U) Much of the Gulf is extremely arid with limited natural freshwater. Approximate dependence on desalinated water:
- Kuwait and Oman: ~90%
- UAE: ~80%
- Qatar: nearly 100%
- Bahrain: ~85%
- Saudi Arabia: ~70%

(U) Major cities such as Dubai, Doha, Kuwait City, Abu Dhabi, and Jeddah could face significant disruption if key plants go offline for an extended period.

**Vulnerabilities**:
- Capacity is concentrated in a limited number of large coastal plants, creating single points of failure.
- Plants are within potential range of missiles and drones.
- Desalination is energy-intensive, so strikes on power infrastructure can indirectly halt water production.
- The Atlantic Council has warned that attacks could trigger broader humanitarian and economic crises beyond immediate physical damage.

(U) A prolonged outage could pressure supplies for households, hospitals, businesses, and public services, potentially forcing emergency measures including evacuations in a worst-case scenario. While Iran is less dependent on desalination overall, the technology remains important for some of its coastal communities. For Gulf Arab states, protection of these facilities is a critical concern as the conflict intensifies.
