(U) The article highlights cybersecurity research showing how **AI assistants embedded in enterprise email and office accounts** (such as Microsoft Copilot) can be weaponized by threat actors to act as the ultimate "insider threat". Red team simulations reveal that once a single user account is compromised, attackers can use the built-in AI tool to rapidly conduct reconnaissance, escalate privileges, and execute financial fraud.
### **Key Takeaways from the Research**

- **Instant Reconnaissance:** Attackers used a compromised employee's AI assistant to instantly parse months of emails, calendar items, and documents to map the corporate structure, identify high-value targets, and uncover sensitive conversations.
- **Evasion and Persistence:** The AI was prompted to create hidden inbox rules that concealed sign-in alerts and security notifications from the actual user, maintaining stealthy persistence.
- **Impersonation and Spear Phishing:** Armed with inside context, the AI drafted highly convincing phishing messages matching the victim's natural writing style. This allowed attackers to compromise a higher-level executive (the CEO) via session-token theft.
- **Executing Financial Fraud ($250K Heist):** Inside the CEO's mailbox, the AI quickly surfaced active invoices and a pending wire transfer (worth nearly $250,000). The attackers used the AI to draft a legitimate-looking request to the finance team instructing them to alter the destination bank account.
- **Covering Tracks:** The AI assisted in setting up forwarding rules to intercept confirmation emails and locate evidence to delete, bypassing traditional security controls because the requests originated from authentic, authenticated mailboxes.
### **Conclusion**

The primary security risk is not that AI creates new vulnerabilities, but that it exponentially increases the **speed, scale, and efficiency** of an attack post-compromise. Because the AI behaves like a deeply knowledgeable insider with access to vast historic data, organizations must adapt by treating AI-enabled accounts as high-value assets, applying strict access controls, and closely monitoring behavioral anomalies.
