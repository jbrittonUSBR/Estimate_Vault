# The Case for Economic Espionage

[Source](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/case-economic-espionage-david-cohen)

(U) WASHINGTON, DC — In a Foreign Affairs essay published October 8, 2026, David S. Cohen, a former deputy director of the CIA and undersecretary of the Treasury, argued that the United States should lift its long-standing self-imposed ban on economic espionage and allow its intelligence agencies to collect commercial secrets from foreign companies and share them with American frontier-technology firms. He cited the Center for Strategic and International Studies' record of 224 incidents of Chinese-sponsored espionage against the United States between 2000 and 2023, more than half targeting commercial technologies, and contended that U.S. national security now depends heavily on private-sector innovation in areas such as artificial intelligence, semiconductors, quantum science and bioengineering. Cohen traced the policy's origins from Cold War debates through President Barack Obama's 2014 codification in PPD-28 and its reaffirmation under later administrations. He proposed authorizing the practice through an executive order and complementary Intelligence Authorization Act provisions, targeting adversaries while excluding close allies, prioritizing signals intelligence, and requiring congressional oversight and inspector-general audits to prevent politicization or corruption. (This is an attributed opinion piece.)
