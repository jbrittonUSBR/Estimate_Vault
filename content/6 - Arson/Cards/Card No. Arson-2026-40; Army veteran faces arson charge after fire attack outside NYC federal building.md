(U) **Army veteran faces arson charge after attack outside NYC federal building: 'Came armed with bad intentions'** (Fox News, July 21, 2026) Andrew Arrabaca, 43, a U.S. Army veteran (former Patriot missile systems mechanic), faces an arson charge for the Monday attack outside 26 Federal Plaza in Lower Manhattan, a building that houses an immigration court, ICE offices, the FBI, and other agencies. Three people were injured. He appeared in Manhattan federal court Tuesday and did not enter a plea.

**Key details from prosecutors and the FBI**:
- Arrabaca allegedly arrived around 8:30 a.m. pulling a four-wheeled utility wagon with a sign reading “ICE Off Our Streets.” The wagon contained BB/pellet rifles, fireworks (including one labeled “Nuclear Warhead”), and other items. He was wearing a helmet with phrases including “Kill yourself.”
- He lit a large firework, poured a flammable liquid, and ignited a fire near a busy entrance. He also allegedly fired five to seven pellets from an airsoft/pellet rifle toward the building.
- Upon arrest, authorities recovered two axes, a hammer, a machete, three knives, and other items. FBI Assistant Director James Barnacle described him as having “come armed with bad intentions” and as an “anti-American, anti-government extremist.”
- He made anti-government and anti-ICE statements and allegedly said he was okay if he hurt or killed people (federal employees or civilians) entering or leaving the building.
- Prosecutors said he had been planning the attack since January, buying supplies and visiting potential sites. He spent the night before in a park, targeted a busy entrance, and admitted awareness that people could be hurt or that he could die. At least one victim suffered a head gash from debris.
- One of the injured was an immigrant who was at the building for a hearing.

(U) U.S. Magistrate Judge Robyn F. Tarnofsky called the crime “troubling,” noted it was planned over time, and ordered Arrabaca released to a mental health facility after a bail hearing. Defense counsel cited mental health issues and requested placement in a VA facility; prosecutors noted he lacked stable housing or employment and had been living out of a car. A preliminary hearing is set for Aug. 4. He faces a mandatory minimum of seven years in prison if convicted on the arson charge. Voter records showed he was registered as a Democrat and had been a member of the Working Families Party.
