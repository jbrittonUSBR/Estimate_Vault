# Man Arrested After Allegedly Shooting Flaming Projectile at Flock Cameras, Melting Them Into Slag

[Source](https://futurism.com/future-society/flock-alpr-arrest-flaming-projectile-melt-slag)

**Query:** `Arson`

(U) A man is facing numerous felony charges in Georgia after police allege he allegedly used a bizarre projectile to melt automatic license plate readers into smoldering heaps of metal and plastic. According [to _Fox5_ in Atlanta](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/how-police-used-flock-cameras-catch-man-accused-burning-them), 25-year-old Colby Williams was hauled into jail after allegedly setting fire to three automatic license plate readers (ALPRs) installed by the [extremely controversial](https://futurism.com/future-society/flock-alpr-surveillance-major-trouble-cancellations) company Flock. Though it isn’t known how the fires started, at least one camera was found “completely melted,” and had a “DeFlock” sticker slapped onto what remained of the pole. A fourth camera nearby was reportedly found with two bullet holes, the news channel reported. Ironically, Williams was arrest using video captured from the ALPRs, which picked up a vehicle registered under his name. Williams is now being charged with four counts of criminal damage to property and four counts of arson, as well as reckless conduct and interference with government property — though Flock operates using a model known as “hardware-as-a-service,” meaning its ALPRs are only rented, not owned by police agencies.The use of fire to disable Flock ALPRs is a new one, as far as we can tell. So far, anti-surveillance vigilantes have mostly resorted to using tools like [hand saws](https://www.actionnewsjax.com/news/local/man-who-cut-down-flock-camera-st-johns-county-claimed-he-was-working-with-nsa/5CGW3OYPKRFKFD3OYU33WNL5KQ/) and [power drills](https://futurism.com/future-society/flock-alpr-cameras-activist-police-station-pickup-truck), or barring that, [spray paint](https://www.news4jax.com/news/local/2026/07/30/19-year-old-accused-of-spray-painting-over-half-a-dozen-flock-cameras-at-the-beaches/).
