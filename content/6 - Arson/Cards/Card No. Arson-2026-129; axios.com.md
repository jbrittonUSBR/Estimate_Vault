# Violence, Looting, Arson: Limoges Town Hall Destroyed as France Faces Cost-of-Living Unrest

[Source](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/19/france-bond-market-limoges-protests)

(U) LIMOGES, France — The historic Limoges town hall was largely destroyed by a deliberately set fire during nationwide cost-of-living protests on October 17, 2026, with masked demonstrators breaking in after torching a police vehicle outside and setting the 19th-century building alight. The prefect of Haute-Vienne reported the roof collapsed onto lower floors and roughly two-thirds of the exterior was lost, calling the damage to the 143-year-old landmark a "catastrophe for heritage." The interior ministry said about 550,000 people took part in protests nationwide, including roughly 110,000 in Paris; nearly 2,400 people were detained and around 1,000 police and gendarmes were injured, seven seriously. Shops were looted in central Limoges and in Paris, Bordeaux, and Toulouse, with the French retail federation estimating 1,500 businesses directly affected and damage in the hundreds of millions of euros. A suspect was arrested over the town hall fire on October 20; the Association of Mayors of France called for rallies of solidarity outside town halls nationwide, and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu pledged the building would be rebuilt.
