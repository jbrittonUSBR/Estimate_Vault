(U//FOUO) Overview: On 10 April 2026, news outlets reported that an employee recorded himself setting fire to paper products in a Kimberly-Clark warehouse in Ontario, CA. In the posted video, the suspect can be heard saying "If you're not going to pay us enough to f*****g live, at least pay us enough not to do this s**t,” prompting significant support for the act. The fire destroyed the entire structure, causing more than $600 million in damage. Following the incident, the employee reportedly compared himself to the individual accused of assassinating the United Healthcare CEO, in a text to his coworkers. Overall, the fire and the suspect continue to receive positive attention online, with many calling him a hero and his actions justified. Social media users have recently been tracking warehouse fires as evidence that politically motivated attacks are on the rise. Users have also been posting videos of their own controlled fires as an act of solidarity with the suspect who burned the Kimberly-Clark warehouse. Additionally, this incident has contributed to conspiracy theories gaining traction online attributing all warehouses fires to politically motivated direct action, with some users claiming that the war on the ruling class has begun. The following are subsequent incidents of direct action targeting corporations and business executives: 
- (U) On 15 April 2026, a social media thread showed users supporting the suspected arsonist of the Kimberly-Clark warehouse and proposed “elites”, such as business executives and politicians, as targets for attacks. Additionally, users suggested targeting other warehouses and supply chains.
- (U) On 13 April 2026, mainstream media reported that a man was arrested for throwing an incendiary device at the home of OpenAI’s CEO and striking the glass doors of the home with a chair while saying he wanted “to burn it down and kill everyone inside.” During the investigation, the suspect was found to be in possession of documents titled “Your Last Warning” that advocated for the killing of CEOs of AI companies. Following this incident, social media users compared this attacker to the individual accused of assassinating the United Healthcare CEO, calling them both “heroes” and their actions “justified.”

(U//FOUO) Analyst Comment: While the DVIC is currently unaware of any specific or credible threat, recent online rhetoric in support of these attacks may lead to increased anti-capitalist direct action targeting large corporations and business executives in the Philadelphia area. Due to the support of the Kimberly-Clark fire, large warehouses may be targeted as a means of gaining support and admiration online. Philadelphia-based anti-capitalist and anarchist communities have a long history of carrying out vandalism and direct action, targeting large businesses. Historically, companies in Philadelphia such as Comcast, Bank of America, Starbucks, Amazon, and Citizen’s Bank have received negative attention online and in some cases been victims of vandalism and direct action in support of anti-capitalist causes. Additionally, due to current controversy regarding AI advancements, businesses that profit from AI, as well as government officials who support legislation protecting large AI companies, may be targeted. Furthermore, due to ongoing international conflicts, local defense contractors such as Day & Zimmermann and Ghost Robotics have had their offices and employees’ residences targeted. When carrying out direct action, local threat actors often engage in acts of vandalism using projectiles such as rocks and paint bombs as well as spray paint and, on occasion, improvised incendiary devices (IEDs). These incidents usually occur within the overnight hours of 2300- 0300 hour and are carried out by small groups of individuals consisting of around 2-3 offenders. Furthermore, anti-capitalist action could include incidents of shoplifting, sabotage to ATMs and bank storefronts, as well as disruptive demonstrations designed to block traffic and ingress/egress routes
