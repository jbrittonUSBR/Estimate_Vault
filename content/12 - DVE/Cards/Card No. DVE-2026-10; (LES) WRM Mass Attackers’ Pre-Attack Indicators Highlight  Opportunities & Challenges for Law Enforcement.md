(U//LES) Of the 17 attackers evaluated, 15 experienced social isolation. Attackers may have experienced social isolation due to difficult life circumstances, feeling like they didn’t fit in, or chose to self-isolate to conceal their plans to conduct an attack. In the incidents below, the attackers were described as secluding in their rooms, remaining unemployed for a decade, feeling like outsiders, or cutting off friends.
-
- (U) Prior to his 26 August 2023 attack, Ryan Palmeter failed out of college, lost a retail job, and subsequently spent all of his time in his room, according to open source reporting. Palmeter’s neighbors and former classmates described him as socially awkward and introverted. Palmeter targeted African Americans in a shooting at a Dollar General in Jacksonville, Florida.
- (U) Following his 17 June 2017 attack, Darren Osborne’s trial judge revealed Osborne was unemployed for 10 years and stated the subject was described as a loner, according to court documentation. Osborne threatened to kill himself twice in the weeks prior to committing a mass attack, which his romantic partner believed stemmed from Osborne feeling worthless and like he did not fit in. Osborne drove a van into a group of Muslims near a mosque in London, England.
- (U) Following his 22 October 2015 attack, Anton Lundin Pettersson’s brother stated Pettersson felt like an outsider as an adolescent, according to open source reporting. After primary school, Pettersson became more isolated and did not develop friendships at his industry school. After the attack, a former classmate also described Pettersson as a loner living in his own world.7 Pettersson conducted a stabbing attack at a school he targeted due to its high immigrant population in Trollhättan, Sweden.
- (U) Prior to his 22 July 2011 attack, Anders Behring Breivik’s friends stated he became increasingly withdrawn, cut contact with friends, and spent most of his time playing a video game, according to open source reporting. In 2006, Breivik filed for bankruptcy and moved in with his mother, at which point Breivik’s friends noticed changes in his behavior. Breivik detonated a bomb outside a government building in Oslo, Norway before traveling to Utøya, Norway where he opened fire on the Norwegian Labor Party’s youth camp.

**(U) Mental Disorder Diagnoses 

(U//FOUO) Of the 17 attackers evaluated, 15 had either been professionally diagnosed or believed they had a mental disorder. Eleven of the 15 received professional diagnoses before or after their attack, and three believed they had a mental disorder. One attacker was killed during his attack, but a pediatrician who studied the case and a relative believed he had a mental disorder.
- (U) Prior to his 14 May 2022 attack, Payton Gendron kept a diary on Discord in which he stated several times he had never been diagnosed but believed he was autistic and “mildly schizophrenic or schizotypal,” according to open source reporting. In one entry, Gendron posted a screenshot of his results from an online autism test, which described him as having moderate signs of autism, specifically Asperger Syndrome. Gendron targeted African Americans when he opened fire on Top’s Market in Buffalo, New York.
- (U) Following his 6 June 2021 attack, Nathaniel Veltman met with a forensic psychiatrist several times and received several diagnoses, according to open source reporting. Veltman was diagnosed with anxiety and depression beginning when he was 12 or 13, autism spectrum disorder, obsessive compulsive disorder, complex trauma resulting from childhood experiences, and a personality disorder with schizotypal, paranoid, and borderline traits. Veltman drove his vehicle into a Muslim family on a street in London, Canada.
- (U) Prior to his 27 October 2018 attack, Robert Bowers suffered from mental illness beginning in his childhood, which continued through adulthood, according to open source reporting. At 10 years old, Bowers expressed suicidal ideations, and by the age of 13, he was admitted to an adult psychiatric ward after he chased his mother with a flammable liquid he attempted to light on fire. Doctors described Bowers as depressed and oppositional with bizarre behavior. Bowers attempted to commit suicide at least four times, including by overdosing on aspirin and lighting himself on fire as a teenager.14 Bowers opened fire on a synagogue in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania.
- (U) Prior to his 29 January 2017 attack, Alexandre Bissonnette suffered from anxiety, depression, and suicidal ideations, according to open source reporting. The subject lied on his application for a firearms permit by reporting no history of mental health issues or suicidal thoughts to purchase six firearms over several years, and he attempted to use one of the handguns to commit suicide several times. Bissonnette opened fire on a mosque in Quebec, Canada.
- (U) Following his 22 July 2011 attack, Anders Behring Breivik was evaluated by two pairs of psychiatrists who diagnosed him with different mental disorders, according to open source reporting. The first pair determined he had paranoid schizophrenia due in part to Breivik’s bizarre grandiose delusions. A second pair determined he had severe narcissistic personality disorder combined with pathological lying.

**(U) Pre-Attack Surveillance 

(U//LES) Of the 17 attackers evaluated, 11 conducted pre-attack surveillance of their target, either online or in person. Eight of the 11 are believed to have conducted the surveillance in person, with some visiting the targeted location multiple times. One attacker only conducted surveillance online, including studying photos of the target and researching when the location would be closed.20,21 The final two conducted surveillance online and in person.
- (U) Prior to his 14 May 2022 attack, Payton Gendron detailed his three visits to Top’s Market on 8 March 2022 in his Discord diary, according to open source information. During his visits, Gendron took notes and created maps of the market’s interior with some versions including markings for the security guards’ locations. Gendron counted the number of people he perceived to be white or black in the store during each visit. In between the visits, Gendron located options for where to park his car and to “gear up” on the day of the attack. During the final visit, security personnel questioned Gendron for having entered the store several times that day, to which he replied he was collecting “consensus data.”
- (U//LES) On the morning of 3 August 2019, Patrick Crusius walked through the Walmart in El Paso, Texas twice before conducting his attack, according to FBI reporting. The first walk through occurred approximately one hour and 20 minutes prior to the attack, and Crusius did not make a purchase. During the second walk-through, Crusius purchased oranges. Upon entering the store the third time, Crusius conducted his attack targeting Hispanics.
- (U) Following his 15 March 2019 attack, law enforcement determined Brenton Tarrant had used a drone to conduct surveillance at one of the mosques he targeted on 8 January 2019, according to open source reporting.25 Tarrant also downloaded a walk-through video of the same mosque from Facebook on 11 March 2019. Some attendees stated Tarrant visited the mosque at least three times prior to the attack, claimed to be Muslim, and prayed in the mosque.27 Tarrant opened fire on two mosques in Christchurch, New Zealand.
- (U) Prior to his 17 June 2015 attack, Dylann Roof visited the church he targeted on eight occasions between 22 December 2014 and 16 May 2015, according to open source reporting. On the day of the attack, Roof sat in on the church’s weekly Bible study for 40 minutes before beginning his attack. Roof opened fire in a historic African American church in Charleston, South Carolina.

**(U) Interest in Previous Mass Attackers 

(U//LES) Of the 17 attackers evaluated, 13 showed interest in previous mass attackers who conducted WRM and non-ideological attacks. Some of them mentioned the previous attackers in their manifestos and writings, while others researched attackers or communicated their interest in past attacks to others.
- (U) Following his 9 October 2019 attack, Stephan Balliet stated he was inspired by Brenton Tarrant’s attack, according to a federal prosecutor. The prosecutor also stated Balliet was inspired by other past attacks and hoped to inspire future attacks. Balliet attempted to conduct a shooting at a synagogue in Halle, Germany but could not access the building and instead targeted people in the street and at a local kebab shop.
- (U) Prior to his 15 March 2019 attack, Brenton Tarrant praised several previous WRM mass attackers in his manifesto, according to open source information. Specifically, he expressed support for Luca Traini, Anders Breivik, Dylann Roof, Anton Lundin Pettersson, and Darren Osborne. However, Tarrant stated he only took true inspiration from Anders Breivik and claimed to have had brief contact with him. Tarrant wrote several messages on his firearms and magazines, including the names of WRM mass attackers Alexandre Bissonnette and Luca Traini. 
- (U) Following Anton Lundin Pettersson’s attack on 22 October 2015, law enforcement discovered he had studied previous mass attackers, according to open source reporting. The mass attackers included the non-ideologically motivated Columbine shooters, Anders Breivik, and involuntary celibate violent extremist Elliot Rodger.

**(U) Ideological Online Content 

(U//FOUO) Of the 17 attackers evaluated, 15 consumed or produced ideological content online. Some of these attackers were “lurkers” in ideological online communities,h while others posted and interacted in the communities.
- (U) Prior to his 12 October 2022 attack, Juraj Krajčík wrote in his manifesto that he joined 8chan after viewing other violent extremists’ manifestos posted to the anonymous imageboard site, according to open source reporting. Krajčík said he was never the same after joining 8chan and it changed his worldview.37Additionally, Krajčík was the first WRM attacker tied to Terrorgram, and Terrorgram affiliates provided Krajčík with ideological and operational materials that contributed to his radicalization. Juraj Krajčík opened fire outside an LGBTQ+ bar in Bratislava, Slovakia.
- (U) Prior to his 27 April 2019 attack, John Earnest made a post on 8chan with his final words, a link to his manifesto and livestream, and his soundtrack for the attack, according to open source reporting. Earnest stated, “I’ve only been lurking for a year and a half, yet what I’ve learned here is priceless.”40 Earnest opened fire on a synagogue in Poway, California.
- (U) Prior to his 27 October 2018 attack, Robert Bowers regularly posted, liked, and shared anti Semitic content on Gab, according to open source reporting. Bowers joined Gab in January 2018, and his biography stated, “jews are the children of satan.” On the morning of his attack, Bowers made a final Gab post in which he criticized a Jewish non-profit organization and stated, “I can’t sit by and watch my people get slaughtered. Screw your optics, I’m going in.”
- (U) Following his 12 August 2017 attack, James Alex Fields Jr. admitted to using social media to express and promote his white supremacist views, including his support of Adolf Hitler, Nazi-era Germany, and the Holocaust, according to DOJ reporting. Fields also stated he used social media to espouse violence toward communities he perceived to be non-white, including African Americans and Jewish people. Fields drove his car into a crowd of people protesting the white nationalist “Unite the Right” rally in Charlottesville, Virginia.

**(U) Weapon Acquisition

(U//FOUO) Of the 17 attackers evaluated, 12 used firearms as the primary weapon to conduct their attack. Eight of those acquired the firearms legally, and two purchased their firearms by lying on their firearm applications. Another made his own firearms and explosives, while the remaining attacker stole his father’s firearm. Three other attackers used vehicles and two used bladed weapons.
- (U) In his 12 August 2024 attack, Arda Küçükyetim originally planned to use a homemade explosive but was worried about the dangers of making the explosive at home and armed himself with a knife and hatchet instead, according to open source reporting. Küçükyetim stabbed five people in the garden of a mosque in Eskişehir, Turkey.
- (U) During his 6 June 2021 attack, Nathaniel Veltman drove a Dodge Ram into a Muslim family walking on the street, according to open source reporting. Veltman purchased the Dodge on 19 May 2021 and had a 200-pound push bar added to the front.
- (U) Stephan Balliet used homemade firearms and explosives in his 9 October 2019 attack, according to open source reporting. He claimed to have used 3D-printed parts as well as “low-tech components” from a standard warehouse to assemble his weapons.49 According to his manifesto, Balliet had several types of homemade explosives, including nail bombs, pipe bombs, and fragmentation grenades. His manifesto also included photos and explanations for the six firearms he created, and Balliet stated he had a secret weapon that would be seen on the livestream of his attack.
- (U) Prior to his 3 February 2018, Luca Traini legally acquired the firearm he used in his attack in Macerata, Italy, according to open source reporting. Traini drove through the streets of Macerata looking for individuals he believed were African migrants and shot at them while he drove.
- (U) During his 17 June 2015 attack, Dylann Roof used a firearm he purchased after lying on ATF Form 4473, according to FBI and open source reporting. Earlier that year, Roof was arrested on a felony drug charge and admitted the drugs in his possession were not prescribed to him, which made Roof ineligible to purchase a firearm.55 Roof’s application was sent to the National Instant Criminal Background Check System for a background check, but the retailer did not receive a response within three days and sold Roof the firearm. In addition to the firearm, Roof purchased enough ammunition for five magazines.
