(U) The release announces that eight defendants tied to what prosecutors call the "North Texas Antifa Cell" were sentenced for a July 4, 2025 attack on the Prairieland ICE Detention Center in Alvarado, Texas, marking the first sentencing of defendants affiliated with Antifa following President Trump's September 2025 executive order designating the group a Domestic Terrorist Organization. Benjamin Hanil Song, described as the group's leader, received a 100-year sentence for the attempted murder of a responding Alvarado police officer he shot during the incident; combined, the eight defendants received 450 years — Maricela Rueda (70 years), and Cameron Arnold, Savanna Batten, Zachary Evetts, Bradford Morris, and Elizabeth Soto (50 years each), and Daniel Rolando Sanchez-Estrada (30 years). A ninth defendant, Ines Soto, was granted a continuance and was scheduled for sentencing on July 1, along with seven others who pleaded guilty before trial to providing material support to terrorists. According to the release, trial evidence — from a 12-day trial with 46 witnesses and over 210 exhibits — showed the group acquired more than 50 firearms, wore "black bloc" clothing to conceal identities, brought fireworks/explosives and military-grade medical kits to the scene, and coordinated over encrypted, auto-deleting messaging apps. The DOJ says at least eleven defendants threw fireworks, vandalized property, and that Song opened fire when police responded, striking an officer in the neck; Song fled and was captured on July 15, 2025. Charges included riot, providing material support to terrorists, use of an explosive during a riot, attempted murder, and (for two defendants) obstruction related to concealing Antifa-related documents. Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche, FBI Director Kash Patel, U.S. Attorney Ryan Raybould, and Acting ICE Director David Venturella are all quoted characterizing the sentences as a warning against political violence targeting law enforcement; the sentencing judges (Pittman and O'Connor) are also quoted describing the attack as attempted murder and "an assault on Democracy."
