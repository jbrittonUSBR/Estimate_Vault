(U) The Guardian article (published June 26, 2026) reports that, after securing long prison sentences against anti-ICE protesters in the "Prairieland" case in Texas, the Trump administration is now pursuing federal prosecution of protesters connected to the "Stop Cop City" movement opposing the Atlanta Public Safety Training Center. It notes the Department of Justice recently indicted two protesters over an alleged attack on a contractor and two employees working on the facility, despite Georgia state prosecutors having repeatedly failed to make similar cases stick (a Fulton County RICO case was dismissed, and a follow-up Cobb County case was also dismissed). The Guardian quotes attorney Xavier de Janon, who represents one of the Georgia defendants, drawing a parallel to the Texas case and suggesting prosecutors are building a preset "antifa cell" narrative around the protests. It also quotes filmmaker/author Will Potter, who studies government responses to protest movements, on concerns about how language like "fireworks" being recast as "explosives" is being used to build these cases.
