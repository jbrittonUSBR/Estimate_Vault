(U) The fatal school shooting in Tacloban has renewed concerns in the Philippines about the growing influence of online extremist subcultures and nihilistic violent extremism, as authorities investigate whether the attack—along with several prior incidents involving minors—stems from grooming and radicalization through gaming and social media platforms. The Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group reported rescuing 12 minors across five operations since late 2025, with two fatalities and a foiled plot involving seven others, often starting on Roblox before shifting to Discord or Telegram, where vulnerable youth are drawn into communities glorifying violence for status or misanthropic reasons. Similar patterns have been noted globally, including cases in Singapore and Russia involving game-inspired attacks, while the Tacloban incident featured possible references to violent games like Gorebox and symbols linked to school-shooting subcultures. Although officials have not yet confirmed direct online grooming in the Tacloban case, the Department of Justice has pointed to a broader extremism trend beyond simple bullying, prompting calls for stronger platform safeguards and monitoring of these digital spaces that increasingly connect isolated or troubled young people to harmful ideologies.
