(U) Flock Safety CEO Garrett Langley faced intense public and media scrutiny—and later issued an apology—after characterizing citizen-led transparency groups that map automated license plate reader (ALPR) cameras as "terroristic" organizations.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Target of the Remarks:** The backlash centered on Langley's comments labeling grassroots groups like **DeFlock**—volunteers who publicly log and map the geographic coordinates of public surveillance infrastructure—as "terroristic," drawing sharp contrasts with traditional civil liberties organizations like the ACLU.
- **Industry Defensiveness:** The remarks shed light on mounting tensions within the private surveillance industry as public pushback grows. Critics, privacy advocates, and automotive outlets noted that branding peaceful citizen transparency efforts as extremist threats attempts to inverse the debate, turning public scrutiny of mass tracking into a security issue.
- **The Subsequent Apology:** Following widespread condemnation from privacy groups, journalists, and civil rights advocates (including the ACLU), Langley walked back the rhetoric, admitting to _Forbes_ that he should not have labeled non-violent transparency activists with terroristic classifications.
- **Broader Public Friction:** The incident underscored the deepening cultural friction between municipal tech providers expanding AI-driven data collection networks and local communities demanding accountability, data restrictions, and visibility into public surveillance contracts.
