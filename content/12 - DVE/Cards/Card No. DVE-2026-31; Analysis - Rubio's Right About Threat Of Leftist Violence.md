(U) **Rubio's Right: Leftist Violence Is A Threat And It Has Happened Before** (MEMRI Daily Brief No. 957, Amb. Alberto M. Fernandez, July 21, 2026) Ambassador Alberto M. Fernandez argues that Secretary of State Marco Rubio is correct to highlight leftist political violence as a serious and historically recurring threat, countering skepticism from media and academic critics of the Trump administration’s focus on “violent left-wing extremists.”

(U) Fernandez frames current developments as potentially the start of a “third wave” of left-wing terrorism, following earlier historical patterns:

- **First wave**: Late 19th/early 20th-century anarchist bombings and assassinations, plus the large-scale Red Terror of communist regimes (e.g., Soviet Gulag, Chinese Cultural Revolution, Cuba, Cambodia).
- **Second wave**: Late 20th-century revolutionary groups such as the Red Brigades, Baader-Meinhof, Shining Path, and Weather Underground, often cross-fertilized internationally and supported by regimes including Cuba, Libya, the KGB, and East Germany. Survivors of these movements later entered academia and activism.

(U) He notes that left-wing terrorism never fully disappeared and that recent data (including from liberal publications) show it rising to levels not seen in decades. Fernandez links today’s activism—particularly protests following the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack—to the potential for radicalization, drawing parallels to how 1960s campus ferment contributed to 1970s violence.

(U) Contemporary examples cited include American leftists (e.g., Jackson Hinkle, Chris Helali of the American Communist Party, and Calla Walsh) attending the funeral of Iran’s Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, and the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) as a growing political force that mixes mainstream social-democratic branding with more extreme discourse. MEMRI’s Violent Extremist Threat Monitor is referenced as documenting such rhetoric. Fernandez praises the U.S. government’s current seriousness in addressing warning signs—through measures such as international cooperation to “disrupt, identify, defund, debank, arrest and prosecute” far-left violent extremists—arguing this proactive approach is preferable to the unpreparedness of the 1970s.
