(U) The essay, titled "The Right Wing Myth of the Tech Resistance" and dated June 29, 2026, contends that the media narrative around a conservative populist alliance with anti-tech commentator Joe Allen misreads the underlying motive. Its central claim is that populist skepticism of AI isn't a philosophical rejection of technology itself, but distrust of who currently controls it — the author argues the base would embrace an AI trained on nationalist or traditionalist content rather than reject AI outright. It argues Trump-aligned voters have little appetite for federal AI regulation because they fear such agencies would be captured by the same tech elites they distrust, framing the movement as "anti-gatekeeper" rather than "anti-tech." It also argues that political campaigns' own reliance on data infrastructure and automated tools undercuts the sincerity of anti-AI rhetoric, and concludes that the "real" fight is over control of physical infrastructure (energy, data centers, compute) rather than software, predicting the populist-right/Joe Allen alliance will dissolve once alternative, ideologically-aligned tech platforms emerge.
