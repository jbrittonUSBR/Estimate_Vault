(U) **Civil Terrorism Is a Threat to American Democracy** (City Journal, Tal Fortgang, July 20, 2026) Legal Policy Fellow Tal Fortgang argues that “civil terrorism”—lawless tactics such as vandalism, arson, nighttime demonstrations, and assaults intended to intimidate or coerce political opponents—poses a serious threat to democratic institutions, even when it falls short of bombings or assassinations. He cites Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s recent remarks distinguishing “overt” left-wing terrorism from “quieter” forms that suppress speech, intimidate opposition, and sabotage institutions, both of which Rubio classified as terrorism when used to achieve political goals through coercion.

(U) The central example is Rep. Adam Smith (D-Wash.), ranking Democrat on the House Armed Services Committee. On the day of Rubio’s remarks, Smith issued a statement explaining his vote to cut American aid to Israel. He described his family and home being vandalized, a fire set in his driveway, neighbors disrupted by middle-of-the-night demonstrations, town halls shut down, and a staff member physically assaulted. Fortgang calls this a “remarkable admission” that a member of Congress altered an important vote, at least in part, because of sustained intimidation by anti-Israel activists.

Fortgang identifies two scandals:
1. Smith’s capitulation rewards coercion and makes him and other lawmakers likelier future targets. Officials should respond to such tactics with law enforcement action and reinforced policy support rather than concession.
2. Institutions failed to deter the campaign of intimidation before it influenced a congressional vote. Tolerating systematic, lawless efforts to circumvent normal political persuasion undermines democracy.

(U) He recommends that states increase penalties for crimes committed with the intent to intimidate or coerce (Utah’s recent law elevating civil terrorism acts to felonies is cited as a model) and that the federal government consider parallel measures. Unpunished acts of civil terrorism, he argues, only embolden further efforts to distort politics through fear.
