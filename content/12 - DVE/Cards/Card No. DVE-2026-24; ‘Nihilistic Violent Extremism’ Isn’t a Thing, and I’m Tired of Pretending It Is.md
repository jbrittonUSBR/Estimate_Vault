(U) **Core argument:** Clifford argues the term "nihilistic violent extremism" (NVE) — introduced by the FBI in a spring 2025 court filing and since adopted across the counterterrorism field — should be retired because it's internally contradictory and analytically unhelpful. His case rests on several points:

- **Definitional contradiction:** The federal NVE definition requires actors to pursue "political, social, or religious goals," which directly conflicts with nihilism's core premise (no inherent purpose or values) — so "nihilistic" and "violent extremism" (which by definition requires a sociopolitical goal) can't logically coexist in one label.
- **Overbroad grouping:** NVE currently lumps together groups with fundamentally different motives and TTPs — including sextortion/CSAM networks like 764, neo-Nazi accelerationist groups like No Lives Matter and Maniacs Murder Cult, and others — solely because they overlap on the same online ecosystem ("The Com"), which he calls a category error.
- **764 specifically isn't a violent extremist group at all**, in his view — evidence suggests its members are driven by clout-seeking, sexual gratification, and status within online communities rather than any sociopolitical cause, so it shouldn't be classified as a counterterrorism threat at all, regardless of the label used.
- **Neo-Nazi accelerationist groups (No Lives Matter, Maniacs Murder Cult) are not nihilists either** — they have explicit white-supremacist goals and should instead be classified under existing terminology like racially/ethnically motivated violent extremism (RMVE).
- **Case study:** He cites the May 18, 2026 shooting at the Islamic Center of San Diego, arguing media outlets (citing CBS News) mislabeled the attackers as NVE-motivated, when their manifesto reportedly mentions "nihilism" only once (as something to oppose) and instead repeatedly self-identifies with neo-Nazi accelerationism.
- **Policy consequence:** He argues mislabeling groups like 764 as counterterrorism threats misdirects analyst resources and may inadvertently give perpetrators "terrorist" status/notoriety they seek, while also diluting analysis of genuine DVE threats.

He suggests alternative terms analysts have used ("misanthropic," "nihilistic violent communities," "sadistic online exploitation") but argues the better fix is disaggregating NVE into more precise, case-by-case classifications.
