(U) In an interview discussing the U.S.-hosted **"Ministerial on the Resurgence of Political Terrorism,"** ICCT Director Thomas Renard argued that the summit—focused heavily on far-left political violence and anarchist movements—illustrates how counter-terrorism frameworks have become deeply politicized and instrumentalized.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Politicization of Counter-Terrorism:** Renard pointed out that the event highlights a trend where counter-terrorism policies are weaponized for political narratives. He noted that far-right terrorism has virtually disappeared from the current U.S. counter-terrorism strategy, despite remaining strongly rooted and active within the country.
- **International Discomfort and Diplomatic Signaling:** European nations caught in the middle of this framing have responded cautiously. Unwilling to directly antagonize Washington, several European governments signaled their discomfort with the summit's narrow focus by sending only junior ministers to attend.
- **Broader Ideological Blind Spots:** The focus on left-wing extremism to the exclusion of other major domestic threats creates structural blind spots in addressing the full spectrum of modern political violence and radicalization trends.
