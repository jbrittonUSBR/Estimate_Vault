(U) The piece argues that “civil terrorism”—lawless acts such as vandalism, arson, nighttime demonstrations, and assaults intended to intimidate elected officials and suppress political opposition—poses a serious threat to American democracy. Fortgang highlights Rep. Adam Smith (D-WA), ranking Democrat on the House Armed Services Committee, who stated that threats and violence against his family and home (vandalism, a fire in his driveway, disrupted neighbors, shut-down town halls, and an assaulted staffer) influenced his vote to cut U.S. aid to Israel. The author calls this a form of successful coercion by anti-Israel activists. He criticizes both Smith’s capitulation (which he says only encourages further intimidation) and the failure of institutions and law enforcement to treat such targeted acts as more serious than ordinary misdemeanors. Fortgang urges states to follow Utah’s example by elevating crimes committed with the intent to intimidate or coerce into felonies, and for the federal government to consider parallel measures.
