(U) Recent Europol-led operations targeting **"The Com"**—a decentralized, nihilistic online extremist network—highlight a profound mismatch between modern digital threats and traditional, outdated national security and counter-terrorism frameworks.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Nature of "The Com":** Unlike traditional hierarchical extremist groups with clear political or religious manifestos, The Com is a loose, borderless digital ecosystem (including sub-groups like _764_ and _CVLT_) driven by pure nihilism, misanthropy, and the "gamification of harm."
- **The Tactics:** Members recruit and groom minors and vulnerable youth on mainstream social media, gaming platforms, and chat apps, coercing victims through extortion and blackmail into committing violent assaults, self-harm, animal torture, and producing child sexual abuse material.
- **Law Enforcement Actions:** Europol and international partner agencies successfully carried out massive referral action days, taking down thousands of URLs and web domains linked to the network's propaganda and violent content.
- **Outdated Security Policies:** The article argues that state security policies are struggling to adapt. Because these networks blur the lines between cybercrime, youth delinquency, sexual exploitation, and violent extremism, traditional bureaucratic counter-terrorism structures often fail to classify, predict, or effectively prosecute these fluid, decentralized threats.
