(U) BANGOR, Maine — A Fairfield man accused of [shooting at three CMP substations](https://www.wmtw.com/article/maine-man-shoots-at-cmp-substations-court-appearance/70397206) earlier this year appeared in federal court in Bangor Tuesday. Thomas Welch, 54, made his first appearance on one count of destruction of an energy facility. If convicted, he faces a maximum sentence of 20 years in prison and fines of up to $250,000. Welch will stay in federal custody for now, though his attorney said they are working on a potential release plan to present to the judge. Welch has not been indicted, so he did not enter a plea Tuesday. Recently-filed court documents reveal more details about what officials say happened in February. According to the FBI, starting just after midnight until after 4 a.m. on Feb. 15, Welch shot up three CMP substations in Windsor, Winslow and Augusta. He also told a friend he planned to target a substation in Albion, but it was too close to houses and he didn’t want to hurt anyone. According to CMP, the shootings caused several million dollars in “substantial” damages. While no motive is mentioned, the FBI notes Welch’s Facebook page contained posts referring to “civil disobedience” and his displeasure with the “Corrupt State and Federal Government.”
