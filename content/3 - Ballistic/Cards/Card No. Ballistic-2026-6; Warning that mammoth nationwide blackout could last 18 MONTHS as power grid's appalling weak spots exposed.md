(U) The United States power grid faces a severe structural vulnerability, with experts warning that a coordinated attack on a small number of critical nodes could trigger a cascading, nationwide blackout lasting 18 months or longer.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Core Vulnerability:** Drawing on analysis highlighted by former Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) Chairman Jon Wellinghoff, models reveal that disabling just **nine critical high-voltage substations** (roughly three in each major U.S. interconnection) could collapse the entire continental power grid.
- **The Origin of Warnings:** The renewed scrutiny stems back to lessons learned from the unresolved 2013 sniper attack on the Metcalf substation near San Jose, California, which exposed how easily physical infrastructure can be targeted.
- **Why an 18-Month Recovery:** A blackout of this scale would heavily damage custom-built large power transformers. Because these massive units have extremely long manufacturing lead times and limited domestic production capacity, replacing them under crisis conditions could take well over a year.
- **Aging Infrastructure:** Reports emphasize that a vast majority of the country's distribution and large power transformers have exceeded or are rapidly approaching the limits of their projected operational lifespans.
