# USBR Brief 2026-11-19

DATAMINR ALERT REPORT

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Dataminr alerts indicate a dispersed set of reservoir and dam related incidents across the United States, led by utility and public health disruptions in Mississippi, Ohio, and Texas, alongside one cyber vulnerability affecting a reservoir safety supervision platform and one unrelated municipal service complaint in New York.¹˒²˒³˒⁴˒⁵˒⁶ Immediate significance is limited by sparse corroboration, but the reporting points to multiple infrastructure adjacent issues that could affect water safety, power continuity, and monitoring resilience.¹˒²˒³˒⁴˒⁶

## DETAIL

A boil water notice was issued for several subdivisions near Ross Barnett Reservoir in Mississippi, indicating a direct public health disruption affecting communities tied to reservoir infrastructure.¹ Power outages were also reported at Greenwood Lake Dam in Delaware, Ohio, and on the 110 block of Beachcombers Loop in Buchanan Dam, Texas, pointing to separate electricity disruptions in dam adjacent areas.²˒³

A fire was detected near West Scott Frost Reservoir Number 1 Levee in Dayton, Texas, adding a potential physical hazard near reservoir infrastructure in a different part of the state.⁴ The available reporting does not indicate whether the fire affected reservoir operations or nearby utilities.⁴

Separately, NVD reported that the Tangshan Pingsheng Reservoir Safety Supervision Platform contains an unauthenticated SQL injection vulnerability identified as CVE-2022-51034, highlighting a cyber exposure involving reservoir related supervisory technology.⁵ The reporting does not provide evidence of exploitation or any connection between this vulnerability and the U.S. incidents in this alert set.⁵

One additional report concerns a missed collection complaint at 3280 Reservoir Oval East in the Bronx, New York.⁶ This appears to be a routine municipal service issue associated with a street name rather than a reservoir infrastructure incident.⁶

## IMPACT

The most operationally relevant shift is the combination of a boil water notice near Ross Barnett Reservoir and separate reported power outages near Greenwood Lake Dam and Buchanan Dam, which together suggest localized service instability across multiple water adjacent communities.¹˒²˒³ The fire detected near West Scott Frost Reservoir Number 1 Levee raises the possibility of an additional physical risk to nearby infrastructure in Texas, although the current reporting is insufficient to determine operational effects.⁴ The reported SQL injection vulnerability in a reservoir safety supervision platform underscores that water management systems can face cyber exposure even when no exploitation is reported.⁵

Taken together, these events support near term decisions to verify utility status, public safety messaging, and any protective measures around affected reservoir and dam sites. It is likely that most of the incidents are isolated and local rather than part of a common operational pattern, because the reporting spans different states, incident types, and source channels. The New York sanitation complaint does not materially change the infrastructure picture presented by the other alerts.⁶

Outlook will depend on whether follow on reporting shows restoration timelines for the power outages, expansion or cancellation of the Mississippi boil water notice, and any indication that the Texas fire spread toward levee or reservoir assets. Additional reporting of exploitation activity, vendor mitigation, or operator advisories would clarify whether the cited cyber vulnerability has practical implications beyond a known technical exposure.
