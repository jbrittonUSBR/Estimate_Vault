# USBR Brief 2026-11-12

DATAMINR ALERT REPORT

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Dataminr alerts indicate a dispersed set of reservoir and dam related developments across the United States, led by an URGENT weather incident near Brier Creek Reservoir, North Carolina, alongside prescribed fire activity near Rampart Reservoir, Colorado, reported power outages at dams in California and Georgia, and infrastructure management concerns in Nebraska and Arizona. These reports point to a mixed operating picture of immediate local hazards and longer-term water infrastructure issues with potential implications for safety, access, and resource management.⁵

DETAIL

An URGENT alert reported a tornado confirmed near Brier Creek Reservoir in North Carolina, marking the most acute immediate hazard in the reporting set.⁵

A separate URGENT thread in Colorado indicated that smoke would result from a prescribed burn to be conducted by the US Forest Service north of Rampart Reservoir, and later reporting said a prescribed burn was scheduled for Rampart Reservoir.⁴˒²

Elsewhere, two alerts reported power outages at dam sites, one at Meiners Oaks 768 Dam in Ojai, California, and another at Lanceford Lake Dam in Ellaville, Georgia.³˒⁹

Additional localized incidents near dam infrastructure included a crash reported at the intersection of CA-128 and Monticello Dam Road in Yolo County, California, and an altercation reported at Cascade Village on the 4600 block of Shasta Dam Boulevard in Shasta Lake, California.⁶˒⁸

Longer-horizon infrastructure and water management issues also appeared in the reporting, with officials considering the future of a Valentine dam because sediment is threatening a Nebraska fish production reservoir.¹

Separate reporting from Arizona said Glen Canyon Dam executive director Balken advocated concentrating storage in Lake Mead and phasing out Lake Powell in Page.⁷

IMPACT

The reporting suggests the most immediate operational concern is short-notice public safety risk near Brier Creek Reservoir, where a tornado was confirmed, and near Rampart Reservoir, where prescribed burn activity and associated smoke could affect visibility, access, and local operations.⁵˒⁴˒²

Reported power outages at dam sites in California and Georgia may indicate localized service disruptions, but the available reporting does not establish any broader infrastructure failure or direct effect on dam integrity.³˒⁹

The California crash and altercation reports appear localized and do not by themselves indicate a wider threat to nearby water infrastructure, though they could complicate access or emergency response in those immediate areas.⁶˒⁸

The Nebraska sediment issue points to a slower-moving infrastructure and resource management problem tied to reservoir function, while the Arizona report reflects ongoing debate over long-term basin storage strategy rather than an acute incident.¹˒⁷

Taken together, the reporting supports near-term monitoring of weather and fire related disruptions, while also highlighting persistent governance and maintenance pressures around water infrastructure. Outlook should focus on any follow-on reports of damage, closures, evacuations, or operational interruptions near Brier Creek Reservoir and Rampart Reservoir. It will also be important to watch for utility restoration updates at the affected dam sites and for any official decisions on the Valentine dam or broader storage policy changes tied to Glen Canyon Dam.
