# USBR Brief 2026-11-13

DATAMINR ALERT REPORT

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

BLUF: Dataminr alerts indicate a dispersed set of U.S. reservoir and dam related developments on 12 to 13 November, led by an URGENT prescribed burn notice north of Rampart Reservoir, Colorado, alongside confirmed tornado reporting near Brier Creek Reservoir, North Carolina, multiple fire detections near dam or levee infrastructure in Arizona, Texas, and Minnesota, and several lower priority infrastructure and maintenance updates with potential local operational relevance.¹

DETAIL

The highest priority reporting concerns Rampart Reservoir, Colorado, where emergency responder reporting said smoke from a prescribed burn to be conducted by the US Forest Service would be visible north of the reservoir.¹ A subsequent responder notice said a prescribed burn was scheduled for Rampart Reservoir, reinforcing that smoke or fire signatures in the area may be planned rather than incidental.²

In North Carolina, government reporting confirmed a tornado near Brier Creek Reservoir, and later local reporting said the National Weather Service confirmed that an EF-0 tornado touched down in the Brier Creek Reservoir area of Wake County on October 11.³˒⁴ Together, these reports indicate official confirmation of a tornado event affecting the reservoir area, though the later report frames it as retrospective confirmation rather than a developing incident.³˒⁴

Separate sensor detections identified fires near several dam or levee sites across multiple states on 12 November.⁵˒⁶˒⁷ NASA FIRMS detected fire near Inspiration Tailings Dam Number One in Miami, Arizona, near Amoco Chemicals Reservoir Levee in Alvin, Texas, and near Green Meadow Group Pond Dam in Gary, Minnesota.⁵˒⁶˒⁷ The alerts do not specify fire size, cause, damage, or whether the detections directly affected the structures themselves.⁵˒⁶˒⁷

Other lower priority infrastructure reporting points to routine works and localized disruption rather than clear emergency conditions.⁸˒⁹˒¹⁰ A blog report said a power outage was reported at Meiners Oaks 768 Dam in Ojai, California, local reporting said test borings will begin on November 16 at New Croton Dam in Croton-On-Hudson, New York, and Nebraska officials were reported to be considering the future of a Valentine dam because sediment is threatening a fish production reservoir.⁸˒⁹˒¹⁰

IMPACT

The reporting suggests the most immediate operational consideration is distinguishing planned fire activity from potentially hazardous fire detections near water control infrastructure.¹²⁵˒⁶˒⁷ In Colorado, the prescribed burn notices reduce the likelihood that visible smoke near Rampart Reservoir alone signals an unplanned incident, but they also raise the chance of public reporting, temporary access concerns, or resource inquiries tied to smoke conditions.¹˒²

The North Carolina tornado reporting is significant because it confirms severe weather has affected the Brier Creek Reservoir area, which may warrant review of any localized damage, debris, or downstream maintenance needs if not already completed.³˒⁴ The sensor based fire detections in Arizona, Texas, and Minnesota merit verification because the alerts place heat signatures near dam or levee infrastructure but do not establish whether the facilities themselves are threatened.⁵˒⁶˒⁷ The California outage report may support local checks on power dependent systems at Meiners Oaks 768 Dam, although the available reporting is limited to a single blog sourced alert.⁸

The New York and Nebraska items point to longer horizon infrastructure management issues rather than acute crisis conditions.⁹˒¹⁰ If sustained, sediment concerns in Nebraska could affect reservoir utility and future dam decisions, while planned borings at New Croton Dam indicate ongoing inspection or project activity that may affect site access or nearby operations.⁹˒¹⁰

Outlook should focus on whether follow on reporting confirms active wildfire spread or structural exposure at the Arizona, Texas, or Minnesota sites, whether any local agencies report damage or service disruption tied to the North Carolina tornado area, and whether additional notices expand the scope or duration of the prescribed burn activity in Colorado. Confirmation of evacuations, closures, utility interruptions, or engineering advisories would indicate a shift from localized monitoring to a more consequential infrastructure incident.
