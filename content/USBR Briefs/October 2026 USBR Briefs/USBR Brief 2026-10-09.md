# USBR Brief 2026-10-09

DATAMINR ALERT REPORT

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Bottom line up front: Dataminr reporting indicates a dispersed set of water and dam related incidents across the western and central United States, led by an URGENT fire hotspot detection near Rampart Reservoir Trail in Colorado Springs, alongside additional fire activity near a tailings dam in Arizona, a reported power outage at a Missouri tailings dam, an unspecified incident at a reservoir dam in California, and a Utah water transfer update with potential resource management relevance.¹˒²˒³˒⁴˒⁵˒⁶

## DETAIL

The highest priority development is an URGENT sensor detection of seven fire hotspots near Rampart Reservoir Trail in Colorado Springs, Colorado.¹ Separate local reporting stated that smoke visible in the area was rising from a prescribed burn at Rampart Reservoir, indicating the hotspot detections may be linked to planned fire activity rather than an uncontrolled incident.²

Additional fire related activity was detected near another water infrastructure site in Arizona, where sensors identified five fire hotspots near Inspiration Tailings Dam Number One in Miami.³ The available reporting does not provide corroborating context on cause, containment status, or any effect on the tailings facility itself.³

Elsewhere, a blog post on Citizen reported a power outage at Cadet Mine Tailings Dam in Cadet, Missouri.⁴ A separate Citizen post reported an incident at Greystone Reservoir 1061 Dam in Beverly Hills, California, but did not specify the nature or severity of that incident in the alert text.⁵

In Utah, Governor Cox reported that nearly 10,000 acre feet of water from Pineview Reservoir is now going to the Great Salt Lake through voluntary leases and partner commitments.⁶ This update points to ongoing water management actions rather than an acute infrastructure disruption.⁶

## IMPACT

The reporting points to a mixed operating picture in which multiple water adjacent or dam adjacent sites are generating alerts, but only one event, the Colorado hotspot detection, carries elevated priority and it is partially contextualized by local reporting of a prescribed burn.¹˒² This likely reduces the immediate concern of a major uncontrolled fire at Rampart Reservoir, though continued monitoring is warranted until there is clearer confirmation that all detected hotspots remain within planned burn parameters.¹˒²

The Arizona hotspot detection near Inspiration Tailings Dam Number One may merit closer review because the alert places fire activity near a tailings facility and provides no additional context on operational effects or protective actions.³ The Missouri and California items are more ambiguous, with one reporting a power outage at a tailings dam and the other citing an unspecified incident at a reservoir dam, so they support heightened awareness but not firm conclusions about infrastructure degradation or public safety impacts based on the available information.⁴˒⁵

The Utah update suggests that water resource decisions remain active at the state level and may affect downstream planning discussions tied to reservoir releases and lake support measures.⁶ More broadly, the current picture supports continued monitoring of fire detections near critical water infrastructure, verification of whether reported utility disruptions affect dam operations, and rapid clarification of vague incident reporting before it drives unnecessary escalation.

Near term, the key indicators to watch are follow on local or official reporting that confirms whether the Colorado and Arizona fire detections are controlled, expanding, or affecting infrastructure operations. Additional specificity on the Missouri outage and the California incident would help determine whether these remain isolated reports or signal a wider pattern of operational stress at water related sites.
