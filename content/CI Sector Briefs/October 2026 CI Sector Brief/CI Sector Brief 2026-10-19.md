In Guadalajara, protest activity around water issues escalated into direct attacks on government facilities, with reports that protesters attempted to enter the State Congress building, removed barricades, knocked down access points, broke doors and lighting, and set fires at entrances and through windows.¹˒²˒³˒⁴˒⁵˒⁶˒⁷ Multiple reports also indicate clashes with police, injuries among both protesters and officers, and a growing emergency support posture that included a volunteer first aid station for the broader clean water march.⁸˒⁹˒¹⁰˒¹¹ Government reporting further stated that five people were arrested for allegedly setting earlier fires to the doors of the State Congress building while clashes remained ongoing.¹²

Water and sanitation stress was also reported elsewhere in Mexico, reinforcing the salience of utility related grievances. A technical failure prompted a water outage in the Carrizal II area of Tabasco, while a drinking water shortage led to the closure of the Fierro Bridge in Tehuantepec, Oaxaca.¹³˒¹⁴ In Jalisco state, a water outage was reported in the Zimalta area of San Pedro Tlaquepaque and a sewage leak was reported in the Revolucion area of the same municipality.¹⁵˒¹⁶ In Morelos, local reporting said only 33 of 134 wastewater treatment plants were operating properly.¹⁷
