(TLP: CLEAR) During a routine underwater inspection at the Big Creek Lake dam, the primary source of drinking water for Mobile County, contracted divers discovered what appeared to be a grenade near the dam’s gate. The dam, along with its associated infrastructure, is owned and managed by the Mobile Area Water and Sewer System (MAWSS). Upon finding the device, the divers marked its location, took photographs, and promptly notified MAWSS representatives. MAWSS immediately contacted the Mobile County Sheriff’s Office, which then reached out to the Mobile Police Department (MPD) due to their dive-certified bomb technicians. MPD, joined by the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA) and FBI bomb techs, responded to the scene. After entering the water, they confirmed the device was indeed a grenade but could not determine if it was live or inert. The team carefully rigged the grenade to a transit buoy, raised it to a shallow depth, and towed it to a safe beaching site. Using remote methods, they pulled the grenade onto land and conducted a controlled detonation, which confirmed the device was live. Following the incident, all responding agencies agreed the grenade was most likely homemade and believed it had been placed by locals, possibly as an attempt to catch fish by detonation, rather than as an act of terrorism. The device was found approximately 25 feet from the dam’s gate on the spillway.
