(U) Federal prosecutors have indicted two Pennsylvania teens accused of attempting an ISIS-inspired bombing outside New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s residence during a March protest. Authorities say the pair brought improvised explosive devices containing volatile TATP, discussed plans to kill up to 60 people, and left behind a notebook detailing attack options. Evidence also included dashcam recordings, bomb-making materials, and statements of allegiance to ISIS. An eight-count federal indictment charges Emir Balat and Ibrahim Kayumi with attempting to provide material support to ISIS, using a weapon of mass destruction, and other terrorism-related offenses. Prosecutors allege the two traveled from Pennsylvania to Manhattan with improvised explosive devices (IEDs) during a March 7 protest outside Gracie Mansion. The charges carry potential sentences of 10 years to life in prison, underscoring the severity of the alleged plot. CBS Philadelphia + 2

- Balat and Kayumi discussed targeting up to 60 people to 'start terror' during dashcam recordings.
- A notebook outlined alternative vehicle attacks at festivals, parades, and celebrations.
- Investigators found a storage unit in Pennsylvania with bomb-making materials rented by Balat days before the attack.

(U) The indictment reveals prosecutors' view that the suspects' actions were part of a meticulously planned ISIS-inspired attack, rather than a spontaneous act of violence. Statements from law enforcement highlight the alleged intent to maximize casualties, with one suspect claiming he hoped to exceed the Boston Marathon bombing’s death toll. This framing could influence both jury perception and the scope of counterterrorism policy discussions. 

(U) Investigators recovered dashcam footage from the suspects’ vehicle in which they discussed killing up to 60 people and targeting both civilians and government. A notebook with alternative attack plans, including vehicle ramming at festivals, was also found, along with bomb-making materials in a Pennsylvania storage unit. Forensic analysis confirmed the presence of TATP, a volatile explosive nicknamed the 'Mother of Satan,' in at least one device. 

(U) The March 7 incident began during dueling protests outside Mayor Mamdani’s residence, where tensions escalated after pepper spray was used. Authorities allege Balat ignited and threw two explosive devices, one obtained from Kayumi, before both were apprehended on site. A third suspicious device was found the next day in a nearby vehicle, adding to the evidence of premeditation. Suspects’ ISIS motivation and statements:

- Kayumi told a bystander he acted in the name of ISIS when arrested.
- Balat wrote a statement declaring allegiance to the Islamic State.
- Balat expressed desire to carry out an attack bigger than the Boston Marathon bombing.
