(U) **‘The system failed him’: Family identifies man who drove into Multnomah Athletic Club** (OPB, Joni Auden Land, Conrad Wilson, and Courtney Sherwood; May 3, 2026, updated May 4) Family members and Portland police identified the man who drove an explosives-laden vehicle into the Multnomah Athletic Club (MAC) in Portland, Oregon, early on Saturday as Bruce Valentine Whitman, approximately 48–49 years old.

(U) Whitman, a former bartender at the exclusive club who was fired more than a decade earlier for cause after repeated aggressive behavior, harbored a long-standing grudge against his former employer. He drove a rented vehicle through the front glass entrance around 3 a.m., then drove around the ground floor attempting to detonate explosives before dying in the vehicle as it caught fire. No other people were injured. Investigators recovered about 20 propane tanks (none detonated) and roughly 10 improvised explosive devices (some partially detonated); robots were used to handle remaining devices. Receipts for bomb components were found at his home. Police said he acted alone and the incident was not terrorism.

(U) Whitman’s mother, Rita Lenzer, told OPB that her son’s mental health deteriorated after a motorcycle accident years earlier that caused severe brain injuries. In February he attempted suicide, was admitted to the Unity Center in Portland for several weeks, and was diagnosed with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia. His cousin, Ric Loza, said: “He should be locked up right now and getting care. But the system failed him and luckily didn’t fail somebody else with their life because of his actions.” Lenzer noted the difficulty of understanding mental illness and that her son fixated on certain things, with “so much hate there.”

(U) Family members also described Whitman as someone who kept a food pantry outside his North Portland home, fed homeless people, and sometimes allowed them to stay. Multnomah County District Attorney Nathan Vasquez later commented on the case as a reminder of the public-safety consequences of untreated mental illness and the need for sustained treatment resources.
