(U) **Explosion outside immigration court disrupts lower Manhattan** (Axios, Josephine Walker, updated July 20, 2026) A man was arrested Monday after setting off fireworks and starting a small fire outside 26 Federal Plaza in New York City, a federal complex that houses an immigration court, ICE’s New York field office, and other agencies. According to an FBI spokesperson, around 9 a.m. a man poured liquid on the ground, placed fireworks on top, and ignited them, causing a small fire. The FBI New York Joint Terrorism Task Force is investigating. One federal employee and one civilian were injured; both were treated and released.

(U) The suspect was identified as 43-year-old Andrew Arrabaca, a former U.S. Army mechanic (2001–2005) who worked on Patriot missile systems. FBI Assistant Director James Barnacle said Arrabaca “came armed with bad intentions” and “was looking to injure people.” Investigators recovered two axes, a hammer, a machete, three fixed-blade knives, six incendiary devices/fireworks, and other items. Arrabaca was pushing a cart with anti-ICE literature and a sign reading “ICE off our streets,” and shouted profanities at ICE officers during his arrest. Barnacle described him as an “anti-American, anti-government extremist.” Local reporting indicated a history of anti-ICE activism and mental health issues. As of the afternoon of July 20, he had not been charged.

(U) The incident closed roads, disrupted transit, and prompted a large emergency response. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani called the event “deeply disturbing,” expressed relief that no one was seriously injured, and said the city would support the federal investigation. The building’s ICE holding center has previously drawn criticism over crowded and unhealthy conditions and has been a focus of immigration enforcement debates.
