(U) A series of vehicle and car bomb attacks rattled Colombia during the political transition surrounding the inauguration of right-wing President Abelardo de la Espriella. The violence highlighted the intense security challenges facing the new administration as it moves away from previous peace negotiation frameworks.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Highway Attack:** A car bomb destroyed a toll booth on the Pan-American Highway in southwest Colombia (Cauca department), resulting in minor injuries to two security guards. Witnesses reported the vehicle was abandoned before the blast.
- **Attributed Culprits:** Colombia's military attributed the highway bombing to dissident factions of the former FARC rebel group that rejected the 2016 peace agreement.
- **Pre-Inauguration Blasts:** The unrest followed an earlier explosive-laden truck attack outside a police station in Cúcuta near the Venezuelan border that injured 11 people, underscoring a broader wave of pre-inauguration security threats.
- **Shift in Leadership Approach:** President de la Espriella succeeded former leftist President Gustavo Petro, taking a hardline stance that rejects prior dialogue-heavy policies in favor of intensified crackdowns on narco-terrorism and stronger security alliances with international partners like the United States.
