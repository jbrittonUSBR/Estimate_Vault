(U)A German court convicted a Ukrainian man for his involvement in a parcel tracking plot linked to Russian intelligence, which prosecutors described as part of an ongoing campaign of European sabotage.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Ruling:** The Higher Regional Court in Stuttgart sentenced a 30-year-old Ukrainian man to one year and three months in prison for acting as an agent for the purposes of sabotage. Because of time served in pre-trial detention, he was considered to have completed his sentence. Two co-defendants were acquitted due to insufficient evidence that they knowingly participated.
- **The Plot:** Prosecutors established that the defendants shipped packages containing GPS trackers and spare auto parts from Germany to Ukraine via a commercial parcel service. The court found the operation was orchestrated by a Russian state entity—acting through intermediaries in occupied Mariupol—to scout logistics routes and scope out vulnerabilities for potential future attacks.
- **Broader Hybrid Warfare Context:** European security officials have repeatedly accused Moscow of waging a sustained "hybrid war" involving sabotage, arson, and mysterious parcel incidents across countries supporting Ukraine. Russia has consistently denied these allegations, dismissing them as groundless.
