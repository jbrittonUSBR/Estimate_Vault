(U) WASHINGTON, D.C. — The Department of Homeland Security [announced](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/04/03/dhs-reveals-suspects-connected-explosive-device-macdill-air-force-base-are-children) Friday that federal agents arrested the parents of two siblings accused in an attempted bombing at MacDill Air Force Base, revealing the couple had remained in the U.S. illegally for nearly 30 years. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, or ICE, took into custody Qiu Qin Zou and Jia Zhang Zheng on March 18. According to DHS, the Chinese nationals illegally entered the U.S. in the 1990s and were ordered removed by an immigration judge in 1998 after their asylum claims were denied. Despite multiple failed appeals, the pair illegally remained in the country for decades. The arrests have thrust the case into the center of a high-stakes constitutional battle over birthright citizenship. The couple’s children, Ann Mary Zheng and Alen Zheng, were born in the U.S. during the period their parents were evading deportation orders. “Automatically granting citizenship to children of illegal aliens born in the U.S. is based on a historically inaccurate interpretation of the Citizenship Clause and poses a major national security risk,” Acting Assistant Secretary Lauren Bis said. “This incident underscores the severe national security threat that illegal immigration and birthright citizenship pose to the United States.” The siblings are connected to a March 10 incident at the visitor’s center of the Tampa-based Air Force facility, which serves as the headquarters for U.S. Central Command. Federal prosecutors charged Ann Mary Zheng with acting as an accessory after the fact and tampering with evidence, alleging she helped her brother, Alen Zheng, after he placed an improvised explosive device at the base. Ann Mary Zheng was arrested by federal authorities upon returning from China, where investigators believe her brother remains. The DHS announcement comes as the Supreme Court prepares to hear a challenge to President Donald Trump’s “[Protecting the Meaning and Value of Citizenship](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/protecting-the-meaning-and-value-of-american-citizenship/)” executive order. The administration argues that the 14th Amendment’s citizenship provisions were intended for the children of former slaves following the Civil War and have been misapplied to the children of those in the country illegally. “The privilege of United States citizenship is a priceless and profound gift,” Trump said in the order. Administration officials contend the MacDill plot illustrates how the current interpretation of the law can be “abused” and create domestic vulnerabilities. Under the direction of DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, the department said it will continue prioritizing an immigration system that “puts the American people first.”
