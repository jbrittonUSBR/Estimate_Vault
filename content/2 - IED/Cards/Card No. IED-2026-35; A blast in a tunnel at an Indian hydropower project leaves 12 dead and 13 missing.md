(U) A suspected methane gas explosion on July 20 collapsed part of an under-construction tunnel at the 500-megawatt Teesta Stage-VI hydropower project in Samardung village, Sikkim (northeastern India, near the Chinese border). At least 12 workers were killed and 13 remained missing at the time of the report. Workers (initially reported as about 21–27 people inside) heard a loud blast before debris and toxic gases filled the tunnel; two escaped. Some officials who later entered to help also became trapped. Rescuers from the National Disaster Response Force faced toxic gases (methane, carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulfide) and hazardous conditions that limited entry time. State Chief Minister Prem Singh Tamang said the blast was believed caused by a methane buildup.
