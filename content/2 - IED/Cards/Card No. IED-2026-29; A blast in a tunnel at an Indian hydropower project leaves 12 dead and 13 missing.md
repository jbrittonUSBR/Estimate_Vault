(U) **A blast in a tunnel at an Indian hydropower project leaves 12 dead and 13 missing** (AP News, Wasbir Hussain, updated July 21, 2026) A suspected gas explosion collapsed part of a tunnel under construction at a hydropower project in India’s northeastern Himalayan state of Sikkim, near the Chinese border, leaving at least 12 people dead and 13 missing, officials said Tuesday. The blast occurred Monday while 21 laborers were working inside the tunnel on the 500-megawatt Teesta hydroelectric project in Samardung village. Two workers escaped. Six power utility officials later entered to help with rescue efforts but became trapped themselves. State Chief Minister Prem Singh Tamang said the explosion was believed to have been triggered by a buildup of methane. Survivors reported hearing a loud explosion before debris collapsed into the tunnel.

(U) By Tuesday, rescuers had recovered 12 bodies. National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) teams, equipped with oxygen gear, were racing to reach the remaining 13 people believed still trapped. Toxic gases including methane, carbon monoxide, and hydrogen sulfide were detected, limiting rescue shifts to about 15 minutes at a time. Construction accidents are relatively common in India amid rapid infrastructure development, fragile geology, and safety challenges. The Teesta River basin is seismically active with young, fractured rock formations that can trap gases. A geology expert noted that methane and other gases may have accumulated and triggered the blast. Similar past incidents include a 2023 Uttarakhand tunnel collapse and a February coal mine explosion in neighboring Meghalaya.
