(U) **What is Pickaxe Mountain? Trump says US will hit Iranian nuclear site 'pretty soon'** (ABC News, Chris Boccia, July 21, 2026) President Donald Trump said Tuesday that the United States would target Pickaxe Mountain (also known as Kūh-e Kolang or Mount Kolang), an under-construction Iranian nuclear facility buried deep underground, “pretty soon, and very heavily.” The comments came amid ongoing escalation and 10 straight days of strikes.

**About the site**:
- Located only miles from Natanz (one of three nuclear sites hit in a June 2025 U.S.-Israeli operation).
- Declared by Iran in 2021 as a centrifuge assembly site after a similar plant at Natanz was destroyed.
- Analysts at the Institute for Science and International Security (ISIS), reviewing satellite imagery, say construction continues; the facility is buried about 100 meters underground, and it is unclear when it could become operational.
- The Wall Street Journal reported that Israeli intelligence assessed Iran moved centrifuges (which can enrich uranium) to the site last fall. Trump did not deny the report but said the U.S. does not have it “on record” and that centrifuges matter only if Iran has the material. He emphasized that the U.S. “follow[s] the material,” referring to Iran’s stock of highly enriched uranium (reportedly close to 1,000 pounds of near weapons-grade material).

**Context and expert views**:
- IAEA inspectors have been barred from Iran since earlier U.S. attacks this year, leaving major uncertainties about Iran’s nuclear capabilities.
- Experts note the site was spared in prior strikes, suggesting it was not viewed as an imminent threat. Kelsey Davenport (Arms Control Association) said the depth would make destruction difficult even with a massive ordnance penetrator (MOP), with “no guarantee” of success. ISIS assessed the site in its present condition would be more suitable for ground forces or sabotage; collapsing entrances could hinder construction and provide visible early warning of rebuilding efforts.
- Trump also said there was evidence Iran was trying to reconstitute a nuclear site and that any such site would be hit “very, very powerfully.” Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth expressed confidence in the U.S. military’s ability to reach any target.

(U) Uncertainty remains about how many centrifuges survived earlier strikes and how quickly Iran could reconstitute its program, underscoring calls for resumed IAEA inspections.
