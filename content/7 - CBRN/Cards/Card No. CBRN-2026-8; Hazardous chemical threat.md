(U) Residents in Boyle Heights and Garden Grove are demanding greater accountability and regulatory reform after a June 17 warehouse fire at a Lineage cold storage facility in Los Angeles involving over 12,000 pounds of anhydrous ammonia and a separate incident at GKN Aerospace in Garden Grove raising fears of a methyl methacrylate explosion or leak that prompted the evacuation of 50,000 people. Both facilities had prior safety and emissions violations yet continued operating under federal and state oversight, with incomplete records and gaps in regulating reactive chemicals contributing to concerns that a catastrophe was narrowly avoided. Air monitoring in Boyle Heights later detected hazardous pollutants, while local leaders and environmental advocates highlighted the disproportionate impact on already burdened communities and the need for better transparency, emergency planning, and stronger local authority over industrial sites. State Sen. Tom Umberg introduced Senate Bill 883 to create a statewide inventory of such facilities, add methyl methacrylate to risk management programs, and update environmental reviews, arguing that lessons from these incidents must prevent future tragedies amid ongoing questions about oversight and cumulative risks from clustered warehouses.
