(U) Retired Adm. Dennis Blair, former director of national intelligence, argued during an October 2022 talk at UNC-Chapel Hill that nuclear war in Ukraine remains unlikely despite Russian President Vladimir Putin’s threats to use “all available means” to defend annexed territories. Blair viewed Putin’s emphatic statements, including “I’m not bluffing,” as indicators of bluffing rather than genuine intent, noting no plausible scenario in which nuclear use would advance Russia’s geopolitical objectives. He outlined potential U.S. responses to any Russian tactical nuclear strike—including ramped-up military aid with advisers and long-range weapons, diplomatic and economic pressure, non-nuclear strikes on accessible Russian forces such as the navy in Crimea, and possibly a limited retaliatory nuclear strike on a Russian military installation—while emphasizing the high psychological threshold for nuclear use and the uncontrollable nature of escalation that would leave no winners. Blair stressed that large-scale war, let alone nuclear conflict, was not imminent and highlighted the instinctive reluctance across nations to cross the nuclear threshold due to the devastating historical precedent of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
