# Sudan’s military is accused of using chlorine bombs: here’s how they work and what the law says

[Source](https://theconversation.com/sudans-military-is-accused-of-using-chlorine-bombs-heres-how-they-work-and-what-the-law-says-291620)

(U) LEIDEN, Netherlands — New evidence reinforced persistent allegations of chemical weapons use in Sudan's civil war, with The New York Times and The Washington Post reporting in September 2026 on materials from an anonymous Middle Eastern intelligence agency, according to an analysis by Leiden University scholar Anneleen van der Meer for The Conversation Africa. The article described chlorine as an asphyxiating agent that can cause symptoms from airway irritation to pulmonary oedema, noting it is a crude weapon relatively easy to develop and assemble. It stated that chemical weapons including chlorine have been outlawed since the 1925 Geneva Protocol and the 1993 Chemical Weapons Convention, and that Sudan, a state party, received renewed US sanctions in 2025 over the allegations. The piece is an academic expert commentary and analysis, not a news report.
