(U) Public health officials and infectious disease experts warned that California is facing a severe year for the mosquito-borne **West Nile virus**, with a significant spike in early human cases and fatalities compared to previous years.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **Rising Cases & Fatalities:** Human cases of West Nile virus surged early in the season, climbing to nearly triple the five-year average for the same period. The California Department of Public Health (CDPH) reported multiple fatalities tied to the virus.
- **Weather Factors:** Experts attribute the surge to atypical weather patterns, including a warmer winter that allowed more mosquitoes to survive, followed by record-breaking spring temperatures that accelerated both mosquito reproduction and the virus's incubation rate.
- **Spike in Infected Wildlife:** A dramatic increase in virus detection among local wildlife—such as hundreds of infected dead birds—served as an early warning sign of heavy virus circulation across multiple counties.
- **Health Risks & Neuroinvasive Forms:** While about 80% of infected individuals remain asymptomatic and roughly 20% experience mild, flu-like symptoms, roughly 1% develop severe "neuroinvasive" forms of the disease (such as encephalitis or meningitis), which can cause permanent neurological damage or be fatal.
- **Prevention Guidance:** Health experts advise residents to protect themselves by using EPA-registered insect repellents (such as DEET or picaridin), wearing long sleeves during peak mosquito hours (dawn and dusk), and eliminating standing water around homes.
