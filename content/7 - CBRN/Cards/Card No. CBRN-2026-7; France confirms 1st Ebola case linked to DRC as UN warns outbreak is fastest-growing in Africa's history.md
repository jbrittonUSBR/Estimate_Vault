(U) The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, which has spread beyond 1,000 confirmed cases as of mid-June 2026, likely originated in the gold-mining town of Mongbwalu in Ituri province, where a rarer Bundibugyo strain was confirmed on May 15 after mysterious deaths beginning in April. The first suspected case involved a nurse who fell ill on April 24 and was buried in Mongbwalu, followed by rapid deaths among health workers and more than 50 fatalities in the Shuni neighborhood alone by mid-May; the virus has since reached Bunia and neighboring provinces as well as Uganda. Contributing factors include the town’s mobile mining workforce, severe lack of safe water and sanitation (only 20% have safe water access per Oxfam), and community distrust fueled by rumors of supernatural causes or aid workers spreading the disease. Health responders, including Doctors Without Borders, have established treatment centers amid challenges of underreporting, testing delays, and resistance to safe burial protocols, while survivors like hospital bookkeeper Florence Mangembo have urged recognition that “the virus is real” after recovering from infection contracted while caring for family.
