(U) **Burel: Time to take bioweapons seriously** (Boston Herald opinion, Greg Burel, July 20, 2026) Former Strategic National Stockpile director Greg Burel argues that the United States must treat biological weapons and broader CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) threats as urgent national-security priorities rather than distant risks. He notes that threats have evolved beyond historical examples such as anthrax and now include potential AI-enabled bioweapons. The Office of the Director of National Intelligence’s spring Annual Threat Assessment highlighted growing chemical and biological warfare capabilities among adversaries, including Iran’s reported research and development of biological agents and toxins for offensive purposes, and the knowledge/capability retained by China, North Korea, and Russia regarding traditional pathogens and toxins.

(U) Burel contends that despite calls for a “wartime footing,” CBRN readiness remains a weak point. He identifies four key tools that need sustained support: the Pandemic and All-Hazards Preparedness Act (which Congress must reauthorize), the Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority (BARDA), the Administration for Strategic Preparedness and Response, and the Strategic National Stockpile. He urges adequate funding and stockpiling so the U.S. is prepared for a biothreat crisis before it occurs.
