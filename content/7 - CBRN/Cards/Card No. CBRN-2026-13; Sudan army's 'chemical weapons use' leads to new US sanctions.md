(U) **Sudan army's 'chemical weapons use' leads to new US sanctions** (The National, Hamza Hendawi, July 22, 2026) The United States has imposed new sanctions on Sudan’s military-aligned government over the alleged use of chemical weapons in its war against the Rapid Support Forces (RSF). The measures, which took effect Monday, restrict Sudan’s access to U.S. financing and exports and ban state-owned Sudanese airlines from using American airspace. The U.S. State Department determined last month that the Sudanese government used chemical weapons in 2024 in violation of the Chemical Weapons Convention (which Sudan ratified in 1999). The weapons were reportedly used during fighting for control of greater Khartoum, specifically in Bahri (one of the three cities making up the capital region). Residents said the attack targeted an area already evacuated of civilians, leaving primarily army troops, allied militiamen, and RSF fighters. An earlier *New York Times* investigation identified the chemical as chlorine. The State Department statement did not specify the type, timing, or exact location.

(U) Both sides face serious allegations: the Sudanese Armed Forces for indiscriminate shelling and chemical weapons use, and the RSF for ethnically motivated killings, sexual violence, and extrajudicial killings (particularly in Darfur). Army chief Gen. Abdel Fattah Al Burhan and RSF commander Gen. Mohamed Dagalo already face U.S. sanctions related to alleged war crimes. The conflict, now in its fourth year, has left about 20 million people facing hunger, roughly 14 million displaced, and tens of thousands killed, according to UN figures. The army currently controls the capital and northern, eastern, and central regions, while the RSF holds Darfur, parts of Kordofan, and sections of Blue Nile state. Repeated ceasefire efforts have failed, with Al Burhan insisting on continuing the fight until the RSF surrenders or is defeated.
