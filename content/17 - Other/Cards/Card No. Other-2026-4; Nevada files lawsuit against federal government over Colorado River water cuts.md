(U) The state of Nevada, alongside the Colorado River Commission of Nevada and the Southern Nevada Water Authority, filed a federal lawsuit against the U.S. Department of the Interior and Interior Secretary Doug Burgum. The legal action aims to block a newly released federal management plan that mandates severe Colorado River water cuts for Lower Basin states, arguing the framework unfairly targets Nevada.
### **Key Details**

- **The Contested Decision:** The Department of the Interior issued a "record of decision" establishing a framework for Lower Basin water allocations and future operational guidelines for Lake Mead and Lake Powell.
- **Impact on Nevada:** Under the worst-case scenario of the framework, Nevada faces losing up to 71% (or roughly 213,556 acre-feet) of its annual 300,000-acre-foot allocation from the Colorado River—water that almost entirely supports the Las Vegas valley.
- **Disproportionate Burden:** The lawsuit highlights that while Lower Basin states (Nevada, Arizona, and California) face mandatory reductions, the Upper Basin states (Colorado, New Mexico, Utah, and Wyoming) are spared from comparable mandatory cuts.
- **Legal Arguments:** Plaintiffs argue that the federal government violated the Administrative Procedure Act, the National Environmental Policy Act, and the historical "law of the river". They also contend that the Interior Department failed to adequately analyze the devastating economic impacts—such as risks to Southern Nevada's estimated $180 billion economy—before pushing forward with the cuts.
- **Context:** The cuts are a response to a decades-long drought and climate change that have dramatically lowered water levels in the river's critical reservoirs. State officials emphasize that Nevada has already aggressively conserved water, cutting per-capita usage significantly over the last two decades.
