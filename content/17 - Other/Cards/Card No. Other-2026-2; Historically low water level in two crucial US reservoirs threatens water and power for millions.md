(U) The combined water storage in Lake Powell and Lake Mead, the two largest reservoirs on the Colorado River, has fallen to a historic low—the lowest since 1957 (before Lake Powell was fully filled). On July 12, levels broke a previous record set in March 2023. Lake Powell is about 23% full and at its lowest July level on record; Lake Mead is about 27% full and near its all-time low. Experts call it a significant milestone in the Colorado River crisis. The river supplies water to about 40 million people, irrigates more than 5 million acres of farmland, and generates hydropower for hundreds of thousands of homes. Prolonged drought, reduced snowpack, high demand, and climate change have driven the decline. Lake Powell could reach “minimum power pool” by spring 2027, potentially stopping hydropower generation. Negotiations among the seven basin states on new water-sharing rules remain unresolved after a February deadline passed without agreement. Officials warn that even a strong winter snowpack is unlikely to resolve the long-term shortfall.
