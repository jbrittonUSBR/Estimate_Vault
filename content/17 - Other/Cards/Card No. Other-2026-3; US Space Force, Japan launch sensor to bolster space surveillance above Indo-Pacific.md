(U) The U.S. Space Force and Japan successfully launched a **Space Domain Awareness (SDA) payload** aboard Japan's Quasi-Zenith Satellite 7 (QZS-7). Lifted by a Japanese H-3 rocket from the Tanegashima Space Center, this milestone marks the completion of the Quasi-Zenith Satellite System-Hosted Payload (QZSS-HP) program—the first national security space partnership of its kind between the United States and Japan.
### **Key Details of the Mission**

- **Strategic Objective:** The newly deployed sensor will feed near-real-time data into the U.S. Space Surveillance Network, significantly improving the Space Force's capability to track, identify, and characterize objects within the critical geosynchronous orbit regime (roughly 22,000 miles above Earth) over the Indo-Pacific region.
- **Operational Control:** Combat Forces Command’s Mission Delta 2 will operate the U.S. sovereign payload utilizing the Multi-Mission Space Operations Center enclave in Colorado.
- **Design and Development:** Both U.S. payloads utilized in the two-launch program were designed and built by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology’s Lincoln Laboratory (MIT/LL), while Mitsubishi Electric built the Japanese navigation host satellites.
- **Bilateral Milestone:** Stemming from a 2020 agreement between the U.S. Space Force and Japan's National Space Policy Secretariat, the program follows the initial launch of the first sensor aboard QZS-6 in February 2025.
### **Broader Significance**

(U) Military leaders from both nations emphasized that the partnership fortifies shared deterrence capabilities, enhances asset resilience, and expands the U.S.-Japan security alliance directly into a contested space domain.
