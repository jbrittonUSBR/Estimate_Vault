(U) The piece argues that deploying large language models (LLMs) against data collected under warrantless foreign-intelligence authorities—primarily FISA Section 702 and Executive Order 12333—would significantly expand the government’s ability to analyze Americans’ communications while weakening existing privacy protections. Key points:

- **Section 702**: Targets foreigners overseas but “incidentally” collects large volumes of Americans’ communications. The FBI has repeatedly queried these databases for U.S. persons, often in violation of rules. AI could expand the pool of foreign targets (and thus incidental U.S. collection) and enable far more sophisticated analysis of Americans’ emails and calls.
- **EO 12333**: Allows bulk collection overseas with no judicial oversight and is believed to capture far more data than Section 702. Minimization rules (retention limits, masking of U.S. person identities, purpose restrictions) are already weaker than under 702 and would be further undermined by LLMs, which can process vast datasets, generate justifications for prolonged retention, and extract insights that human analysts could not.

(U) The author warns that AI would erode both the practical and legal constraints that currently limit the impact of these programs on Americans’ privacy and civil liberties, while also risking the production of unreliable intelligence.
