(U) Surveillance images from May 2026 reveal that Russia has mounted heavy machine guns on the bridge of the Marshal Vasilevskiy, a Gazprom-owned civilian LNG tanker that regularly supplies energy to Kaliningrad, effectively turning the vessel into an armed asset operating in the Baltic Sea. The ship, which has carried passengers with military and FSB backgrounds on its voyages along the coasts of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, has been fitted with 12.7mm Kord machine guns fortified with sandbags, capable of engaging targets up to 2 kilometers away. Experts assess the armament as serving dual purposes—defending against potential Ukrainian sea drone attacks and signaling resolve to NATO countries—while Estonian officials warn that the tanker could become a legitimate military target and view Russia’s broader fleet activities in the region as both a security and environmental risk. The militarization of what appears to be a civilian vessel blurs the lines between commercial and military operations, raising the stakes for any potential NATO inspections or confrontations in the increasingly tense Baltic Sea theater.
