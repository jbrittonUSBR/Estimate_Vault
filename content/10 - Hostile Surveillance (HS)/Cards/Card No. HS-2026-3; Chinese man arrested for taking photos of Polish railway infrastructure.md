(U) The 48-year-old man was apprehended on Easter Monday in Kowalów, a village near the German border in the Lubuskie region, following two separate reports from concerned witnesses. Officers from Poland’s Railway Protection Guard (SOK) located and detained him at the scene.  Police found that the suspect had been [taking photos of railway equipment and tracks](https://tvpworld.com/89599788/poland-accuses-ukrainian-of-spying-on-critical-infrastructure-for-russian-intelligence). “It turned out to be a 48-year-old Chinese citizen. The phones he had with him have been secured and will undergo detailed examination,” Maciej Kimet, spokesperson for the Lubuskie police, said in a statement. As of Tuesday, investigators said that they were analyzing the contents of the devices and awaiting a sworn interpreter before questioning the man. Officials have not yet determined why the man was photographing the site and say they are not ruling out any scenario. “This is extremely important for public safety, given the tense international situation and the related threats,” Kimet said, adding that prosecutors and other services have been informed. Police urged the public to remain vigilant. “If anyone notices anything concerning, it should be reported as soon as possible,” they said. “Every report from the public will be thoroughly examined and checked.” Poland has recently [tightened rules on photographing critical infrastructure](https://tvpworld.com/86161179/poland-to-ban-photography-at-over-20000-sites-under-new-national-security-law). Since April 2025, taking pictures or filming sites deemed important for national security—marked with special signs—has been banned, with violations punishable by fines, arrest or confiscation of equipment. Authorities have also been conducting a [nationwide operation](https://tvpworld.com/90100332/poland-to-counter-sabotage-with-10000-troops) codenamed “TOR,” launched in late 2025, following a suspected sabotage attack on the Warsaw–Lublin railway line, including an explosion that damaged tracks and overhead power infrastructure. The operation involves joint patrols by police, the Railway Protection Guard and Territorial Defense Forces at stations, rail hubs and key transport points.
