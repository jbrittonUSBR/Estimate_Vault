(U) Vermont police have circumvented the state’s 2013 law restricting automated license plate reader surveillance by accessing data from out-of-state Flock cameras through the regional New England State Police Information Network, conducting over 100 searches since 2023 for investigations ranging from serious crimes to lower-level cases. The law, designed for smaller local systems before the rise of national networks like Flock’s 80,000-plus cameras, did not anticipate cross-border data sharing, allowing agencies such as the Vermont State Police and departments in Berlin, Brattleboro, and Colchester to query plates tracked in other states. A notable example involved tracking a relative’s vehicle in a 2025 child luring case in Hartford, while Manchester’s limited Flock deployment saw its data accessed millions of times nationwide before use was paused. Law enforcement officials defend the practice as an essential tool for solving crimes that would otherwise go unaddressed, but privacy advocates, including the ACLU, warn of unchecked government tracking of public movements with minimal oversight or accountability. As the 2013 law approaches its sunset next year, lawmakers are calling for clearer restrictions to close the loophole and align policy with the original intent of limiting pervasive vehicle surveillance.
