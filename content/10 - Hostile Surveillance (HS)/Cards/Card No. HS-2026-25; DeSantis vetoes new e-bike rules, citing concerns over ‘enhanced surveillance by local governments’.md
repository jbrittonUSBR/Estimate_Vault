(U) Gov. Ron DeSantis vetoed a unanimously passed bill (SB 382) that would have established statewide regulations for electric bicycles in Florida, citing concerns that provisions like a 10 mph speed limit near pedestrians could lead to “enhanced surveillance” and increased policing of e-bike users by local law enforcement through speed detection and monitoring devices. The legislation, aimed at addressing rising e-bike injuries and crashes—particularly among children—also created a task force for further rules, which DeSantis viewed as unnecessary government overreach. While supporters argued for uniform standards to improve road safety amid growing e-bike use, DeSantis questioned the need for such regulations and expressed broader opposition to measures that could expand surveillance of residents. The veto leaves e-bike oversight primarily to local governments and aligns with DeSantis’ recent actions against other unanimous bills he saw as posing risks of unintended surveillance or partisan misuse, highlighting ongoing tensions between public safety initiatives and concerns over government monitoring.
