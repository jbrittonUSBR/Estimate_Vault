(U) A KXAN investigation into approximately 30 drownings in Austin’s Lady Bird Lake over the past decade—mostly linked to intoxication near the Rainey Street entertainment district and debunking online rumors of a serial killer—has prompted the city to upgrade surveillance cameras and explore additional safety measures. In direct response to the reporting, Austin installed a new hardwired camera near the Rainey Street Trailhead in February 2026 after a previous solar-powered unit failed, though officials denied KXAN’s request for footage citing security risks. The city is also discussing broader improvements, including lighting upgrades across urban trails where 91% have identified gaps, and considering a volunteer late-night patrol program modeled on a successful initiative in La Crosse, Wisconsin, though progress has stalled due to funding, scale, and logistical challenges despite initial interest from city council members and UT student government. At least two drownings occurred after prior infrastructure upgrades like lighting, fencing, and added EMS resources, underscoring ongoing safety concerns. The investigation has shifted focus from unfounded conspiracy theories to practical steps aimed at preventing future tragedies through better monitoring, visibility, and community engagement.
