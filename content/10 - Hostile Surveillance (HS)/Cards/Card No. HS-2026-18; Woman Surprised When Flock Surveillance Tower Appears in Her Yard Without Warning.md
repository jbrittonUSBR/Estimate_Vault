(U) A Virginia woman was surprised to find a Flock Safety “Raven” audio detection pole—similar to controversial ShotSpotter gunshot sensors—installed without notice in the public strip in front of her Roanoke home, part of a city-approved rollout of 75 such devices. Kat Vaughn discovered the pylon after a short trip and, with help from a responding police officer who was initially unsure what it was, confirmed it was an early-installed gun surveillance unit scheduled for July deployment; the location was not on the official list, and she received no prior notification by mail or email. City police offered only a vague response to media inquiries—“we are working on this”—highlighting a lack of transparency in the rollout. The incident underscores growing concerns over expanding police surveillance technologies, including AI-integrated cameras and audio sensors, which critics say operate with insufficient oversight, raise serious privacy and civil liberties issues, and are being deployed in ways that can catch residents off guard even on their own property.
