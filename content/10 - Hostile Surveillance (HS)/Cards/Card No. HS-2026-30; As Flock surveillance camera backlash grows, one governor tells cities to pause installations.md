(U) Amid mounting public resistance, growing community protests, and local contract cancellations, Connecticut Governor Ned Lamont called for a statewide pause on the installation of automated license plate readers (ALPRs) and safety cameras.
### **Key Takeaways**

- **The Governor's Directive:** Governor Lamont urged municipalities and local police departments to immediately halt new surveillance camera installations. He directed the Connecticut Police Officers Standards and Training Council to establish comprehensive guidance addressing data collection and citizen privacy within 30 days.
- **Balancing Privacy and Safety:** While acknowledging that safety equipment is intended to assist law enforcement, Lamont emphasized that the public is "justifiably concerned" about where the cameras are deployed, how data is managed, and who has access to it.
- **Legislative Action:** The directive follows recent state legislation—such as Public Act 26-14—designed to enforce stricter confidentiality rules, limit data retention, and ban the sharing of surveillance data with federal authorities for immigration, reproductive rights, or gender-affirming care tracking.
- **Local Backlash:** The state-level intervention mirrors national trends, as communities across Connecticut (such as Killingworth) pull out of contracts early or face intense resident pushback regarding the normalization of mass tracking on public and private properties.
