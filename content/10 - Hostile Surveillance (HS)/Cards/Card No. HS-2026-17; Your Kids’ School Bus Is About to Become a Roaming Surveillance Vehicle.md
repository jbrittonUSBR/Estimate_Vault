(U) BusPatrol, a company that operates over 40,000 stop-arm cameras on school buses across more than two dozen states to catch drivers illegally passing stopped buses, is planning to repurpose those cameras into always-on automatic license plate readers that would continuously scan and collect data on nearby vehicles to sell to law enforcement. The shift, driven by a new investor seeking additional revenue, would turn the cameras—which currently activate only for specific violations—into roaming surveillance tools capable of tracking movements around school buses at all times, with testing already underway and plans to equip 100 buses by the end of next month. Civil liberties advocates, including the ACLU’s Jay Stanley, have raised alarms about the potential for unchecked mass surveillance, AI-driven over-enforcement, and abuse, noting that similar systems have already been used by police to stalk individuals without warrants. The plan adds to a growing ecosystem of unregulated surveillance technologies, from license plate readers to device trackers, as laws struggle to keep pace with rapid technological advances in monitoring public movements.
