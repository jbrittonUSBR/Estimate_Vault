(U) Automated license plate readers (ALPRs), primarily from Flock Safety, are widely deployed by police departments in Missouri, Kansas, and across the U.S., enabling real-time tracking of vehicle movements and creating detailed location histories that raise significant privacy and civil liberties concerns. In Lenexa, Kansas, resident Canyen Ashworth was tracked approximately 150 times over two years after criticizing local police, while in Weston, Missouri, residents successfully blocked a Flock contract over fears of unchecked surveillance and data sharing with other agencies. Although proponents highlight benefits—such as aiding over a million investigations last year and helping locate thousands of missing persons—critics point to documented abuses, including officers using the systems to stalk ex-partners, and the lack of warrants or strong guardrails for accessing sensitive movement data. Resistance movements like DeFlock and legal challenges are growing, even as some states impose restrictions and federal agencies push for broader access, underscoring the tension between public safety gains and the erosion of privacy in an era of pervasive vehicle surveillance.
