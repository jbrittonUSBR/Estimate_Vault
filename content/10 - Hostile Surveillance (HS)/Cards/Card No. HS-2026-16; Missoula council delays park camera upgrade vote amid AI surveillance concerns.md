(U) The Missoula City Council has delayed a vote on upgrading surveillance cameras in city parks and facilities amid concerns over the AI capabilities of the proposed vendor, Verkada, which could enable facial recognition and license plate scanning. Officials argued that the upgrades are needed to address rising vandalism and safety issues—such as rocks thrown into pools, damage to soccer fields, and muggings at skateparks—while noting that modern cameras inherently include AI features and that existing systems are failing. Public commenters raised alarms about data privacy, citing Verkada’s $2.95 million FTC settlement over security practices, prompting Ward 5 Councilmember Bob Campbell to successfully move to send the matter back to committee pending development of a formal surveillance technology policy. The 8-3 vote reflects growing resident unease about expanded monitoring, even as city staff emphasized limited data retention (60 days) and restricted access, leaving the timeline for a new discussion undecided.
