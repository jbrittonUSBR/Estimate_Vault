(U) A Guardian investigation reveals that the National Design Studio (NDS)—a White House office created by executive order in 2025 and led by Airbnb co-founder Joe Gebbia—has redesigned key federal websites, including previews for passport applications and voter registration, while embedding commercial visitor-tracking and session-recording software that could capture detailed user interactions without required privacy assessments or notices. The sites used tools like PostHog, configured to bypass adblockers, along with an in-house monitoring system sending data to non-public servers, raising alarms about violations of federal privacy laws and the potential for invasive surveillance of sensitive activities such as applying for passports or registering to vote. Critics, including privacy advocates, warn that routing these services through White House-controlled infrastructure bypasses agencies like the State Department and Election Assistance Commission, risks exposing voter data to DHS databases, and erodes public trust by creating an unaccountable “skunk-works” surveillance apparatus outside normal oversight. Tracking features were reportedly removed after media inquiries, but experts argue the episode highlights broader dangers of centralized control, hidden spending, and the unchecked expansion of government monitoring tools under the current administration.
